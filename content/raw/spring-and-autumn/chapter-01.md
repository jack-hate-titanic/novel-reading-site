# Shuo Chunqiu - Part One: The Rise of Qi and Chu (Chapters 1-10)

## Chapter 1: The Fairy Maiden

A song says, "Love your kingdom, but love beauty even more."
That is not true.
Then what is the truth?
The truth is this: people love most what they do not have.
Men who have kingdoms want beauty, and men who have beauty want kingdoms.
There is a saying that behind every successful man stands a woman.
But the truth is, behind every man who loses a kingdom, there is often a woman too.
Do women change history?
No, they just often make history go in a different direction.

### The Fairy Maiden

Let us go back to 780 BCE.
It was the Zhou dynasty, the third dynasty in Chinese history.
An old love story began on the Hanzhong Plain.
The main man was named Bao Hongde, and he was twenty-four.
He was the only son of Bao Xu, the chief official of Baocheng.
Baocheng lay in what is now Mian County, Shaanxi, at the western edge of the Hanzhong Plain.
Its ruling official was something like a county official.
That day Bao Hongde had nothing to do, so he walked out of the city.
By the river, he met the first heroine of this book.
What did she look like?
She was a beauty, looking no more than fifteen or sixteen.
She wore a flowered head scarf with a wildflower tucked into it.
She had on a flower-patterned dress made of coarse linen, a little loose on her body.
On her back she carried a large basket full of mushrooms.
She looked clean, lovely, and a little bit wild.
Close your eyes and imagine Song Zuying at fifteen.
As The Book of Songs says, "A graceful maiden is a gentleman's perfect match."
A girl like that made Bao Hongde lose all self-control.
Anyone who saw her would want to win her heart.
Hearing someone singing a love song, the girl looked up and saw a young nobleman looking at her with charm.
Her face turned red at once, and she gave him a soft look back.
That single smile stole Bao Hongde's heart away.
That same smile would also send history in a new direction.
In Zhou times, it was not unusual for people to choose their own partners.
The Book of Songs had already shown this to be true.
That was how the two of them fell in love at first sight.
Then they began singing love songs to each other.
No one can say for sure anymore what they sang.
Most likely it was just some Zhou-dynasty version of "Girl, go boldly forward."
No one knows whether they sang well or badly.
All at once a toad jumped from the grass and plopped into the river.
Then came a second, a third, and a fourth.
Before long there were too many to count.
Thousands and thousands of toads were jumping into the water.
What kind of sign was that?
Were the toads also dreaming of eating swan meat?
Or did they just want a bath?
In any case, the two of them got to know each other in no time.
The girl was actually only fourteen.
She only looked a little older than her age.
Her family lived in the nearby mountains.
Her father sold mulberry-wood bows, so people called him Sang Shu.
Her mother wove baskets, though of course her last name was not Basket.
She said, "My parents call me the Fairy Maiden because they found me by the river."
"They say I was a gift sent by Heaven, a fairy girl."
So she really was the legendary Fairy Maiden.
No wonder she was so charming.
Bao Hongde hurried to introduce himself too.
He said, "My name is Bao Hongde, but everyone at home calls me Baozi."
With a laugh, the girl said, "Then I will call you Brother Baozi."
The more they talked, the closer they grew.
By the time the sun was about to set, the two had already promised to marry each other in private.
A Chinese version of Cinderella was already playing out.
As they held each other, the earth suddenly shook, and the river seemed to boil.
Could love really shake heaven and earth the moment it began?
That would have been a little too much.
"This is bad, it is an earthquake!"
The two of them panicked at once and ran for home in different directions.
Mushrooms were left all over the ground.
Now we know those toads did not come for swan meat or for a bath.
They had come to warn of the earthquake.
Since ancient times, toads seem to have a gift for knowing when earthquakes are coming.

### The Qishan Earthquake

The earthquake measured 7.9 on the Richter scale.
Its center lay in Qishan, so history remembers it as the Qishan Earthquake.
Where is Qishan?
It is in what is now Qishan County, Baoji, Shaanxi.
What did the earthquake cause?
The Records of the Grand Historian says, "In the second year of King You, the three rivers of the western region were shaken. That year the rivers ran dry, and Mount Qishan collapsed."
The "three rivers" meant the Jing, the Yellow River, and the Luo.
How terrible was it?
There is no need for a long description; just think of the Wenchuan earthquake and you will be close enough.
Qishan and Wenchuan lie on the same fault line.
As for why the rivers stopped flowing, the simple answer is that landslides blocked them.
The whole Zhou kingdom was terrified.
That was because Qishan was the birthplace of the Zhou dynasty.
In later words, it was the Zhou dynasty's dragon vein.
If the dragon vein collapsed, who would not be afraid?
The Grand Historian Boyangfu began saying the Zhou would fall almost at once.
The Grand Historian kept the records of the state and held great power.
He was something like the head of a national academy today.
He said, "The state is finished. The people live by land and water. If the water is gone, how can they live?"
He did not care at all how people would feel hearing this.
He even brought up past examples to prove his point.
He said that when the Yi and Luo rivers dried up, the Xia dynasty fell.
When the Yellow River dried up, the Shang dynasty fell too.
Now it was Zhou's turn, and he feared the result would be the same.
He also said, "A state must depend on its mountains and rivers. When the mountains fall and the rivers dry up, it means ruin."
"If the state is going to fall, it will happen within ten years."
He even worked out the timeline for Zhou's fall.
Did his prediction come true in the end?
The Zhou capital, Haojing, stood where Xi'an is today.
Its distance from Qishan was about the same as Chengdu's distance from Wenchuan.
Haojing felt the earthquake strongly, but the damage there was not too bad.
Duke Zhou, the Grand Preceptor, and Duke Shao, the Grand Protector, right away sent troops and people to Qishan to help.
Half a month later, the rescue work was finished.
Duke Zhou and Duke Shao went back to the capital and went to see King You of Zhou in great anger.
Who was King You of Zhou?
He was the Son of Heaven of the Zhou.
In those days there was no word for "emperor"; rulers were simply called kings.
The name "King You" already sounds like bad news.
Later generations called him that because what he did was so bad.
Remember this: ancient rulers got such names only after they died.
So while he was alive, he had no idea that history would later call him King You.
If someone in a TV show calls him "King You of Zhou" to his face, that usually means the writer did not do their history homework.
Why were Duke Zhou and Duke Shao so angry?
Because they had come to scold him.
Duke Zhou began, "Your Majesty, you do not do your duty, you work the people too hard, you keep building big projects, and you have made Heaven angry."
"That is why Heaven has punished us with an earthquake."
Duke Zhou was not polite at all.
King You did not believe a word of it.
He said, "Rebuilding palaces only shows that the state is rich."
"Besides, if we had not fixed up the old halls, they might have fallen down this time."
Duke Shao also told him to stop looking for beauties for a while and pay more attention to the suffering of the people.
The king was even less willing to listen.
He said, "An earthquake is a natural disaster, what does that have to do with me?"
From the day he became king, he had been searching the whole country for beauties.
It was almost like an ancient talent show.
But after more than a year, he still had not found anyone he really liked.
So he was already in a bad mood to begin with.
From that angle, he really did look like the father of beauty contests.
He could never have guessed that thousands of years later the whole country would still be doing the same thing.
Then why did he still put up with Duke Zhou and Duke Shao?
Because the two men were well respected, powerful, and both his elders.
So no matter how much he hated their words, he had to sit there and listen.
The moment the two dukes left, King You was full of anger with nowhere to let it out.
Just then, Guo Shifu, the Grand Steward, arrived.
His title was something like prime minister.
The king glared at him and said, "Old Guo, have you come to bother me too?"
Guo Shifu smiled and said, "Your Majesty, I have come to bring you good news."
The king asked, "And what is there to celebrate?"
Guo Shifu replied at once, "Your Majesty led the earthquake rescue work to a great victory."
At that, the king's face softened at once.
Seeing the king's mood get better, Guo Shifu became even more excited.
He said the rescue work had been fast and complete, that everyone had tried their best, and that countless people had been saved.
Now, he said, the people were full of thanks and had even written songs of praise.
That caught the king's interest right away.
Guo Shifu cleared his throat and began to sing.
The meaning of his song was this: when disaster comes, even dying gives you no right to complain.
Duke Zhou and Duke Shao run around giving orders, the Son of Heaven worries deeply, and the lords all weep.
Even as ghosts, everyone would feel lucky.
Warhorses and chariots race to the disaster area, while shovels and hoes work from left and right.
The people all raise their voices to thank the king.
They would be happy even if they had to die all over again.
The king grinned from ear to ear when he heard it.
He asked, "So if you are my people, even dying for no good reason still makes you happy?"
Guo Shifu hurried to say, "Of course, that is exactly how the people feel."
The happy king said, "Good, spread it everywhere."
Guo Shifu was enjoying his success when the king suddenly burst into loud laughter.
Laughing as he spoke, the king said that if Guo Shifu were hit by a cart and killed on the way home, he would personally come cry over him so that he too could die happy.
The king was not stupid, of course.
He knew very well that Guo Shifu had made up the whole song just to flatter him.
Even so, he happened to like both the song and the man who sang it.

### The Lord of Baocheng

Baocheng was closer to the center of the earthquake, so its buildings were damaged much more badly.
For more than ten days, Bao Hongde followed his father around doing rescue work.
He had no chance to go see the Fairy Maiden.
Once the worst of the work was done, Bao Xu decided to take a trip to Haojing.
One reason was to report how Baocheng was doing after the disaster.
Another was to pay his respects to King You.
He also brought along a few folk songs praising the king.
On the way, he heard that Duke Zhou and Duke Shao had just scolded the king.
And the common people were all praising them for it.
That made Bao Xu think.
He figured that if Duke Zhou and Duke Shao could speak up without getting in trouble, then a few words from him should be safe and good for his name.
So after arriving in Haojing, he washed his face, straightened his hat, and hurried off to see the king.
But he forgot to look at himself first.
In every age, whether you flatter someone or give them advice, you first have to read their face right.
Bao Xu began, "Your Majesty, the earthquake was caused by bad rule. You really should stop selecting beauties."
King You got angry the moment he heard it.
He thought that Duke Zhou and Duke Shao could get away with lecturing him because they were older and more senior.
But who did Bao Xu think he was?
The king asked, "Are you saying I caused the earthquake?"
Bao Xu panicked at once.
He stammered so badly that he could not even finish a sentence.
The king slapped the table and ordered him to be seized and thrown into prison.
By accident, the king had done one thing right.
Why had the beauty search gone on for more than a year without success?
Because no one had any reason to work hard, and deep down most people did not want to do it.
Naturally, the work was done badly.
If you want people to work hard, a sense of duty alone is not enough.
There are only two methods.
Either you offer a big reward.
With a big reward, brave men will always show up.
Or you lock up someone's father and see if he still dares to hold back.
To put it plainly, that method is kidnapping.
From a management point of view, the second one often works better.
Once Bao Xu was put in jail, the household fell into chaos.
Bao Hongde right away rushed to the capital carrying silver and local gifts.
As for back-door favors, things were much the same then as now.
But the capital was not Baocheng.
It was full of princes and noble sons.
Bao Xu might be someone in Baocheng, but in Haojing he was nobody.
Only after arriving in the capital with gold and silver did Bao Hongde truly understand what it meant to carry a pig's head around and still not find a temple to give it to.
He did not even know whom to bribe.
His father was nearing a full year in prison, and still nothing had changed.
Mother and son were at a loss.
If the old man died in jail, the family would not be able to keep their land.
At this key moment, Bao Hongde's mother suddenly had a bright idea.
She said, "Is it not just a beauty that the Son of Heaven wants?"
"If we give him a beautiful woman, why would your father not be set free?"
That sounded simple, but doing it was another matter.
King You had searched the whole country for more than a year and still found no one good enough.
Where were they supposed to find such a woman?
Bao's mother said, with a heavy heart, that they should send her own daughter in.
Bao Hongde said no at once.
He said his sister's bowed legs might at best get her a job as a palace maid.
Mother and son talked for a long time and still got nowhere.
And then, right at that moment, a bright idea flashed through Bao Hongde's mind.
He thought of the Fairy Maiden.
If he gave her to King You, the king would surely like her.
So Bao Hongde took wedding gifts and went to the Fairy Maiden's home.
Sang Shu was delighted.
The earthquake had cracked the house, and they badly needed money for repairs.
Now he could get silver, and his daughter seemed to have found a good future. Why would he not be happy?
That was how the Fairy Maiden entered the Bao family.
She herself was quite happy too.
From now on she could eat well, drink well, and work her way into high society.
She also knew that, by the rules of class, she could never be the main wife.
At most she could only be a concubine.
But what was so bad about that?
With enough luck, she might still rise one day.
But that sweet dream was broken almost at once.
Bao Hongde told her the whole situation.
Then he told her many stories about giving up things for others and giving up one's life for a greater cause.
At last he told her, plainly and fully, the plan to trade her for his father's freedom.
The Fairy Maiden cried.
Brother Baozi cried too.
Bao's mother joined in crying too, and even knelt down to beg her to save the whole family.
The Fairy Maiden thought that if she truly loved Brother Baozi, then she should give something up for him.
In the end, she agreed.
Bao Hongde was so moved that tears and snot ran down together.
But when he had to choose between his own father and the woman he loved, he still chose his father.
At that point, who could still go on singing about loving beauty more?

### Bao Si's First Time

That was how the matter was settled.
But before she could be sent to the palace, some things still had to be done.
The first task was to give the Fairy Maiden a new identity.
With her real background, there was no way she could enter the royal palace.
Luckily, the Bao family controlled the household records of the whole city.
So there was no need to find a forger.
They could make the fake papers themselves.
From that moment on, she no longer had the last name Sang, and she was no longer called the Fairy Maiden.
She was given a new name, Bao Si.
Her official identity became that of Bao Hongde's cousin, a noblewoman.
Once the identity was made, the next step was training.
The training was not very complicated.
It was mainly about dress, manners, how she spoke, and how she walked.
There was only one goal.
It was to make people feel right away that she was born a noble.
Not a country girl who had just put on better clothes.
The training was a great success.
That also showed that the Fairy Maiden had a noble air about her.
From then on, the Fairy Maiden was gone.
Bao Si was born.
Once everything was ready, Bao's mother herself ordered a carriage to be prepared.
Early the next morning, she would have her son take Bao Si to the capital.
But that night, Brother Baozi still could not help going to see her one last time.
He even said with a sigh, "Nothing is happier than new love, and nothing sadder than parting while still alive."
In the dim light, both of them were heartbroken.
When feelings run deep, it is hard to keep control.
They fell into each other's arms.
Bao Si said, "Brother, take me now."
At such a moment, any normal man would not be able to refuse.
In truth, that was exactly what Brother Baozi had come for in the middle of the night.
So the lamplight flickered, and soft moans filled the room.
The next morning, both of them set out looking very tired.
Bao's mother thought they had been crying, not knowing they had done more than cry.
Baocheng was not too far from Haojing.
But crossing the Qinling Mountains was not easy.
It took them three whole days to reach the capital.
And along the way, they naturally did not hold back from their lovers' pleasure.
King You's beauty search followed a basic process.
First, ministers did the first round of picking.
Then King You himself met the final picks in court.
Court was the place where he handled state affairs.
It was something like the White House as people today imagine it.
In the three years since he became king, King You had met countless beauties there.
But not one of them had truly moved his heart.
He often said sadly that there were no beauties under Heaven and that being the Son of Heaven was no fun at all.
So when the interview day came, he sat on the throne with very little hope.
Once Bao Hongde stepped into court, he first had to introduce the beauty.
After all, he was the one who had brought this beauty.
He said to the king, "Your Majesty, my father Bao Xu grew old and foolish and offended you."
"To make up for his mistake, I have found a beauty like no other and present her to Your Majesty."
The king said coolly, "Bring her in and let me see."
To the sound of music, Bao Si entered the hall.
Bao Hongde looked back and saw her walking forward slowly, like a fairy coming down to earth.
Everyone in court held their breath.
Every eye was on the same person.
When Bao Hongde turned back to the king, he already understood.
His father would surely be set free this time.
King You was already stunned.
He was almost drooling.
At that moment, he knew the rest of his life would belong to this woman.
What he did not yet know was that his kingdom would be dragged in too.
Very soon, the king gave his order.
The beauty would stay.
Bao Xu would be set free.
He would not only get his old job back, but also receive a rich reward as payment from the state.
His father was saved.
But the Fairy Maiden was lost to him from that day on.
Bao Hongde's eyes filled with tears.
He himself could not tell whether he was happy or sad.
Perhaps he was both.
Bao Si walked toward the inner palace with the palace staff.
As she left, she could not help looking back at Bao Hongde one more time.
At that moment, she did not yet know.
From that moment on, she would become a famous woman in Chinese history.
And she would help rewrite that history.
From a Fairy Maiden to a Sister Furong-style star, there are many ways to become famous.
This was only one of them.
How exactly would Bao Si rewrite Chinese history?

## Chapter 2: Turmoil in the Inner Palace

The beauty selection ended, and on the surface, everyone was happy.
And so King You's reign produced its own "Super Girl champion."
The king immediately dismissed the court.
As soon as he returned to the harem, he held Bao Si and looked at her from every angle.
He looked at her with clothes on, and he looked at her without clothes too.
No matter how he looked, she seemed so beautiful that it was hard to describe.
He thought she might even be more lovely than the famous Daji.
After he had looked and touched as much as he wanted, King You decided it was time to "give her the prize."
What was the highest reward a Son of Heaven could give a woman?
It was to spend the night with her.
To put it simply, it meant taking her to bed himself.
Some people might ask: did the king not notice that this "champion" was no longer a virgin?
The answer is simple.
In those days, the Chinese did not yet care so much about virginity.

### The Staffing Problem in the Harem

First, we need to talk about how the Zhou harem was organized.
That is, how many women the Son of Heaven was allowed to have by law.
The Book of Rites says the harem was ruled by the queen.
Below the queen were three chief consorts, nine lesser consorts, twenty-seven court ladies, and eighty-one royal women.
That made 121 women in all.
That was about the size of a reinforced company.
The number happened to be eleven times eleven.
In other words, they could stand in eleven neat rows, with eleven in each row.
With 365 days in a year, each woman got about three days on average.
If your turn came during your period, you were just out of luck.
The Son of Heaven, meanwhile, got a day off.
So being the king was not that easy either.
Among these 121 women, the queen and the three chief consorts had extra rules.
They all had to be daughters of feudal lords or high officials.
In the very first year of his rule, King You had already filled every spot.
Now Bao Si had arrived, but there was no empty place for her.
So what could be done?
King You's idea was very simple.
Enjoy her first, and deal with the staffing problem later.
A new person had come into the palace.
The news of Bao Si's arrival spread through the whole harem very quickly.
At first, no one took her very seriously.
Was she not just another "Super Girl"?
By habit, King You's interest in such newcomers usually lasted only one to three days.
Three days at most, and he would get bored.
Once he lost interest, the newcomer would be sent to work as an ordinary maid.
This time, however, everyone was wrong.
Three days passed, and Bao Si was still at the king's side.
Thirteen days passed, and he was still with her.
By the thirtieth day, the whole harem was in an uproar.
Everyone realized that something was very wrong.
This time, King You had been completely trapped.
As the song goes, "Rain and dew make the crops grow strong."
The hundred or so women on the official list got only a few drops of that "dew" a year.
But once Bao Si arrived, every cloud in the palace seemed to rain only on her piece of land.
Her field was watered without end, while everyone else's fields were turning into dry hills.
Why had things become like this?
Why did King You grow tired of every other beauty, but never grow tired of Bao Si?
What magic did this fourteen-year-old mushroom girl have?
Bao Si was fourteen, which by today's standards is only middle-school age.
By today's standards, what the king was doing would be a crime against a child.
But Bao Si had already been pushed into the adult world.
She may have grown up faster than others, but inside she was still lost and unsure.
There were no mulberry bows in the palace, and no baskets either.
There were no mushrooms to pick.
She did not even know what she was supposed to do all day.
She missed Brother Baozi.
She did not really like the old man in front of her.
So she said whatever she wanted and did whatever she wanted.
Strangely, the old man treated her better and better.
Sometimes he even seemed to be trying to please her.
Why was that?
Bao Si herself did not know.
There were two reasons.
Let us start with the first.
As the saying goes, the flower at home is never as sweet as the wild one outside.
The wilder a woman seems, the more people are drawn to her.
The women on King You's official list were not bad at all.
The problem was that they were too perfect.
They were either great ladies from noble families or refined beauties.
They knew their manners and knew when to step forward and when to step back.
Each had her own skill in music, chess, calligraphy, and painting.
To put it simply, they were polished and smart beauties of the highest kind.
But King You did not like them.
From childhood on, every woman he had known had been of that type.
From his grandmother to his mother, to over a hundred aunts, and then to his own hundred or so women.
Every one of them was gentle, good, and perfect in her manners.
But to him, such women were like lukewarm water.
They had no strong character, and they all seemed the same.
In the end, he had simply grown tired of them.
He had grown tired of them to the very limit.
Bao Si was completely different.
She was innocent, lively, and spoke her mind.
She made a big fuss over the smallest things.
Sometimes she even used bad words.
When she got angry, she even scolded the king himself.
She liked running around with bare feet.
She liked chasing butterflies in the garden.
She even liked spitting wherever she wanted.
To put it plainly, she was just like Little Swallow from My Fair Princess.
Rare things are precious.
People say women do not love men who are too good.
The same is true the other way around.
So Bao Si was only fourteen years old.
She had not eaten well, and her body was not yet fully grown.
In theory, a man in his forties should not have been won over by her body alone.
But the fact was clear: King You had fallen for her.

### The Palace Storm

Generally, once a new star becomes famous, the old stars feel unhappy.
When Bao Si became popular, the truly unhappy ones were the leaders of the inner palace.
In theory, the harem was not led by the king but by the queen.
That is exactly what the Book of Rites says.
The queen was the daughter of the Marquis of Shen, and her family name was Shen.
Of course, you could also say her family name was Jiang.
That was because the Shen family first came from the Jiang clan.
So the history books call her Shen Jiang, Queen Shen, or Queen Jiang, and none of them is wrong.
Here we will just call her Queen Shen.
Queen Shen was not a "Super Girl."
She had not won any contest at all.
Under Zhou rules, there were two rules for picking a wife for the crown prince.
First, she had to be the daughter of a feudal lord.
Second, she had to be from a different clan, and she could not have the same royal family name Ji.
Shen was ruled by a marquis, and its ruling family belonged to the Jiang clan.
So King Xuan of Zhou had married the Marquis of Shen's daughter to the crown prince.
Later, she became queen as a matter of course.
A queen, of course, was no ordinary contestant.
She was the mother of the whole country.
Queen Shen was very angry.
She thought to herself that this little witch had become too proud and needed to be taught a lesson.
She was the mother of the country, yes.
But even she had not seen King You for over thirty days.
Rules in theory are often weak.
Reality is what shows the truth in the end.
At this point, the queen in name did not dare to fight King You directly.
Because the king could throw out those rules whenever he wanted.
So Queen Shen could only sit in her room and feel angry.
Just then, her son, Crown Prince Yijiu, arrived.
Yijiu had just come back from his mother's parents' home.
The moment he walked in, he saw the sadness on his mother's face.
He asked, "Mother, who has been mean to you? Was it Father?"
Normally, that would have been a silly question.
Who besides King You would dare to be mean to Queen Shen?
But that day, the question hit the most painful spot in her heart.
Queen Shen told him everything about Bao Si.
When she finished, she let out a deep, worried sigh.
She said Bao Si was dangerously charming.
She had charmed the king so much that he forgot to eat and sleep, and he even played with her in broad daylight.
If things kept going like this, she would sooner or later give birth to a younger brother for the prince.
She herself was getting older; if she lost her place as queen, so be it.
What really scared her was that even her son's place as crown prince might not be safe.
The prince grew upset at once.
He said they should just scratch the woman's face to pieces and see if his father still liked her.
The only idea he could think of was to ruin her face.
To be honest, the plan was rough and cruel.
Queen Shen hurried to stop him.
She told him that if he really did it, the king would surely have him killed.
But the crown prince had already made up his mind.
He was ready to act.
The plan even had a name.
It was called "the Palace Storm."
Chances always favor those who are ready.
The crown prince was just such a man.
That day there was no wind and no rain, and the sun was shining bright.
To put it simply, it was a very fine sunny day.
King You had gone to hold court.
Lately he had been showing up now and then, just so people knew he was still alive.
Operation Palace Storm began.
Bao Si was very bored in her room.
An old maid was telling her ghost stories.
Suddenly, a loud noise broke out outside.
What was stranger was that a man's voice was mixed in with the noise.
A maid soon ran in to report.
The crown prince had brought a group of Queen Shen's maids to pull up flowers.
Since they had no permission, they were almost like flower thieves.
The two sides had started arguing outside over this very thing.
Hearing this, Bao Si decided to go out and take a look.
Was it not just about pulling up a few flowers?
At worst, it was just a small fight among their own people.
When she stepped outside, she saw it right away.
A good-looking young man of about seventeen or eighteen stood there, telling the others to pull up flowers however they pleased.
There was no need to ask; this was the crown prince.

## Chapter 4: Fooling the Lords with Beacon Fires

But King You was, in truth, deeply unhappy.
He had never known that making someone else happy could bring its own kind of joy.
And Queen Bao's coldness happened to give him just such a chance.
If he had loved his people the way he loved Queen Bao, he might really have become a great Son of Heaven.
One night, Queen Bao cried out in her sleep, "Baozi, Baozi."
King You woke with a start.
He thought she was craving steamed buns.
So before dawn, he quietly got out of bed.
He wanted to surprise her.
When Queen Bao woke, she smelled something nice before she even opened her eyes.
The moment she looked, she froze in surprise.
More than a dozen steaming baskets of buns sat right by the bed.
Pork, fennel, mushroom - every kind was there.
And King You stood there looking pleased with himself.
Queen Bao asked what all those buns were doing there.
King You said, "You kept calling for baozi in your dream, so I knew you wanted to eat them."
This time Queen Bao did smile.
But it was a bitter smile.
The "Baozi" in her dream was not something to eat.
The person on her mind was Brother Baozi.
Those steaming buns in front of her only made her sadder.

### A Thousand Pieces of Gold for a Smile

King You did not lose heart.
He believed that nothing was impossible if a person truly set his mind to it.
Not long after, a palace maid came to report something.
Two years earlier, she said, she had accidentally torn a silk robe.
The ripping sound had been oddly special.
And when Queen Bao heard it back then, she had actually laughed.
King You was delighted.
He right away ordered bundles of silk to be brought in.
Then he had several strong palace maids tear them apart in front of Queen Bao.
Bundle after bundle was torn to pieces.
Queen Bao still did not smile.
Later she not only refused to laugh, but even frowned whenever she heard that sound.
The Records of the Grand Historian says that King You tried every way to make Bao Si smile.
But she simply would not.
He had money, power, and the will to try hard to please his wife.
By today's standards, that alone would already seem rare.
But even after all that, King You had run out of ideas.
So he had no choice but to use his final move.
King You decided to turn to the wisdom of the crowd.
Put simply, he was offering a reward.
He made sure the order was known both inside and outside the palace.
Whoever could make Queen Bao smile would be rewarded with a thousand pieces of gold.
The "mass line" had always worked well.
Sure enough, someone offered a clever plan.
That man was Guo Shifu.
He said he had a plan.
But the plan would need a big mobilization.
Whether it was worth it depended on how far the king was willing to go.
King You answered without hesitation.
If it could win even one smile from Queen Bao, then let alone mobilizing a crowd, he said he would even give away half his kingdom.
After hearing that, even Guo Shifu had to admit that the king was hopelessly in love.
Guo Shifu explained that in the past, to guard against the western tribes, the former kings had built beacon towers along Mount Li.
There was one every twenty li, and they stretched all the way to the feudal states.
As soon as enemies attacked, the fires would be lit at once.
When the lords saw the beacons, they would march through the night to rescue the king.
Now all was peaceful, and the beacons had long gone unused.
Why not take Queen Bao to Mount Li for an outing?
At night, light the beacons.
The lords would surely rush over in alarm.
But when they arrived, they would find that there was no enemy at all.
Each of them would be left staring in confusion.
At the sight of that, Queen Bao might finally laugh.
Guo Shifu could hardly stop himself from laughing first as he described it.
The moment King You heard it, he said it was excellent again and again.
He gave Guo Shifu five hundred pieces of gold on the spot.
He also promised the other five hundred after the plan succeeded.

### Beacon Fires to Toy with the Lords

Three days later, the moon was bright, the stars were few, and the weather was perfect.
Guo Shifu had chosen just such a night on purpose.
He wanted to make sure the signal would carry clearly.
Once everything was ready, King You left Haojing with Queen Bao.
Together they made their way to Mount Li.
There was a palace on the mountain.
Guo Shifu had already prepared a banquet.
There were also foreign dancers to liven things up.
As soon as night fell, the banquet began on the mountain.
And below, the beacon fires were lit.
The flames shot high into the sky.
Forget twenty li.
Even from fifty li away, they could be seen very clearly.
So the beacon towers along the route lit up one after another.
The signal raced outward like a relay.
Before long, it had reached the feudal lords in every direction.
The lords all thought that some great crisis had struck the capital.
Who could still bother with eating or sleeping?
Some threw down their bowls and left at once.
Some barely had time to pull up their trousers before jumping on horseback.
Some rulers led the troops in person.
Others sent out the finest chariots in their states.
Everyone raced toward the same destination.
That destination was Haojing.
Nearly three thousand years ago, there were no paved roads.
There were no rubber tires or comfortable vehicles either.
To rush in from all directions was no easy task.
And yet they still pressed on through the night.
For each of them held fast to one belief.
They had to save the Son of Heaven and defend the capital.
Dust rose everywhere, and chariots and horses thundered loudly.
Banners whipped in the air while men shouted and horses screamed.
Once the armies moved, supplies moved with them.
How many people spent that night awake with worry need not be told in detail.
By dawn, the Qin army was the first to arrive.
They reached Haojing first, yet found no enemy there.
When they heard that King You was at Mount Li, they turned there at once.
At the foot of Mount Li, the sky was just getting bright.
The beacon fires were still burning above.
But where was the enemy?
As the Qin troops stood puzzled, the Zheng army arrived as well.
They looked at one another in confusion.
No one could explain what had happened.
Not long after, the Jin troops arrived too.
Three armies gathered below the mountain, and the whole place became a mess.
At that moment, drums and gongs rang out from above.
A figure stepped out in the firelight.
It was none other than Guo Shifu.
He shouted down toward the men below.
"The Son of Heaven is perfectly fine. You may all go home."
"The beacons were lit tonight only for fun."
At those words, the lords below were left speechless.
They had raced through the whole night in exhaustion.
And in the end, they had been made the butt of a cruel joke.
Angry and humiliated, they withdrew while cursing under their breath.
At the sight of it, Queen Bao finally laughed.
And she laughed more than once.
For after that, more than a dozen other lords kept arriving one after another.
King You was overjoyed.
He at once paid out the remaining five hundred pieces of gold.
That is how the saying "a thousand pieces of gold for a smile" came to be.
But from that day on, King You lit the beacons more and more often.
Yet fewer and fewer lords came to the rescue.
Little by little, no one was willing to believe him anymore.

### Marquis Shen's Clever Plan

As the saying goes, if you do not pull weeds out by the root, they will grow back when the spring wind blows.
In the blink of an eye, it was the eleventh year of King You's reign.
That was 771 BCE.
After Crown Prince Yijiu had been deposed, he had been living with his grandfather, the Marquis of Shen.
And in a flash, eight years had passed.
Yijiu was miserable.
Marquis Shen was even more miserable.
His daughter had lost her place as queen.
His grandson had become a deposed crown prince.
They had once looked like two promising people.
Now both had gone to ruin in his hands.
But no matter how bitter he felt, he could not fight head-on.
After all, an arm cannot twist harder than a thigh.
So he kept enduring it.
At last, a chance appeared.
Why call it a chance?
First, King You had long been fooling the lords with false beacon fires.
Everyone was already thoroughly fed up with him.
Second, envoys had come from the Quanrong.
They said they wanted to do some trade with Zhou.
The Quanrong lived to the west of Zhou.
They were a nomadic people.
They had few goods of their own.
But they really wanted Zhou's supplies.
Usually they wanted to trade, but Zhou was unwilling to deal much with them.
And what if trade failed?
Then they robbed.
They rode well and struck fast.
Only their numbers and tactics were not very strong.
So they usually only raided the borders.
Although Shen did not directly border the Quanrong, it still had some dealings with them.
So Marquis Shen came up with what he thought was a foolproof plan.
He would join hands with the Quanrong and attack Haojing.
Why did he have to bring the Quanrong in?
Because no Zhou lord would willingly attack the capital.
Then what would each side gain?
The Quanrong could rob as much as they pleased.
Marquis Shen, meanwhile, could use them to get rid of Queen Bao, her son, and men like Guo Shifu.
Then Yijiu could become crown prince again.
And Queen Shen could return to her old place.
What about the risks?
Marquis Shen scarcely thought about them.
But he overlooked one key thing.
The Quanrong could not be trusted.
They often did not keep their word.
That is why, in anything you do, choosing the right partner matters a lot.
Marquis Shen sent envoys to the Quanrong ruler.
They conveyed his plan.
Naturally, they took gifts as well.
The Quanrong ruler was delighted the moment he heard it.
It was like the son of a rich family inviting bandits to help rob his own home.
How could they possibly refuse?
The two sides agreed on a time.
The Quanrong ruler himself led fifteen thousand experienced fighters.
Marquis Shen also set out with one hundred chariots to meet them.
This was the first large-scale foreign invasion in Chinese history.
And by the same logic, Marquis Shen became the first traitor to Zhou.

### Letting the Wolf Inside

The Shen-Quanrong army soon closed in on Haojing.
Scouts rushed the news to King You.
King You was shocked.
He right away called an emergency meeting.
But by this point, Zhou had not fought a serious war for many years.
Now foreign raiders were at the gate, with an insider leading them in.
What else was there to do?
Guo Shifu had only one idea.
Light the beacons.
Fast riders raced to Mount Li, and the beacon fires were lit at once.
The weather that day was bright and mild.
A light west wind blew at just the right strength.
Once the smoke rose, it could be seen a hundred li away.
But the allied army did not attack right away.
They were waiting too.
They feared that if they stormed the city at once, they would be surrounded in turn when the lords' reinforcements arrived.
So they only encircled the city without attacking.
If reinforcements came, they could still turn and run.
Three days passed.
Not a single lord sent a single soldier.
Seeing this, Marquis Shen laughed.
King You had gone too far with his old tricks.
Now at last, no one believed him.
Inside the city, everything fell into panic.
With no relief force coming, who was to meet the enemy?
Everyone turned their eyes to Guo Shifu.
It had been your idea to turn the beacon fires into a joke.
Now the lords were not coming.
So you had to clean up this mess.
Guo Shifu had no choice.
He could only gather his courage and lead a hundred chariots out of the city.
But he had no idea how to fight a war.
Once he reached the battlefield, he did not last even a single round.
His head was cut off before anyone even knew what had happened.
Those hundred-odd chariots were wiped out as well.
From the city wall, King You kept calling, "Old Guo! Old Guo!"
Guo Shifu barely opened his eyes.
He cast one glance toward the wall.
He smiled faintly.
Then he died.

### The Death of King You

The war horns sounded outside the city.
The Quanrong army launched its attack.
At that very moment, Marquis Shen was still on the toilet.
By the time he stood up, Quanrong banners were already flying over the city wall.
What happens when wolves get into a sheepfold?
No one knew the answer better than the sheep-herding Quanrong.
Once they entered Haojing, they were wolves among sheep.
The splendor of the Zhou capital seemed new to them in every way.
Everything looked like a rare treasure.
So they grabbed whatever they saw and killed whoever stood in their way.
Marquis Shen's troops entered the city afterward.
Only then did he realize how serious the trouble was.
They were far more savage than he had imagined.
He wanted to stop them, yet he had no power to do so.
King You might not have had to die.
What the Quanrong wanted was wealth.
And the people Marquis Shen wanted dead were Queen Bao and her son.
But King You would not save himself alone.
At his side remained only Ji You, the Lord of Zheng.
He was the king's own uncle.
When disaster struck, it was still family who could be relied on.
Ji You urged him to hand over his royal robe and insignia.
He himself would put them on and draw the enemy away.
King You could then escape among the common people.
King You refused.
He said that as the Son of Heaven, he could not bear such shame.
Ji You then urged him to break out on fast horses.
But King You still refused.
He said that if he left, he would leave with Queen Bao and Bofu.
And if he died, the whole family would die together.
Even at such a moment, he still could not let go of his feelings.
After hearing this, even Ji You was moved.
So he ordered a carriage prepared.
King You, Queen Bao, and Bofu got in.
Ji You himself mounted a war chariot.
He also took twenty or thirty guards with him.
He personally led the escort, halberd in hand.
They fought their way toward the eastern gate.
Ji You truly was a fierce fighter.
And the Quanrong had been caught off guard for a moment.
So he actually managed to protect the king out through the eastern gate.
The Quanrong ruler had not even meant to chase them.
But Marquis Shen kept pressing him, insisting that Bao Si must be killed.
So the pursuers chased them all the way to the foot of Mount Li.
If King You had been on horseback, he might have escaped.
But he was in a carriage.
How could a carriage outrun cavalry?
Ji You defended the king to the death and finally fell when his strength gave out.
At that moment, King You at least behaved like a man.
He shouted that they were not to harm women and children.
If someone had to be killed, then kill him first.
The Quanrong soldiers had not necessarily meant to kill him.
But since he himself asked for death, they rushed him all at once.
King You was hacked to death.
Bofu too was cut down with a single stroke.
Only the terrified Queen Bao was taken away alive.
That year, Haojing fell.
And King You died amid the chaos of war.

### Marquis Shen Is Stunned

Once the invaders truly flooded into the city, Haojing was filled with burning, killing, and looting.
The Quanrong ruler moved into the royal palace and enjoyed himself thoroughly.
Marquis Shen watched it happen and burned with anger inside.
Yet he did not dare say a single harsh word.
If he truly turned against them, his small force would stand no chance against the Quanrong.
Now Marquis Shen was truly stunned.
The people he wanted dead had not been killed.
And the ones he had not meant to kill had died instead.
At that point, there were only two things left that he could do.
The first was to protect his daughter.
The second was to send men out at once to seek help.
Only then did the lords realize that this time the beacon fire was real.
In truth, the lords did have some loyalty.
They did care about the capital and the Son of Heaven.
It was simply that King You had used up all his trust before this.
Now that the news had been confirmed true, the lords did send troops after all.
This time four powers came: Qin, Jin, Wei, and Zheng.
And each was led by its ruler in person.
Their armies hurried toward Haojing.
When the Quanrong saw four relief armies arriving, they no longer dared to keep fighting.
They withdrew under cover of darkness.
The lords pursued them afterward, but could not catch them.
As for Queen Bao, after she was carried off by the Quanrong, no one can say for certain what became of her.
Some say she killed herself.
Some say she became the Quanrong ruler's woman.
Others say that Bao Hongde later bought her back.
In short, many versions still circulate.
In the end, Bao Si was a pitiful woman.
She had nearly been drowned at birth.
She grew up in a poor home.
At five, she was picking mushrooms in the hills.
At fourteen, she entered the palace.
Later she was torn away from the man she loved.
After that, her husband was killed, and so was her son.
She herself was carried off as well.
And yet this very woman was blamed by many later generations as the beauty who ruined a dynasty.
Is that fair?
And where could she ever go to tell her side of the story?

## Chapter 5: The Spring and Autumn Era Begins

### King Ping of Zhou

When the tide turns, even traitors try to make themselves look like loyal men.
The armies of the four lords were about to enter Haojing.
Marquis Shen opened the city gates first.
That could be called working together from inside and outside.
But in truth, there was not much left inside to help.
Marquis Shen put on an innocent face.
He went out himself to welcome the rulers of the four states.
Then, with tears pouring down, he spoke out against the crimes of the Quanrong.
He said that if he had not done his best, the nobles in the city would all have been killed.
At the same time, he gave a light self-criticism.
He said he had meant well, but had made a terrible mistake.
He had been foolish enough to trust the wolves of the grasslands.
Strangely, no one punished him for it.
That was not because he acted so well.
It was because he still held one important card.
That card was the former crown prince Yijiu.
King You was dead.
Bofu was dead too.
No matter how you counted, Yijiu was the only one left who could take the throne.
So a temporary "public order committee" made a shared decision.
Yijiu would take the throne and become the Son of Heaven.
From that day on, he was King Ping of Zhou.
After he became king, he first gave his mother Queen Shen the title of Queen Dowager.
He also wanted to raise his grandfather Marquis Shen to Duke of Shen.
A short explanation is needed here.
The Zhou feudal ranks had five grades.
From highest to lowest, they were duke, marquis, earl, viscount, and baron.
Duke was the highest rank.
When King Wu defeated Shang, he gave out lands to many lords.
Very few were given the rank of duke.
So when King Ping tried to raise Marquis Shen this time, the reward was very great.
But Marquis Shen waved his hands again and again.
He said his guilt was too heavy, and he dared not accept.
This time, at least, he knew himself well enough.
King Ping thought about it and did not push him.
Then he began to reward people for their merits.
The Marquis of Wei was raised to Duke Wu of Wei.
He also stayed at the Zhou court as Grand Minister of Works.
The Marquis Wen of Jin did not get a higher title.
But he was given the region of Henei.
Juetu of Zheng inherited his father's rank.
He also received a thousand mu of good farmland.
He stayed at court as a chief minister.
Duke Xiang of Qin had only been a small lord under Zhou before.
Now he was formally made an earl.
From then on, Qin joined the ranks of the feudal lords.
So the four lords had not made the trip for nothing.
In the end, being fooled by a ruler a few times was not always a bad thing.
If you held on until the end, there might still be rewards.
Marquis Shen also took a liking to Juetu.
He thought the young man was smart, capable, and good-looking.
He happened to have a younger daughter too.
So he asked Duke Wu of Wei to be the matchmaker.
He married his daughter to Juetu.
In this way, the two families became relatives.
Marquis Shen had caused King You's death.
Yet he went on to put King You's son on the throne.
He had also caused the death of Ji You.
Then he married his daughter to Ji You's son.
He destroyed the father, and then did favors for the son.
Marquis Shen was truly a strange man at the start of the Spring and Autumn age.

### Moving the Capital

King Ping of Zhou felt uneasy in his heart.
Not everyone who becomes the Son of Heaven feels happy.
He had two reasons for feeling this way.
The first reason was that the state treasury had been emptied.
The Quanrong had taken almost everything they could carry.
The palaces and city walls were also badly damaged.
The whole place was dirty and messy.
Even the air seemed to smell of sheep.
The second reason was even more practical.
After tasting success, the Quanrong kept coming back to raid and steal.
Within a few months, Zhou had lost the western lands of Qi and Feng.
With its outer shield gone, Haojing was in great danger.
King Ping found that even a Son of Heaven could live in fear every day.
If he could not beat them, he might as well move away.
He remembered that his ancestors had left a piece of land in the east.
That place was Luoyi.
The next day, he called together his nobles and ministers.
The agenda had only two words: move the capital.
Long ago, King Cheng of Zhou had felt that the lords traveled too far to reach court.
So he built Luoyi in the center of the realm.
Whenever court assemblies were held, the Son of Heaven received the lords there.
Its palace buildings were almost as complete as those in Haojing.
Simply put, it came with all the furniture and fittings.
You could move in and live there right away.
And after more than two hundred years, Luoyi had grown rich and busy.
It was even more lively than Haojing.
Who would not want to sleep in peace in a safer and livelier place?
The ministers all agreed.
Move.
Haojing was in the west, and Luoyi was in the east.
The court moved from the old western capital to the new eastern one.
From that moment on, Western Zhou became Eastern Zhou.
When the Son of Heaven moved, Duke Xiang of Qin helped a great deal.
He provided men and supplies, and personally escorted the court with three hundred chariots.
He was like a moving company and an armed guard service all in one.
King Ping was very pleased.
He said that since they had just moved the capital, he had little to give as a reward.
But Qi and Feng were now held by the Quanrong.
If Qin could take them back, that land would belong to Qin.
Duke Xiang thanked him and left.
Later, Qin did defeat the Quanrong and greatly grew its lands.
That became one of the key steps in Qin's rise.
In the first year of King Ping, that is, 770 BCE, he moved the capital east only eight months after taking the throne.
It was exactly ten years after the earthquake at Mount Qi.
But why had he waited eight months before leaving?
The reason was simple.
By Zhou rules, a living person could not prepare his own funeral in advance.
Shrouds, coffins, outer cases, and burial mounds all had to be arranged only after death.
And the higher the rank, the longer the burial took.
After a king died, the body lay in mourning for seven days.
Then the tomb was built, and the lords were told to come for the burial.
Only when everyone had arrived was he buried, seven months later.
A feudal lord was buried after five months.
A minister after three.
Scholars and common people after two.
To put it bluntly, a beggar's burial depended on when the wild dogs came.
It was not just the timing that was strictly graded.
There were also strict rules for coffins.
The inner case was called the coffin, and the outer one was the casket shell.
By Zhou rules, a king had seven layers, a lord five, a minister three, and a gentleman two.
So King Ping had no choice but to bury King You first.
Only after all the rites were finished could he officially move.
Seen this way, the Zhou system made some sense.
At least it was not like the emperors of later times, who began building huge tombs as soon as they took the throne.
By the way, King You was given the title "You" after his death precisely because he died a violent death.
From here on, the curtain truly rose on the Spring and Autumn period.

### A Brilliant Spring and Autumn Age

Three men.
King You of Zhou, Guo Shifu, and Marquis Shen.
Together they made two huge mistakes.
One was lighting the beacon fires to fool the lords.
The other was letting the wolf into the house.
It was these two foolish acts that together buried Western Zhou.
They also shook the very roots of the Zhou dynasty.
But once Western Zhou fell, the Spring and Autumn and Warring States ages began.
It was an age full of wisdom, talent, and bold spirit.
That sounds strange.
But history often moves forward in just such ways.
And it is not without reason.
After the royal house of Zhou declined, the Zhou rites began to decay as well.
More importantly, the feudal lords no longer respected the central power.
When the central power was strong, the lords' thoughts and actions were tightly controlled.
They had to follow the rules set by the center.
That brought stability, of course.
But it also crushed character, imagination, and ambition.
Such a society could easily stop moving forward.
Once the center grew weak, the local lords could finally follow their own ideas.
Ambitious men and plotters appeared one after another.
Old chains were broken.
Reform, openness, and new life all followed.
From the economy to politics, from material life to the mind, the Spring and Autumn period was truly a golden age.
But once the old order failed, a new balance could only be rebuilt through war.
So states fought, allied, split, and reunited.
The whole Spring and Autumn era was, for the most part, a history of war.
Later generations often said, "There was no righteous war in the Spring and Autumn period."
But that saying itself has a problem.
In every age and every land, war has mostly been fought for gain.
The game of killing has little to do with what is right.
Since war exists, people must find ways to survive.
To survive, they must develop.
And that is when heroes stepped onto the stage.
The first hero to appear was named Ji Wusheng.
He was later known as Duke Zhuang of Zheng.
History's judgment of him has never been the same.
But if we look at what he actually did, we may come to see him in a new light.

### A Far-Sighted Schemer

From the very start, Zheng was no simple state.
You could even call it a state built on plotting.
Or, more kindly, a state of far-sighted planning.
King Xuan had a younger brother named Ji You.
He was brave, smart, and able to bear hardship.
During King Xuan's reign, Ji You served as Minister of Land.
That was the official in charge of land matters.
His position was very high.
King Xuan later gave him the land of Zheng.
His rank was earl.
At that time, Zheng was not yet in the area of modern Zhengzhou in Henan.
It was in Shaanxi, right next to the western tribes.
The land was small, and the situation was dangerous.
By King You's time, the western tribes were getting stronger.
And King You himself did not rule properly.
Ji You saw the situation clearly.
Zhou would decline sooner or later.
At such a time, a man had to find a way out for himself.
What makes a hero?
Knowing when to move forward and when to step back.
And knowing the weight of honor and shame.
So Ji You went secretly to see Boyangfu.
Boyangfu was one of the smartest men in Zhou.
After a few cups of wine, they got to the point.
Ji You's question was very direct.
With the state in such chaos, he feared being dragged in and losing his life.
Where could he find a piece of land to save himself?
If the state truly could not be saved, then one should save oneself first.
The trouble was that Zhou's lands had mostly been divided up already.
That was why he had come to ask Boyangfu.
Boyangfu did not hide anything.
He laid out the whole situation clearly.
To the west were the western tribes, hard to deal with.
To the south was Chu, too strong to beat.
To the north was Jin, too powerful to provoke.
To the east were Qi and Lu, neither of which could be touched.
Only the central region was left.
There lay two small states, Eastern Guo and Kuai.
One was a viscount and one a baron.
Both held low rank.
And neither ruler was loved by his people.
Instead of clashing head-on with a great state, why not first get a piece of land there?
When the time was right, the two states could be swallowed up.
Would that not make Zheng powerful?
The moment Ji You heard this, he understood.
He acted at once.
First he used heavy bribes to win over the rulers of Eastern Guo and Kuai.
Then, using his power as a central official, he asked them to give up some land.
Since the two rulers had taken the gifts, and also wanted to please the center, they each handed over ten cities.
Ji You then told King You that he had found a new place and wished to move Zheng there.
King You was happy to do him this easy favor.
He agreed at once.
So the people of Zheng moved as a whole to the area of modern Xingyang in Henan.
That was the Zheng state of later history.
Ji You himself was Duke Huan of Zheng.
He also became the founding ancestor of many family names, including Zheng, Duan, and You.
Ji You arranged Zheng's affairs well.
But he himself did not escape his fate.
Later Ji You died in battle.
His son Juetu took his place.
And, just as predicted, he later destroyed Eastern Guo and Kuai.
Zheng thus became a major power.
All of this had been within Boyangfu's plans years before.

### The First Male Lead

The first male lead of the Spring and Autumn era was about to appear.
By the old rules of Chinese history, heroes are usually born with a touch of legend.
Either the mother dreams of a god, or thunder from heaven marks the birth.
In short, something supernatural is expected.
But this one was the exact opposite.
In the year King You was killed, Duke Wu of Zheng became engaged to the younger daughter of Marquis Shen.
The girl was still young at the time.
Not until the tenth year of Duke Wu, in 761 BCE, did he formally marry her.
The histories call her Wu Jiang.
That was because she was Duke Wu's wife, and her clan name was Jiang.
Four years later, she gave birth to her first son.
Wu Jiang was a very strong woman.
Legend said she had once killed a wild boar with her bare hands.
She carried the child for ten months with no sign of giving birth.
She did not think much of it.
That day she was sleeping deeply.
In her dream, she was fighting wolves.
Suddenly she heard a baby's cry.
At first she thought she was still dreaming.
Then the crying grew more and more real.
When she opened her eyes, the child had already been born.
And it was a boy.
Because he was born as she woke from sleep, he was named Wusheng.
The word *wu* means "to wake."
Later generations gave another explanation.
They said Wusheng really meant the baby was born feet first.
That is, the feet came out before the head.
In the traditions of Shen, such a child was considered unlucky.
So Wu Jiang could not feel happy at all.
She simply gave him that name.
At the time, Duke Wu was serving at the Eastern Zhou court as a chief minister.
He could not look after matters at home in detail.
What his son was called did not matter much to him.
Three years later, Wu Jiang gave birth to a second son.
This time the birth was much more normal.
The boy was named Duan.
From then on, Wu Jiang's favoritism became very clear.
She did not like her elder son Wusheng.
But she loved her younger son Duan.
To put it a little sharply, the younger son seemed like her real child.
The elder one seemed almost borrowed from next door.
Later, when Duke Wu was dying, Wu Jiang even tried to persuade him to make Duan the heir.
Duke Wu refused flatly.
Succession by the eldest son of the main wife was the rule set by the ancestors.
Many things could be broken, but not this rule.
Not long after, Duke Wu died.
That was 744 BCE.
Wusheng was thirteen.
Duan was ten.
A short note is worth adding here.
In the Zhou period, even death had different words.
When a Zhou king died, it was called *beng*.
When a feudal lord died, it was called *hong*.
A minister's death was called *zu*.
A gentleman's death was called *bulu*.
Only common people simply "died."
So strictly speaking, Duke Wu did not just "die."

### Enduring in Silence

When Duke Zhuang of Zheng took the throne, he was only thirteen.
Even by the old way of counting age, he was only fourteen.
Duan was ten.

You inherited your father's rank and hundreds of li of land.
Yet your younger brother has only a small place like Gongcheng.
Can you really bear that?"
From childhood on, she had always scolded Wusheng.
Now that he was the ruler, she could not scold him so openly.
So she just called him by name.
Duke Zhuang did not talk back at all.
He said his mother was right.
If his younger brother wanted land, she had only to say where.
Queen Dowager Jiang first asked for Zhi.
Zhi was an important place in Zheng.
It was the area later known as Hulao Pass.
But Duke Zhuang replied that this land truly could not be given.
His father had given special orders before he died.
Zhi was a key pass of the state.
It could not be given to anyone.
Any other place would be possible.
Hearing this, Queen Dowager Jiang changed her mind and asked for Jingcheng.
Jingcheng was also a big city of Zheng.
Though slightly less strategic, it was no worse in other ways.
In fact, Duke Zhuang was even more unwilling to give up Jingcheng.
But his mother kept pushing him.
She even threatened to send Duan back to his grandfather's house.
In the end, Duke Zhuang still agreed.
He said that if his mother wanted to give something to his brother, how could he bear to refuse?
The next day he called together his ministers and announced it.
The chief minister Jizu objected at once.
Jingcheng was such a big city.
How could it be given away?
Duke Zhuang answered with only one sentence.
It was the Queen Dowager's will.
Jizu grew worried.
He knew that if this went on, trouble would come sooner or later.
But Duke Zhuang spoke words that later became famous.
He who does too much evil will destroy himself.
Just wait and see.
From then on, Duan looked less and less like an ordinary prince.
People slowly began to call him Great Uncle Duan of Jingcheng.
It was not that Duke Zhuang did not know his mother disliked him.
Nor did he fail to know that Jingcheng should not have been given to his brother.
He simply knew one thing even more clearly.
For now, he had to endure.

## Chapter 6: Even Your Own Mother Cannot Be Trusted

### The Queen Dowager's State of Mind

The Zhou dynasty had a rule.
Anyone who was given a fief had to go live there, unless he still worked in the central government.
It was a very good rule.
It saved people from a lot of suspicion and awkwardness.
That was how things worked when King Wu first gave out lands to the lords.
Even Jiang Taigong had to leave for his state right after he was given his land.
The Duke of Zhou and the Duke of Shao stayed to help the king, but only because they had official posts.
The same rule applied to the sons of the lords.
Once a prince was given land, he had to go there no matter how young he was, unless he still served as a minister.
Even if he still wet the bed, he had to go do it in his own land.
So after Duan was given Jingcheng, he had no choice but to leave Xingyang.
He had to go live in Jingcheng.
Of course, the Queen Dowager could not bear to let him go.
But there was nothing she could do.
She even wanted to go with him.
But the old family rules did not allow it.
A queen dowager had to stay in the inner palace.
In this world, every gain comes with a loss.
On the day Duan left for Jingcheng, the Queen Dowager walked him all the way to the palace gate.
Duan cried.
He was only ten years old.
He did not understand why he had to leave his mother.
The Queen Dowager cried too.
Duan had always been the apple of her eye.
He had never left her side for even a moment.
Before he left, she told the nurses and tutors again and again.
They were to take good care of Duan.
She sent along the most experienced maids, the best bedding and clothes, and even her favorite chamber pot and cook.
Duke Zhuang also sent his brother a glowing pearl.
He just did not come to see him off in person.
He knew that if his mother saw him now, she would only be more upset.
For a long time after Duan left, the Queen Dowager stayed in a bad mood.
Her temper grew worse and worse.
The maids were often punished.
Everyone knew it was because she missed Duan.
Every few days, she sent someone to Jingcheng with gifts.
When they came back, she made them tell her about Duan over and over.
Only when she heard that Duan was doing well did she feel a little better.
But as time went on, she became sad and heavy-hearted.
Her moods went up and down.
In today's words, you might say she had become depressed.
And every time she saw Duke Zhuang, she felt even more annoyed.
She often thought to herself, if only Duan had been the ruler.
Then she could have seen him every day.
Instead, she only saw the elder son who made her unhappy.
Duke Zhuang did try to cheer his mother up.
But no matter what he did, the Queen Dowager could always find something wrong.
Once a woman truly dislikes someone from the bottom of her heart, nothing that person does will ever seem right.

### Duke Zhuang's Calculations

Though he was only thirteen, Duke Zhuang understood everything.
His father, Duke Wu, had served for a long time as a chief minister at Zhou.
He had a lot of experience in political struggle.
Whenever he came home, he told his eldest son about what happened at court.
He told him that struggles at the center were harder and crueler than local ones.
From big ideas to real cases, he explained everything in great detail.
Duke Zhuang had been young at the time.
But he remembered all of it.
Now his father was dead.
And now it was his turn to use those lessons.
When his mother asked for Jingcheng for Duan, why did he agree so quickly?
Because he had thought it through very clearly.
In his eyes, his mother was only a woman.
Her plans were not very solid.
And his brother was only ten.
One woman and one child, even with Jingcheng, how much of a threat could they really be?
But if he said no, the trouble would be huge.
His mother would never let it go.
Daily fights would be the smaller problem.
What he feared most was that she might be pushed too far.
She might poison him one day when he least expected it.
Then he might die without even knowing how.
But once Jingcheng was given to Duan, his mother would have less reason to keep causing trouble.
No matter how much she disliked him, she would probably not hurt her own son unless pushed to the edge.
That was why he gave away the city so easily.
After Duan left, Duke Zhuang made two kinds of plans.
The first was to win them over with kindness.
He often went to see the Queen Dowager.
He also often sent good things to his brother.
He thought that after all, they were his own mother and brother.
If he could slowly warm up their relationship, would it not be better for the whole family to live in peace?
The second was to be on guard.
He placed his own people in Jingcheng.
They watched every move Duan made.
At the same time, he put great trust in Jizu and Prince Lü.
The former ran the state.
The latter trained the army.
When there was free time, they even used nearby small states to practice fighting.
That way, Zheng would keep its fighting power strong.
But as the Queen Dowager grew colder toward him, Duke Zhuang saw more and more clearly that the first plan was probably hopeless.
From then on, he would have to put all his weight on the second plan.

### Duan Makes His Move

At ten, Duan understood nothing.
When he first arrived in Jingcheng, he did not even dare to sleep alone.
Every night he squeezed in beside his wet nurse.
At first, the people sent by the Queen Dowager only said that she missed him very much.
Later, they began to complain that Duke Zhuang always made her angry.
Still later, the messages were almost all curses against Duke Zhuang.
At first, Duan still missed his brother.
Slowly, he stopped.
After that, he even began to hate him.
By the time he was eighteen, he hated Duke Zhuang deeply.
He believed his brother treated their mother badly.
He thought his brother was no better than a beast.
And the kindness his brother showed him, he saw as nothing but sweet lies.
There was a smile on the surface and a knife hidden below.
He was sure that sooner or later his brother would get rid of him.
So Duan decided to act.
He told himself he would save his mother from her suffering.
North of Jingcheng lay the Northern Border towns, and to the west lay the Western Border towns.
Neither of these cities had belonged to Jingcheng before.
Duan decided to take them for himself.
It was a way of testing the waters.
He called the officials of both cities to come.
He slapped the table and said, "From now on, you answer to me.
All taxes are to be sent to Jingcheng.
You no longer send them to Xingyang."
Everyone knew the Queen Dowager loved Duan more than anyone.
And everyone knew that if the Queen Dowager wanted something, Duke Zhuang was unlikely to say no openly.
The officials touched their hats.
Then they touched their necks.
Xingyang was the rightful capital, but it was far away.
Jingcheng was stepping over its bounds, but it was close.
They had no choice but to obey.
Soon, all of Zheng knew what had happened.
Duke Zhuang naturally knew as well.
He even went specially to see his mother.
The Queen Dowager was already ready for a big fight.
She thought her eldest son had come to blame her.
But to her surprise, Duke Zhuang did not mention Duan at all.
He said only a few polite words and left.
Duan was waiting too.
He waited for his brother to scold him.
Then he could pretend to be sorry and give back the taxes.
But Duke Zhuang acted as if nothing had happened.
Not long after, the Queen Dowager sent Duan a letter.
The message was simple.
Go ahead and act; your mother stands behind you.
Two years passed, and Duan felt he had gathered enough food and supplies.
But no matter how big a city is, it is nothing without an army.
So he began gathering men and horses, using hunting as an excuse.
He gathered soldiers and trained them at the same time.
The whole state of Zheng could see it clearly.
Duan was openly getting ready for war.
Duke Zhuang certainly knew.
But this time he did not even go to talk to his mother.
The Queen Dowager was happy.
She thought it proved her eldest son was either weak or foolish.
Duan was happy too.
He thought his brother was afraid of either their mother or himself.
But Duke Zhuang said only one word to himself.
Endure.

### Prince Lü's Complaint

Duke Zhuang was enduring.
But the ministers could no longer bear to watch.
Prince Lü went to see Jizu.
He said Duan had gone too far.
It was clearly the start of a rebellion.
You are a minister trusted by the late ruler.
Surely you should warn our lord.
Jizu only smiled.
He said it was not that he did not want to speak.
He too was enduring.
Prince Lü did not understand.
So Jizu told him that since he was Duke Zhuang's uncle, he should go ask the ruler himself.
So Prince Lü really did go.
Duke Zhuang welcomed his uncle warmly.
But Prince Lü spoke without holding back.
A state cannot have two centers of power.
If you mean to give the state to Duan, then I will go serve him right now.
But if you do not, then you should get rid of him sooner rather than later.
If you keep dragging it out like this, the people's trust will fall apart.
Duke Zhuang only smiled after hearing this.
He said his uncle should not worry.
He knew exactly what he was doing.
Then Prince Lü understood.
Duke Zhuang had been waiting all along.
And he had waited with great calm.

### Everything Is Ready

How long Duke Zhuang would wait depended on how far Duan wanted to go.
Now Duan had finally decided to act.
He first sent troops to take Yan and Binyan.
Then he drove away the local officials.
The whole state of Zheng was shocked.
Taking two border cities might barely be called a test.
But now he had even changed the local government.
That was open rebellion.
Duke Zhuang still did not hurry.
He let it be known on purpose that he planned to visit Luoyi, the Eastern Zhou capital.
After all, he had taken over his father's high office at Zhou.
It made sense for him to visit the capital from time to time.
The Queen Dowager was happy when she heard this.
She sent a messenger to Duan right away.
They agreed that on the day Duke Zhuang left, Duan would attack Xingyang.
She herself would be the inside helper.
At last, everything seemed ready.
Only the final piece was missing.
But the Queen Dowager still knew her eldest son far too little.
The messenger was caught the moment he left the city.
The letter he carried was found too.
It was quickly brought to Duke Zhuang.
After reading it, Duke Zhuang said only two words.
That will do.
Everything was in his hands.
This time, everything was truly ready.
And the final piece was now on his side.

### Settling the Matter

In the twenty-second year of Duke Zhuang, that is, 722 BCE, Duke Zhuang was thirty-five.
Duan was thirty-two.
That year, Duan led two hundred chariots out of Jingcheng himself.
In great order, he marched toward Xingyang.
He thought that once inside and outside forces joined that day, his brother would be stuck at the Zhou capital forever.
What he did not know was that the day had not been chosen by the Queen Dowager.
It had been chosen for him by his brother.
His brother had prepared everything ahead of time.
He had only been waiting for his brother to walk into the trap.
As soon as Duan's army left the east gate, Prince Lü's troops entered through the west gate.
By the time Duan found out his base was taken and rushed back, Prince Lü had already caught up.
The two armies faced each other.
Each side had two hundred chariots.
Prince Lü shouted first.
He said that Duan was rebelling.
He came by order of the Duke of Zheng to stop the traitor.
If the men across from him had any sense, they should break up at once and go home.
The words had barely left his mouth when a cheer of relief broke out on Duan's side.
The soldiers climbed down and took off their armor.
Then they dusted themselves off and went home.
In terms of right and wrong, Prince Lü stood for the lawful government.
In terms of plain feeling, most of Duan's men came from Jingcheng.
Their homes had been taken.
Who would not hurry back to check on his wife and children?
Duan was stunned.
How could he possibly fight this battle?
He could only run in a panic with a dozen men.
He first fled to Yan.
Prince Lü chased him there at once.
Duan had to flee again.
He fled so fast that he could not even finish peeing.
At last he reached Gongcheng.
Gongcheng was not big.
But its walls were strong enough.
At last Duan caught his breath.
He even finished what he had not finished peeing.
After lying down, he felt dizzy and lost.
He had spent twenty years building this.
In one night, it was all gone.
If he had known it would end like this, he might have been better off staying in Gongcheng from the start.
The city was small.
But there was enough to eat and enough to wear.
At least he would not have ended up like this.
But soon he found something strange.
Prince Lü's army had surrounded Gongcheng.
But it did not attack.
Nor did it send anyone to ask him to give up.
At first Duan could not understand it.
After waiting two hours, he suddenly understood everything.
It was not that his brother could not kill him.
Killing him would have been very easy.
His brother just did not want the bad name of killing his own brother.
But his brother would never let him go either.
Only two paths lay before him.
He could hang himself.
Or he could cut his own throat.
In the end, Duan chose hanging.
Before dying, he called two men he trusted.
He said that after he died, they should take his son Hua to the state of Wei.
There they should seek shelter with Prince Zhouxu.
Zhouxu was his friend.
The men asked how Prince Lü could possibly let them go.
Duan said he would.
Since his brother would not kill his own brother, he would be even less willing to kill a young nephew.
Letting the boy go would cause no great trouble later, and would make him seem kind and generous.
Duan saw this very clearly.
And so Duan died in Zheng.
A brief note should be added here.
Many people with the surname Duan think of him as their ancestor.
Why did his descendants end up using Duan as their surname?
In the Zhou period, xing and shi were not the same thing.
Names like Ji, Jiang, and Zi were clan names.
A lineage name usually came from three places.
It might come from a father's or grandfather's courtesy name.
It might come from the name of a fief.
Or it might come from a high-ranking office held by an ancestor.
A Zhou king's son was called a royal prince.
His son was called a royal grandson.
A lord's son was called a prince of the state.
That prince's son was called a grandson of the ducal house.
Duan could have been called Prince Duan.
But because he did not act like a proper prince, the Zuo Commentary simply calls him Shuduan.
By the rule that family ties grow weak after five generations, the descendants of princes who did not take the throne had to set up a new lineage name.
Usually, by the time of a grandson's son, the grandfather's name became the family name.
That is why the sons of Gongsun Hua later took Duan as their family name.

### Duke Zhuang's Anger

Later the gates of Gongcheng opened.
That was because Duan was already dead.
Duke Zhuang had arrived long before.
After entering the city, he went straight to where Duan was.
Duan lay on the bed, no longer breathing.
To be honest, Duke Zhuang had always loved this brother.
He had only this one brother from the same mother.
Duan had been clever, handsome, and polite.
Duke Zhuang often thought of him.
But now, when they met again, it was the last time.
Duke Zhuang threw himself on his brother's body and burst into tears.
Those tears were not fake.
In that moment, he was truly sad.
No matter how calm he had been before, when he saw his brother's dead body, he could not hold back his grief.
He asked the attendants what Duan had said before he died.
They said Duan had told them it was their mother who had ruined him.
After hearing this, Duke Zhuang only nodded.
He knew that at the end, his brother had finally seen the truth.
But that only made him angrier.
In his heart he said to his mother, you killed your younger son.
And you forced your elder son to bear the name of a brother-killer.
What kind of mother are you?
At that moment, Duke Zhuang had finally reached his limit.
Once you reach your limit, there is nothing left to endure.

## Chapter 7: Meeting at the Yellow Springs

Most history books tell the story of Duke Zhuang and Duan in much the same way.
Their judgment is surprisingly alike.
They all say that Duke Zhuang of Zheng let his younger brother do whatever he wanted on purpose.
And then pushed him, step by step, toward death.
But that version of the story is not really fair.
Duke Zhuang gave Duan a very long time.
He gave him time to stop and time to turn back.
Was twenty-two years not long enough?
If Duke Zhuang had truly wanted his brother dead, would he really have waited twenty-two years just to find an excuse?
The root of the trouble did not lie in Duan alone.
It lay even more in Duke Zhuang's strange and twisted mother.
Over something that had nothing to do with her son, she could still hate her own child that much.
Duke Zhuang, in truth, had almost no real choice.
He kept giving way.
He kept yielding until there was nowhere left to retreat.
Yes, he was always pretending.
But in the end, every layer of disguise was just to protect himself.
The author even makes a joke about it.
Why was he called Duke Zhuang?
Because in this whole game, he was the one holding the bank.
Duan was nothing more than a small player.
How could a lone small player ever beat the house?
And then there was that single word: endure.
If one were to name a founding master of endurance, Duke Zhuang of Zheng would surely be on the list.

### The Oath of the Yellow Springs

The Queen Dowager was pacing anxiously in the inner palace.
She had arranged with Duan to work together from inside and outside.
But the palace gates had all been sealed shut.
Guards stood watch outside with the strictest care.
At that point, no one was getting in or out.
It did not matter whether you were the Queen Dowager or a princess.
Deep down, the Queen Dowager sensed that something was wrong.
The more she thought, the more frightened she became.
At last, the palace gate opened.
But the man who entered was not Duan.
It was Prince Lü.
Prince Lü took out a whole pile of silk letters.
In those days, silk was used for writing letters.
That alone was already lavish enough.
The instant the Queen Dowager saw them, she went stiff.
They were all letters she had written to Duan.
They were filled with angry words against Duke Zhuang.
They were also full of urging Duan to rebel.
At that moment, she finally understood that she had completely misjudged her eldest son.
In a trembling voice, she asked only one question.
"Where is Duan?"
Prince Lü answered coldly, "Dead."
She asked again, "How did he die?"
"He hanged himself."
"What did he say before he died?"
"He said you were the one who destroyed him."
The Queen Dowager burst into tears on the spot.
As she wept, she admitted it herself.
In the end, it truly was she who had killed her son.
Only at that point did she truly understand where she had gone wrong.
Then she asked about Duke Zhuang.
Prince Lü said that his lord could not bear to face her.
So she was to move to Ying and live there.
In a low voice, the Queen Dowager said that it was not he who lacked the face to meet her.
It was she who had no face to meet him.
She then asked what else Duke Zhuang had said.
Prince Lü repeated the harsh words.
"Until Yellow Springs are reached, there shall be no meeting between us."
In modern words, it meant they would not see each other again until death.
Grief, shame, and self-blame crashed down on her all at once.
So the Queen Dowager set out for Ying.

### Duke Zhuang's Trouble

Duan was dead.
And the Queen Dowager had been sent away to Ying.
By all logic, Duke Zhuang ought finally to have slept soundly.
That feeling of having a thorn in his back should at last have disappeared.
But this was exactly the strange part.
He could not sleep anymore.
Some people become so used to struggle that they feel lost once it is gone.
But Duke Zhuang later found that his bigger trouble lay somewhere else.
He had driven away his own mother.
No matter how wrong she had been, she was still his mother.
When he thought of that, Duke Zhuang regretted it.
Just as he had once regretted driving his brother to death.
So his heart grew heavier and heavier.
He often tried to drown his sorrow in wine.
When he drank too much, he even sang.
As for what he sang, no one knows anymore.
It was probably the kind of song that says a child with a mother is blessed, while one without her is like grass.
He truly wanted to bring the Queen Dowager back.
But he had made an oath.
And people in ancient times took oaths very seriously.
They stood in awe of spirits and gods.
Once a vow was made, they did not dare break it lightly.
So Duke Zhuang found himself trapped in a knot with no end.
The less he could solve it, the more he thought about solving it.
And the more he thought, the more miserable he became.
In the end, he could only call in two men to talk it over.
One was Prince Lü.
The other was Jizu.
The moment Prince Lü heard it was about the Queen Dowager, he got angry at once.
In his eyes, that woman was nothing but an old monster.
If it had been up to him, he would have sent her back to Shen long ago.
Jizu, however, only said that this was a family matter.
He was not in a position to meddle.
After hearing this, Duke Zhuang could do nothing but sigh.
If even Jizu said there was no way, then perhaps there truly was no way.
But Duke Zhuang failed to notice one thing.
Jizu's first words had been "family matter."
That did not necessarily mean he had no idea.
He simply knew too well that once you step into another man's family affairs, you can easily end up making everyone angry at you.
In the whole state of Zheng, few were sharper than Jizu.
But not wanting to interfere directly did not mean he would not help Duke Zhuang in another way.

### Jizu's Clever Plan

After Jizu returned home, he first washed his feet.
And it was precisely while washing them that the plan came to him.
So the author cannot help joking that washing your feet regularly really does have its benefits.
Jizu was the second among the brothers in his family.
So he was also called Jizhong.
As for the name Jizu itself, it almost sounds like "take good care of the feet."
And since he really did love washing his feet, the author just teases him and says he might as well be the founding master of the foot-bath trade.
Once the idea was settled, Jizu right away ordered a man to fetch someone.
Whom?
Ying Kaoshu.
Kaoshu, of course, was not some "uncle in charge of exams."
Kaoshu was simply his name.
He lived in Ying.
So he was also called Ying Kaoshu.
He held a small office in Ying.
His family was so poor you could hear the coins rattle.
But there was one thing he was famous for.
He was deeply devoted to his mother.
How devoted?
The Zuo Commentary even praises him as "pure in filial devotion."
When Jizu met Kaoshu, his very first question was this:
"Is your mother well?"
Why ask about his mother first?
Because if you did not start with that, Kaoshu might truly have been unhappy.
Kaoshu said his mother was doing well.
Jizu then asked whether he wanted a promotion.
Kaoshu said yes.
If his office rose even a little, he would receive more grain.
Jizu asked again whether he wanted to grow rich.
Kaoshu again said yes.
With money, his mother could finally eat meat.
So Jizu told him that he could show him a path to promotion and wealth.

### Kaoshu's Owls

A year passed in the blink of an eye.
Throughout that year, Duke Zhuang stayed in low spirits.
One day, a report suddenly came in.
A minor official had arrived from Ying.
His name was Kaoshu.
And he had brought two owls.
He said they were a gift for the ruler.
At first Duke Zhuang was in too foul a mood.
On hearing this, he said he would not see him.
But the words had hardly left his mouth before he felt that something was odd.
So he changed his mind at once.
He said Kaoshu should be let in after all.
Why did Duke Zhuang find it strange?
Because under Zhou ritual, one was supposed to bring a proper gift at a first meeting.
The Son of Heaven gave ritual wine.
Feudal lords gave jade tablets.
High ministers gave lambs.
Senior officers gave geese.
Gentlemen gave pheasants.
Commoners gave ducks.
Every gift carried a meaning.
By rank, Kaoshu counted as a gentleman.
By rights, he should have brought pheasants.
Yet he had brought two owls instead.
That was what truly made Duke Zhuang curious.

The pressure was not so heavy.

At last, the moving scene was ready to unfold.

## Chapter 8: Even Your Own Father Cannot Be Trusted

### Shi Hou's Clever Plan

People often say that winning power is easy, but holding on to it is hard.

War.
Pick a state and fight it.
War, he said, had two benefits.

Second, it could turn people's anger inside the country toward an enemy outside.

Zhouxu's eyes lit up the moment he heard this.

Shi Hou had already thought that through.
Great powers were too strong to pick a fight with.
Very small states were not worth the trouble.
The best target would be a state of roughly equal strength.

Zheng was perfect.
Duke Zhuang of Zheng had driven away his mother and caused his brother's death.
That alone was enough to serve as an excuse.

Their cause would look even more respectable.
Once the direction was set, the next step was to gather allies.

Shi Hou did a quick count.
Lu, Chen, and Cai would not be hard to bring in.
A few favors would do the trick.
The real problem was Song.

It would not be easily moved.

### The Story of Song

Since Song has come up, we may as well tell its origins too.

Before he died, he did not leave the throne to his son.

Duke Mu was also a good man.

So he gave the task to Right Prince Ji Zhi.
After taking the job, Right Prince thought it over carefully.
Jizi was so outstanding
that he deserved the most beautiful girl in the world.
After asking around,
everyone said the eldest daughter of Duke Xi of Qi was the most beautiful.
So Right Prince took gifts and papers to Qi.
As soon as Duke Xi heard it was a proposal for Jizi,
he agreed at once.
Before the envoy left,
he even had his eldest daughter come out to meet him.
That way Right Prince could feel sure.
After returning to Wei,
Right Prince praised the princess to the skies.
He said she and Jizi were a perfect match.
As Duke Xuan listened,
a dark desire began to stir in him.
Right Prince never dreamed
that Duke Xuan would want his own future daughter-in-law.

### The New Tower and the Seizing of a Son's Bride

Duke Xuan built a house by the Qi River.
He named it the New Tower.
The reason he gave sounded proper enough.
He said it was for ministers to rest and escape the heat.
In truth, he had other plans.
The New Tower was built fast and richly.
Its design was beautiful.
And the materials were the best.
Once the New Tower was done,
Duke Xuan began to act.
He called for Jizi.
He said the ruler of Song was about to have a birthday.
"Go in my place.
It will be good for you to see a bit of the world."
And so Jizi was sent off to Song.
Right Prince went with him too.
As soon as Jizi was gone,
Duke Xuan called for Left Prince Xie.
He ordered him to go bring the bride from Qi.
Before he left, Duke Xuan gave him special orders.
The bride was not to be taken to the capital.
She was to be brought straight to the New Tower.
"I must check her for Jizi first."
The princess of Qi traveled all the way to Wei.
She knew the man she was to marry
was the famous Jizi.
Naturally, she was shy and happy.
When she reached the New Tower,
she saw a palace that was grand and new.
She even thought Jizi had made it ready just for her.
That made her even more excited.
But the wedding itself
felt a little strange.
It was not very lively.
There were not many guests either.
Though she found it strange,
she did not think too much about it.
Once the rites were done,
the bride and groom went into the bridal room.
The groom blew out the lamp as soon as he came in.
Then he undressed and got into bed.
The princess felt only this:
two thick, heavy hands grabbed her.
Then a heavy, fat body pressed down on her.
She was so let down that she could not help asking,
"Jizi, why are you so fat?"
What answered her
was only heavy breathing.
She asked again,
"Jizi, why won't you speak?"
What came back to her
was still only breathing.
She felt even more let down.
She had pictured Jizi
as a slim, strong young man.
But the man in the bed
was fat and knew what he was doing.
His hands were too skilled.
He had too many tricks.
He was nothing like a young man who had never been married.
And so the night passed.

At daybreak,
the princess woke up to the sound of snoring.
She turned to look at her husband.
It would have been better if she had not looked.
But one look gave her goosebumps all over.
The man lying beside her
was not Jizi at all.
He had a puffy face and big ears.
His face was covered with marks.
His mouth hung open.
And spit ran from the corner of his mouth.
How could this be the seventeen-year-old Jizi?
He looked just like a frozen toad.
The princess screamed.
The ugly man woke up.
And gave her an oily smile.
Her voice shaking, she asked,
"Y-you are Jizi?"
The man laughed and said,
"I am Jizi's..."
"father."
The princess fainted right there.
And that is how the poem "New Tower" came into the Book of Songs.

After taking his own son's bride,
whether from guilt
or because he wanted to enjoy her in peace,
Duke Xuan stayed at the New Tower and would not leave.
Though the princess came from a great state,
once she was in Duke Xuan's hands,
she had no way out.
She cried and begged.
She even tried to kill herself.
None of it worked.
In the end, she had to accept her fate.
Even when Duke Xi of Qi later learned what had happened,
he could only accept it.
What was done could not be undone.
There was no turning back.
From then on,
the princess of Qi was no longer Jizi's bride-to-be.
She became Duke Xuan's wife, Xuan Jiang.
He took his father's woman, and then took his son's bride.
This Duke Xuan of Wei
truly went after three generations in one go.

When did he finally return to the capital?
Not until Xuan Jiang's belly had begun to grow.
After returning to the capital,
the first thing Duke Xuan did
was hurry to find Jizi another wife.
That was, in a way, to make up for it.
Jizi accepted it without a word.
He was a good son.
He was willing to give up the best to his father.
Besides,
he had no real choice.
After Jizi married,
he moved out of the palace.
Duke Xuan's rush to arrange that marriage
had a simple purpose.
First, he wanted his son far away.
Second, he did not want Jizi and Xuan Jiang to meet.
To put it plainly,
he feared his son might take away the woman who had been meant for him.
Xuan Jiang grew even sadder because of it.
She stayed in her room all day and would not come out.
That suited Duke Xuan perfectly.

### A Chance Encounter Rekindles Hope

Before long,
Xuan Jiang's belly grew larger and larger.
After ten months, she gave birth.
She had a lovely baby prince.
His name was Shou.
The child's birth
made Xuan Jiang feel much better.
No matter what,
the child was lovely.
Slowly,
she was willing to come outside carrying him.
She would look at flowers and play with fish and bugs.
The world is, in truth, very small.
So Xuan Jiang meeting Jizi
was almost certain to happen.
That day, the sunlight was just right.
The breeze was light too.
A maid was carrying the child.
Xuan Jiang was walking slowly through the palace.
A young prince came walking toward her.
Who?
Jizi himself.
Though he had moved out long ago,
he still often came back to see his mother.
The moment they saw each other,
both froze.
Xuan Jiang's heart was struck first.
So this was Jizi?
The young man before her
was tall and straight, full of grace.
He was even more handsome than the rumors said.
Jizi was no different.
He had always known Xuan Jiang was beautiful.
But he had never imagined beauty like this.
Even though she had given birth not long ago,
she was still dazzling.
Their eyes met.
And both their faces turned red.
Even the maids nearby were stunned.
They looked like the golden boy and jade girl from legend.
Jizi hurried away.
He did not dare turn his head.
Because he could clearly feel
Xuan Jiang's burning eyes
still on his back.
After that chance meeting,
both of them fell into pain.
Xuan Jiang had already accepted her fate.
But this time,
hope came back to life in her heart.
Jizi was the man she wanted.
He was the man she was supposed to have.
She even thought
that if she could leave with Jizi,
even if they ran away to the edge of the land,
and worked in the fields,
she would be happy to do it.
Xuan Jiang was bold in both thought and action.
She made up her mind quickly.
She would find a chance to run away with Jizi.
For Jizi, the struggle was harder.
People are not wood or grass.
Before a beauty like this,
it is hard for anyone to stay calm.
Jizi had held back again and again before.
He had even tried to accept his fate.
But now
he could not forget Xuan Jiang's face.
Still less could he forget her beautiful, haunting eyes.
He felt as if all kinds of feelings had been stirred up inside him.
Sweet, sour, bitter, and spicy, all mixed together.
He could not help thinking
that she was supposed to be his.
But what should he do?
He was not as firm as Xuan Jiang.
But he truly could not hold back
the urge to see her again.

Though they still wanted different things,
their actions had already come to a silent agreement.
Xuan Jiang began visiting Yi Jiang more often.
On the surface, she went to chat.
In truth, she hoped to meet Jizi.
Jizi also came more often.
On the surface, he was visiting his mother.
In truth, he also hoped to see Xuan Jiang.
And Duke Xuan, at this time,
had let down his guard.
He thought that since Xuan Jiang had given birth to Prince Shou,
she would be completely loyal to him.
The two did indeed meet.
And not just once.
Every time they met,
Xuan Jiang sent him secret looks.
Jizi sometimes grew tense.
Sometimes he could only smile.
Because Yi Jiang was there,
that was all they could do.
At last, one day,
they met again in Yi Jiang's room.
And Yi Jiang had just stepped out for a moment.
The room was left to just the two of them.
Xuan Jiang could no longer hold back.
She asked straight out,
"Why is it
that every time I look at you, you pretend not to see?"
Jizi did not dare look her in the eye.
He feared that one look would break his will.
He only said he truly had not seen.
Xuan Jiang pressed on at once.
"Am I not beautiful enough?"
Jizi said, "No. You are very beautiful."
Xuan Jiang asked again,
"Do you not know
that I was supposed to be yours?"
Jizi paused.
Then he said in a low voice,
"But he is my father."
Xuan Jiang shot back at once,
"And what about your father?
I am yours.
Why won't you take me away?"
For a moment Jizi had no answer.
In truth, the thought
had crossed his mind before.
He just had never been able to decide.
At last, he spoke his greatest worry.
"What about Prince Shou?"
Xuan Jiang answered almost without thinking.
"Take him with us."
Jizi grew more worried.
"But Prince Shou is my brother."
The meaning of those words was already clear.
It would mix up the bonds of family.
And just then,
Yi Jiang came back.
And the conversation was cut short.

### Eloping, One Step Too Late

After that conversation,
Jizi's heart became even more troubled.
Between his father and the woman he wanted,
he could choose only one.
It was a very hard choice.
Some nights he dreamed
that he had run far away with Xuan Jiang.
Other nights he dreamed
that he was saying no to her firmly.
He could not make up his mind.
In a flash, more than two months passed.
During those two months,
he no longer dared go to Yi Jiang's room.
He feared seeing Xuan Jiang.
More than that, he feared those haunting eyes.
When in trouble, a man thinks of his teacher.
At last, Jizi gathered his courage.
He told Right Prince all his trouble.
"Master, what should I do?"
Right Prince did not answer at once.
He first asked Jizi,
"Do you really care so much about being the heir?"
Jizi said he did not care much about it.
After hearing that, Right Prince
took a firm stand right away.
"Then take her and go.
There is no need to flee to the Rong tribes.
Go straight to Qi.
The duke of Qi wanted you as his son-in-law from the start.
He will not blame you."
Right Prince had been troubled by this all along.
In his heart,
Duke Xuan was a truly low man.
Jizi still had his doubts.
"If we do this, it will look very bad."
But Right Prince spoke even more plainly.
"What does proper name matter now?
Do you not know
that your mother once belonged to your grandfather?
Your father could do such a thing.
Why should you alone not dare?
Besides,
she was originally your wife."
All in all,
Right Prince spent a long time talking him into it.
At last he moved Jizi's heart.
Jizi finally nodded.
"Very well. I will do as you say."
Once the decision was made,
Jizi began to prepare.
Travel money, clothes, and route-
all were made ready.
Another ten days passed.
Jizi went into the palace to visit his mother.
On the surface, he was visiting his mother.
In truth, he had come to find Xuan Jiang.
While mother and son were talking,
Jizi casually asked how Xuan Jiang had been.
Yi Jiang answered,
"She has not come by in more than a month."
Jizi was surprised.
He hurried to ask why.
Yi Jiang answered calmly,
"She is pregnant again."
Jizi felt his head spin.
In that moment,
he knew it was all too late.
Before, there had only been Prince Shou.
That had been trouble enough.
Now there was another child not yet born.
Soon there would be two younger brothers.
How could he take her away now?
And besides, she was already far along.
By the time she gave birth,
every chance would be long gone.

And indeed, the chance was gone for good.
Xuan Jiang later became the mother of two sons.
At last she gave up the thought of running away.
For Jizi, it was more than giving up.
It was as if his heart had died.
In time,
the children grew up too.
The older was Shou.
The younger was Shuo.
Jizi pressed all his pain down inside.
He still loved Xuan Jiang.
But he also knew
that none of it was possible anymore.
So he turned the love he felt for Xuan Jiang
toward her two sons.
He liked Shou, and he liked Shuo too.
Whenever he had the chance,
he took them out to play.
But Xuan Jiang knew nothing of this.
She knew only one thing.
Jizi had refused her.
And so she began to hate him.
She hated that she could not have him.
She no longer dreamed of running away with him.
Instead, she began to think of revenge.
The most terrible hatred in the world
is often born from love.
And when a woman turns from love to hate,
it can be even more frightening.

### Revenge Finally Arrives

In a flash, sixteen years passed.
It was now the eighteenth year of Duke Xuan of Wei.
That is, 701 BCE.
Jizi was thirty-three.
Prince Shou was sixteen too.
And Duke Xuan, worn down by too much pleasure,
was growing weaker day by day.
Over all those years,
Xuan Jiang had never found a chance to strike at Jizi.
In truth, she knew why.
Jizi's conduct was too good.
His actions were too careful.
He gave almost nothing to attack.
And throughout Wei,
too many people truly liked him.
But Xuan Jiang also knew
that her time was running out.
Once Duke Xuan died,
and Jizi took the throne,
she would never have another chance.
So she decided to move first.
That day Duke Xuan was sick in bed again.
Xuan Jiang sat by the bed with Prince Shuo.
She sent all the maids away.
Then she began to cry.
Her crying woke Duke Xuan from sleep.
The moment he opened his eyes, he asked,
"Why are you crying?
Is it because I am about to die?"
Xuan Jiang answered
that it was not he who was going to die.
It was she and her two sons who could no longer live.
Duke Xuan was completely confused.
"Why would the three of you die?"
That was when Xuan Jiang began to lie about Jizi.
She said Jizi had long hated her and her sons.
He swore every day to kill Shou and Shuo.
And then take her back as his wife.
The moment Duke Xuan heard this, his heart tightened.
For what he feared most
was his son taking Xuan Jiang away.
But words alone
still made him hesitate.
Seeing that hesitation,
Xuan Jiang pulled Prince Shuo over at once.
She said,
"If you will not act,
then sooner or later I will become Jizi's wife anyway.
Shuo, come, call him Grandfather."
That struck Duke Xuan at his weakest point.
Such a shame
was something he could never bear.
So at last he asked,
"Then what should be done?"
Xuan Jiang answered coldly,
"If he does not die, then we must.
You decide."
Duke Xuan clenched his teeth.
At last he decided to kill his eldest son.
But he could not kill him openly.
That would ruin his name completely.
So he came up with an evil plan.
Qi was preparing to attack Ji.
Wei too had to send a man to set the date for the troops.
The envoy would carry the white mao banner.
Part of the journey would be by boat.
At Xinye he would get off the boat.
If men were hidden there,
and told to kill whoever carried the white mao,
then they could bring back the banner and the head for a reward.
That was Duke Xuan's plan.
What was the white mao?
It was the flag carried by a lord's envoy.
White yak-tail hair was tied to the top.
And for that very reason,
the killers only needed to know the banner, not the man.
Duke Xuan and Xuan Jiang thought they had planned for everything.
But they did not realize
that walls can have ears.

## Chapter 10: His Love Affair with Cranes

Two young men drifted away in the same boat.
The boat floated on, while their hearts were full of worry.
They could only hope the other would stay safe.
These lines come from "Two Sons in a Boat" in the *Airs of Bei* part of the *Book of Songs*.
They were written to remember Jizi and Prince Shou.
Such brotherly love, with each one trying to die in the other's place, feels like something only the Spring and Autumn age could produce.

### Brotherly Devotion

Prince Shou happened to overhear Duke Xuan and Xuan Jiang's plot.
People may grow up in the same palace, yet one becomes cruel and greedy, while another becomes kind and loyal.
Duke Xuan of Wei was rotten to the core.
Yet somehow he had two fine sons.
Heaven, at times, is hard to judge as fair or unfair.
By all logic, Prince Shou had the most to gain from Jizi's death.
If Jizi died, the throne would be his.
But that thought never crossed his mind.
He thought of only one thing: his elder brother must not die.
He had to save him.
Prince Shou went to see Jizi at once.
"Brother, you must run."
He told him the whole plot in one breath.
As long as Jizi escaped Wei, his good name would make him an honored guest wherever he went.
Even if Prince Shou later became ruler, he was willing to give the throne back to his brother.
But Jizi answered with only one sentence.
"I cannot go against my father's order just to save my own life."
He said it calmly.
But deep down he understood better than anyone that the one who truly wanted him dead was Xuan Jiang.
He knew she hated him.
And yet, deep down, he still loved her.
If his death could bring her even a little peace, then perhaps it would be worth it.
Prince Shou pleaded with him again and again.
But Jizi would not give an inch.
Seeing he could not change his brother's mind, Prince Shou left in despair.
On the night before Jizi left, Prince Shou came to see him off.
The two brothers drank through the whole night.
Both of them knew this was most likely their last meeting.
Jizi was the elder brother, but he could not drink as much as Shou.
Cup after cup, until Jizi finally fell into a deep sleep.
At dawn, Jizi woke up.
"Where is my brother?"
"Prince Shou has already left."
Jizi meant to leave at once.
But just as he was about to leave, he found that the white banner was gone.
Only one outsider had come the night before.
Aside from the servants, that person was Prince Shou.
In an instant, Jizi understood.
"Go find him, now."
Word came back soon enough.
Prince Shou had taken some servants and sailed away with the white banner.
Jizi went pale with fear.
He knew better than anyone what that banner meant.
"Launch the boat at once."
All the way, he urged the rowers to go faster.
He had to catch Prince Shou before they reached Xinye.
The boat was already fast, but to Jizi it was still too slow.
As they neared Xinye, he finally saw a boat ahead.
He had just breathed a sigh of relief when a servant pointed something out.
They had not caught up.
That boat was coming straight toward them.
Jizi's heart sank again.
The two boats drew closer and closer.
Only a few meters separated them.
Sword in hand, Jizi leapt across and landed lightly on the other boat.
His own men were stunned.
But none of them could follow.
On the other boat, Prince Shou was nowhere to be seen.
There were only a dozen fierce-looking men, each holding a blade.
Jizi looked around the deck and saw the white banner lying in a corner.
"You killed him?"
The men nodded.
Jizi still refused to believe it.
"Where is he?"
One of them held up a severed head.
It was Prince Shou.
Jizi broke down in tears.
"Brother, why did you do this?"
"When you die, Father will grieve too."
The killers on the boat were terrified.
They had just killed the younger brother.
Now the elder brother had come for them.
From that leap alone, anyone would have thought him a master fighter.
If they had known this was Jizi, the finest swordsman in Wei, they would never have taken the job.
Jizi raised his head.
Every one of them thought he was about to take revenge.
But he did not draw his sword.
He only said, "You killed the wrong man."
"The one who should have died was me, not him."
He said it with complete sincerity.
That only frightened them more.
"Come, kill me."
Jizi even stepped toward them.
It was the first time in their lives someone had begged them for death.
All they could think was that it had to be a trap.
The more Jizi begged, the harder they trembled.
"Please, kill me."
"Otherwise you will never get your reward."
The more earnest he sounded, the less they dared to move.
Jizi grew desperate.
With a shout, he strode toward them.
The leader panicked completely.
He thought Jizi was attacking.
In blind panic, he slashed with his blade.
Steel flashed.
Blood burst forth.
Jizi fell.
The men stood frozen for a long while.
Only then did they realize that there really are people who walk willingly to their deaths.
They were river outlaws who lived by robbery and murder.
They had taken money to kill for someone.
What they never knew was that they had killed two princes of Wei.
So they took Jizi's head as well.
One more head cost them nothing, and Jizi's words might have been true.
The author then adds a very dark joke.
If someone begs too hard to be killed, he may end up scaring the killer instead.
As for Jizi and Shou, they loved each other deeply.
And each tried to die in the other's place.
The people of Wei were so moved that they wrote "Two Sons in a Boat."
By the time you reach this point, you can only let out a long sigh.
The distance between one human heart and another can truly be this vast.

### Incest

The two severed heads were placed before Duke Xuan.
He stared at them in shock.
His two finest sons had both been destroyed by his own hand.
Worse still, he had to pay silver to reward the men who had killed his sons.
Xuan Jiang fainted as well.
Both of them had been determined to kill Jizi.
Yet when they saw the severed head with their own eyes, they regretted it.
What they had not expected was that Prince Shou had died with him.
Compared to his two sons, Duke Xuan was hardly worth mentioning.
After that shock, he died in less than half a year.
Those who ruin others often ruin themselves as well.
Prince Shuo took the throne and became Duke Hui of Wei.
He was only fifteen years old.
But after only four years on the throne, Left Prince Xie, Right Prince Zhi, together with the grandee Ning Gui, rose up to seek justice for Jizi.
They joined forces and drove Duke Hui away.
Duke Hui fled to Qi and sought refuge with his uncle, Duke Xiang of Qi.
Jizi's younger brother Qian Mou was made ruler instead.
At that point, a difficult question arose.
What should be done with Xuan Jiang?
By normal practice, she should have been stripped of her rank and sent back to Qi.
But she happened to be Duke Xiang's sister.
And Wei could not afford to offend Qi.
So the men of Wei debated the matter back and forth.
In the end they came up with a compromise.
She would move out of the palace, live in a fine mansion, keep her guards, and keep all the privileges of a queen mother.
The people of Wei thought this was already generous.
But Duke Xiang of Qi was not satisfied.
He had long wanted to win the throne back for his nephew.
He simply had one concern.
At the time, he was trying to marry into the Zhou royal family, and Qian Mou happened to be the Zhou king's son-in-law.
So it was awkward to break with him openly.
But not breaking with him did not mean he had no schemes.
Qian Mou had a younger brother named Zhaobo, also known as Prince Shu.
Duke Xiang suddenly had a wild idea.
He wanted to protect his sister and did not want her to stay a widow.
So why not marry Xuan Jiang to Prince Shu?
Xuan Jiang herself was willing enough.
But Prince Shu refused outright.
She was his stepmother.
She had even given his father two sons.
If that was not incest, what was?
But once the banner of "state interest" was raised, almost any absurd thing could be made to look reasonable.
Under pressure from both Duke Xiang and Qian Mou, Prince Shu finally married his stepmother.
More absurd still, the two of them grew quite devoted to each other.
Together they had five children.
The eldest died young.
The second son, Prince Shen, and the third, Prince Hui, both later became rulers of Wei.
Of the two daughters, one married Duke Huan of Song and the other married Duke Mu of Xu.
The latter became Lady Mu of Xu, a famous poetess in the *Book of Songs*.
One woman had married a father and then his two sons.
She bore three rulers and two noble wives.
Xuan Jiang's life was a complete monument to absurdity.

### Duke Yi and His Cranes

Wei's theater of the absurd was far from over.
Eight years after Duke Hui was driven out, Duke Xiang of Qi finally sent troops.
Wei was no match at all.
The Left and Right Princes were killed.
Because Qian Mou was the Zhou king's son-in-law, he was sent to the Zhou capital to live with his royal in-laws.
Ning Gui fled to Qin.
And so Duke Hui was restored to the throne.
He did not stay quiet for long.
Twelve years later, angry that Zhou had sheltered Qian Mou, he actually marched against the Zhou king.
King Hui of Zhou was driven out, and Prince Tui was put in his place.
Six years later, Duke Hui died.
His son succeeded him.
This was Duke Yi of Wei.
The year was 669 BCE.
Duke Yi perfectly inherited Wei's family talent for absurd behavior.
But he differed from his grandfather in one way.
His grandfather had been obsessed with women.
Duke Yi was not drawn to women at all.
He was obsessed with only one thing: cranes.
He loved cranes to the point of madness.
If he took a fancy to one, he would pay any price to buy it.
Once word spread, people from everywhere came bringing cranes as gifts.
Inside the palace, there were not many maids, but there were more cranes than maids.
And the maids' real duty was no longer serving people, but serving cranes.
If he lived in the modern world, he might have made an excellent head of an animal protection society.
Duke Yi kept hundreds of cranes.
When he stayed home, he spent his time playing among them.
When he went out, he had to bring the cranes along.
They marched in two neat lines, in perfect order.
So every outing by Duke Yi became a grand spectacle.
But it did not stop there.
If he had only kept cranes, that still would not have been the worst part.
Duke Yi also had too much imagination.
He gave the cranes ranks and titles.
There were crane ministers, crane generals, and crane ladies.
The *Records of the Grand Historian* even says that some cranes rode in carriages.
And these titles were not just empty honors.
They came with real privileges.
When crane officials went out, they had carriages.
They had guards.
At times there was even traffic control for them.
The crane ladies had their own rooms.
Duke Yi even "visited" them as if they were human wives, doing things more shameful than beasts.
At that rate, he almost seemed to wish for a crane child of his own.
Whenever a crane laid eggs, he would secretly hope it might hatch a half-human, half-crane creature.
If a crane died from too much royal favor, he would even bury it with the honors of a queen.
He poured all his energy into cranes.
The people went hungry, and he could not be bothered to care.
While ordinary people lacked food, the cranes feasted on delicacies.
Human generals could win glory and still not be promoted, while crane generals were promoted at the slightest whim.
At court, the cranes stood even closer to the throne than men did.
More maddening still, they would sometimes perch over someone's head and relieve themselves.
Complaints filled the whole state.
Other than the cranes, every human being hated Duke Yi.
In truth, not even all the cranes were happy.
At least the male cranes were probably burning with jealousy.
So the people of Wei gave him a nickname: "Duke Crane of Wei."
At this point, you can only sigh for Kang Shu of Wei.
Such a worthy founder, yet what followed him was a line of descendants who dressed like men but were no better than beasts.

### Chilled Crane Meat

Retribution always comes.
Only the timing differs.
In the ninth year of Duke Yi's reign, that is, in 661 BCE, the Red Di tribes marched south.
They passed through Xing and headed straight for Wei.
The moment the news arrived, all of Wei was thrown into panic.
Duke Yi, who had lost himself in raising cranes, only then remembered that there were things more important than pampering cranes.
He hurried to gather troops.
He also tried to train them in haste.
But everything was already too late.
The common people fled, and those who stayed scattered.
Almost no one was willing to serve.
If you treat the people as less than human in ordinary days, what right do you have to ask them to die for you in war?
The generals all began pleading illness as well.
If victory brought no reward, who would want to risk his life?
Duke Yi could not make sense of it.
These very people, when disaster stood at the gate, had suddenly stopped loving their country.
"Why?" he demanded angrily of his ministers.
By then the ministers could no longer hold back.
They gave him a single answer.
"Since Your Grace loves cranes, let the cranes fight the Di."
That line is recorded in the *Records of the Grand Historian*.
Its meaning could not have been clearer.
Those crane generals only had to shake their feathers and sway their bodies to rank above real men.
If that was how things worked, then they could go to the battlefield.
That finally humbled Duke Yi.
"I know I was wrong."
"What should be done now?"
Shi Qizi answered at once.
He was the grandson of Shi Que.
"What should be done? Serve it cold."
The sarcasm in that reply was sharp enough to cut.
But Duke Yi was too panicked to hear the mockery.
He pressed on.
"How exactly do we serve it cold?"
Shi Qizi then laid it out plainly.
What the people hated most was, of course, you.
What they hated second most was your cranes.
If you want to save your own life, you must kill the cranes.
Chop them up, boil them, make them into a cold dish, and give them to the people.
That way they can vent their anger.
The moment Duke Yi heard this, he agreed at once.
No pet, however loved, was more important than his own life.
So crane generals, crane ministers, and crane ladies alike, every single crane, met with disaster.
It was, quite possibly, the bloodiest massacre in crane history.
Chilled crane meat was served in the streets.
The people of Wei rushed to eat it.
In those days, of course, no one worried about bird flu.
In the end, what crime had the cranes really committed?
Still, whatever the justice of it, the common people had at last vented their rage.
And at last some men were willing to serve.
The cranes, in the end, had simply become scapegoats.

### The Fall of Wei

When war finally came, all Wei could scrape together was fewer than two hundred chariots.
The reason was not complicated.
In the nine years after Duke Yi took the throne, Wei had scarcely trained its army.
Nor had it added many new chariots.
The old chariots, meanwhile, had fallen into ruin.
Where had the military funds gone?
The answer was painfully simple.
They had all been poured into the cranes.
Buying cranes cost money.
Keeping cranes cost money.
Maintaining feathers cost money.
Preparing nesting rooms for the crane ladies cost money.
Building chambers and special carriages cost money.
Every part of it consumed money.
The generals did not train soldiers either.
Those with connections raised cranes.
Those without them raised chickens.
In their own way, nobody stayed idle.
These hundred-odd chariots, along with a few thousand volunteers, were nowhere near enough for a real war.
Only at this point did Duke Yi remember to ask other states for help.
But the same answer remained: it was too late.
Over those nine years, Wei had almost stopped dealing with the other states.
The old diplomats had long since retired.
Those who remained had little real experience.
The envoys made their rounds, but the other states answered much the same way.
"Where had you been all this time?"
"Did you not have crane generals?"
At that point, even chilled crane meat could no longer save the situation.
Only Qi and Song gave something like a polite answer.
One said it would see what could be done.
The other, out of old friendship, was willing to consider it.
But distant water cannot quench a nearby thirst.
In the end, Wei still had to rely on itself.
So Duke Yi personally led the army north.
Perhaps, in his eyes, once the crane ladies were gone, life had lost much of its meaning.
For that very reason, he actually fought with great courage.
But the Red Di were simply too strong.
They were many, and they were fierce.
Under a storm of blades, Duke Yi himself was chopped into a cold dish.
One imagines his soul ran after the crane ladies.
After the army of Wei was crushed, the Red Di advanced without resistance.
They entered the capital, massacred the people, and set the city on fire.
The ruling clan of Wei fled south in panic.
They ran all the way to the Yellow River.
Only because Song sent boats to meet them did they make it across to the southern bank.
When they counted heads at Cao, only seven hundred and twenty people were left.
Later, people from Gong and Teng were brought in to add to the numbers.
Only then did they scrape together more than four thousand people.
Only then could they barely establish a new ruler.
Shen, the son of Prince Shuo, was enthroned as Duke Dai of Wei.
But Duke Dai lasted only a few days.
He soon died as well.
So his younger brother Hui was enthroned, and he became Duke Wen of Wei.
From that point on, Wei declined steadily and never truly recovered.
At this point, it is worth rethinking Shi Que's old act of putting principle above family.
On the level of grand principle, Shi Que's killing of Zhouxu, the usurper, can certainly be justified.
But if we place Zhouxu beside the later rulers of Wei and compare them one by one, a troubling question appears.
After Zhouxu, Wei was chiefly ruled by three men.
Duke Xuan, Duke Hui, and Duke Yi.
Aside from lust and incest, Duke Xuan was almost useless in every other way.
Zhouxu, by contrast, was at least a capable young man who could fight.
Then consider Duke Hui.
He actually dared to lead troops against the Zhou king.
He drove King Hui of Zhou away and installed another ruler in his place.
That was an even greater act of rebellion than mere usurpation.
He did it simply because the Zhou king had sheltered his elder brother Qian Mou.
In terms of spirit, Duke Hui fell far short.
When Duke Xuan fled to the small state of Xing, Zhouxu did not send troops to drag him back.
As for Duke Yi, he ruined his state through his obsession with cranes, and cannot even be compared with Zhouxu.
Still more persuasive is another fact.
Zhouxu's grandfather, Duke Wu of Wei, was Wei's finest ruler since Kang Shu.
Under his rule, Wei grew stronger, and its rank rose from marquis to duke.
But how had Duke Wu himself taken the throne?
He too had killed his elder brother and seized power.
If that is so, then if Zhouxu had not been removed by Shi Que, he might well have become a second Duke Wu of Wei.
So when you hear of usurpation, do not immediately assume it must be evil.
In the stories that follow, there will be many more examples.
