# 《暖暖》第三章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

早上漱洗完、用過早飯後，先在教室聽課。  
That morning, after washing up and breakfast, we sat through a lecture in the classroom.  
有個對長城很有研究的學者，要來跟我們講述長城的種種。  
A scholar who'd made a deep study of the Great Wall had come to talk to us about it.  
他還拿出一塊巴掌大的長城小碎磚，要同學們試試它的硬度。  
He even brought out a palm-sized piece of brick from the Wall and invited us to test its hardness.  
「可用你身上任何部位，弄碎了有賞。」他笑說。  
"Use any part of your body. If you crack it, you get a prize," he said with a smile.  
這小碎磚傳到我手上時，我跟學弟說：『來，頭借我。』  
When the fragment reached me, I turned to my junior. "Here — lend me your head."  
「你要豬頭幹嘛？」學弟回答。  
"What do you want a pig's head for?" he shot back.  

我不想理他。  
I ignored him.  
雙手握緊碎磚，使盡吃奶力氣，幻想自己是《七龍珠》裡的悟空，  
I gripped the brick in both hands, summoned every ounce of strength, and pictured myself as Goku from *Dragon Ball*,  
口中還啊啊啊啊啊叫著，準備變身成超級賽亞人。  
yelling "*aaaaaah!*" as I prepared to transform into a Super Saiyan.  
『碎了。』我說。  
"It cracked," I said.  
「真碎了？」暖暖很驚訝。  
"It really cracked?" Nuannuan was startled.  
『我的手指頭碎了。』  
"My fingers cracked."  
這次輪到暖暖不想理我。  
This time it was Nuannuan's turn to ignore me.  

十點左右上完課，老師們意味深長地讓大家準備一下，要去爬長城了。  
The lecture ended around ten, and with meaningful looks the teachers told us to get ready: we were going to climb the Great Wall.  
記得昨晚老師千叮嚀萬囑咐要穿好走的鞋、女同學別發浪穿啥高跟鞋、  
The night before they had hammered home the rules — wear comfortable shoes; girls, don't get clever with high heels;  
帶瓶水、別把垃圾留在長城、誰敢在長城磚上簽名誰就死定了等等。  
bring water; don't litter on the Wall; sign your name on a brick and you're dead — and so on.  
『還要準備什麼？』我很好奇問暖暖：『難道要打領帶？』  
"What more is there to prepare?" I asked Nuannuan curiously. "Are we putting on neckties?"  
「我估計是要大家做好心理準備，免得樂暈了。」暖暖說。  
"I'd guess they're telling us to brace ourselves mentally — so we don't keel over from excitement," Nuannuan said.  
我想想也有道理。  
That made a kind of sense.  
當初會參加這次夏令營活動，有一大半是衝著長城的面子。  
A big part of why I'd signed up for this camp in the first place was, frankly, because of the Great Wall.  

要爬的是八達嶺長城，距離北京只約七十公里，有高速公路可以直達。  
We were climbing the Badaling section, only about seventy kilometers from Beijing and reachable by expressway.  
萬一古代的騎兵越過八達嶺長城，要不了多久不就可以兵臨北京城下？  
If ancient cavalry had ever broken through Badaling, wouldn't they have been at the gates of Beijing in no time?  
正在為北京捏把冷汗時，忽然車內一陣騷動。  
Just as I was breaking out in cold sweat for Beijing's sake, a sudden stir went through the bus.  
我轉頭望向窗外，被眼前的景物震懾住了。  
I turned to the window and was awestruck by what I saw.  
『這……』我有點結巴。  
"This—" I stammered.  
「這是居庸關。」暖暖說。  
"This is Juyong Pass," Nuannuan said.  

居庸關兩側高山如刀劍般聳立，中為峽谷，居庸關關城即位於峽谷正中。  
The peaks on either side of Juyong Pass tower up like blades, with a gorge between, and the pass town sits squarely in the middle of the gorge.  
地勢險峻，扼北京咽喉，難怪《呂氏春秋》提到：天下九塞，居庸其一。  
The terrain is sheer and treacherous, gripping Beijing by the throat — no wonder *Master Lü's Spring and Autumn Annals* names it as one of the nine great passes under heaven.  
居庸關不僅雄偉，而且風景宜人，兩側山巒疊翠，湛綠溪水中流。  
Juyong Pass isn't just imposing; it's also beautiful — green mountains layered on either side, a clear emerald stream running through.  
很難想像軍事要塞兼具壯觀與秀麗。  
It was hard to imagine a military stronghold that was at once magnificent and lovely.  

『看來北京可以喘口氣了。』我說。  
"Looks like Beijing can breathe easy after all," I said.  
「你說啥？」暖暖問。  
"What do you mean?"  
『越過八達嶺長城的騎兵看到居庸關，一定會下馬欣賞這美景。』我說，  
"Cavalry that broke through Badaling, the moment they saw Juyong Pass, would have to dismount and take in the view," I said.  
『感慨美景之際，也許突然頓悟，覺得人生苦短，打打殺殺太無聊，於是  
"Moved by all that beauty, they might suddenly realize that life is short, that fighting and killing is dull,  
撥轉馬頭又回去也說不定。』  
and turn their horses around for home."  
暖暖睜大眼睛看著我，沒有說話。  
Nuannuan stared at me, eyes wide, and said nothing.  
『別擔心。』我對著暖暖笑了笑，『北京安全了。』  
"Don't worry." I smiled at her. "Beijing is safe."  
「早叫你做好心理準備了。」暖暖瞪我一眼，「現在卻一個勁兒瞎說。」  
"I told you to brace yourself," she said, glaring. "Now look at you spouting nonsense."  

過了居庸關，沒多久便到八達嶺長城。看了看錶，還不到11點半。  
Not long after Juyong Pass we reached Badaling. A glance at my watch — not even half past eleven.  
老師們說先簡單吃碗炸醬麵填填肚子，吃飽了好上路。  
The teachers said we'd grab a simple bowl of *zhajiangmian* — fried sauce noodles — to fill up before hitting the road.  
（吃飽了好上路這句話聽起來很怪，要被砍頭的犯人最後都會聽到這句）  
("*Eat your fill before you hit the road*" sounds odd, since it's usually the last thing they say to a man being led off to be beheaded.)  
吃炸醬麵時高亮打開話匣子，他說小時候母親常常煮一大鍋炸醬，  
Over our noodles, Gao Liang got talking. As a kid, he said, his mother would cook up a big pot of zhajiang sauce,  
只要舀幾勺炸醬到麵條裡，攪拌一下，唏哩呼嚕就一碗，一餐就解決了。  
and you only had to scoop a few spoonfuls onto your noodles, give it a stir, slurp it down, and your meal was done.  
「平時就這麼吃。」他說。  
"That's how we ate every day," he said.  
我突然想到從下飛機到現在，一粒白米也沒看到，更別說白米飯了。  
It hit me that since landing in Beijing I hadn't seen a single grain of rice, let alone a bowl of cooked rice.  
地理課本上說：南人食米、北人食麥，古人誠不我欺也。  
*Southerners eat rice; northerners eat wheat*, my old geography textbook said. The ancients had not deceived me.  

搭上通往南四樓的南索道，纜車啟動瞬間，暖暖笑了。  
We boarded the south cable car heading up to the South Fourth Tower; the moment it lurched into motion, Nuannuan laughed.  
她轉過身，跪在椅子上，朝窗外望去，猛揮揮手，口中還唸唸有詞。  
She turned, knelt on her seat, looked out the window, waved energetically, and muttered something to herself.  
『坐好。』我說。  
"Sit down properly," I said.  
「初次見面，總得跟長城打聲招呼，說聲您辛苦了。」暖暖說。  
"First time meeting, you have to say hello to the Great Wall — *thank you for all your hard work*."  
『妳……』  
"You—"  
「長城我也是第一次爬。」  
"This is my first time climbing the Wall too."  
『早叫妳做好心理準備了。』我說，「現在卻一個勁兒瞎說。」  
"Should've braced yourself in advance," I said. "Now look at you spouting nonsense."  
「你才瞎說呢。」暖暖又轉身坐好。  
"*You're* the one spouting nonsense." She turned back and sat properly.  

下了纜車，老師們簡短交代要量力而為、不要逞強、記得在烽火台碰頭。  
Off the cable car, the teachers briefly told us to know our limits, not push too hard, and meet at the beacon tower.  
我向遠處看，長城蜿蜒於山脊之上，像一條待飛的巨龍，隨時準備破空。  
I looked into the distance: the Wall snaked along the ridges like a great dragon poised to take flight, ready to split the sky.  
往左右一看，兩側城牆高度不一、形狀也不同。  
Looking left and right, the parapets on either side were different heights and shapes.  
高亮說呈鋸齒狀凹凸的叫堞牆，高約一米七，剛好遮住守城者，這是抵禦  
Gao Liang explained: the jagged, crenellated one is the *die* wall, about 1.7 meters high — just tall enough to shelter a defender.  
外敵用的，堞牆有巡邏時瞭望的垛口，垛口下有可供射箭的方形小孔；  
That's for keeping enemies out. The notches between are lookouts for patrols, and the small square holes below are for shooting arrows.  
矮的一側只約一米高，叫宇牆，就像一般的矮牆。  
The shorter wall, about a meter high, is called the *yu* wall — basically a low parapet.  

「宇牆做啥用的？」暖暖問。  
"What's the *yu* wall for?" Nuannuan asked.  
『巡邏累了，可以坐著歇會。』我說。  
"For patrols to sit on when they're tired," I said.  
「別瞎說。」暖暖說。  
"Stop making things up."  
「人馬在城上行走，萬一摔下城了可糟，這宇牆是保護用的。」高亮說，  
"Men and horses moved along the top; if anyone slipped off, that'd be bad. The *yu* wall is for protection," Gao Liang said.  
「而且宇牆每隔一段距離便有道券門，門裡有石階讓士兵登城下城。」  
"And every so often there's an arched doorway in it, with stone steps for soldiers to climb up or down."  
我用尊敬的眼神看著高亮，「來北京後，我沒事就來爬長城。」他說。  
I gave Gao Liang a look of respect. "Since I came to Beijing, I climb the Wall whenever I can," he said.  

我們一路往北爬，坡度陡的地段還有鐵欄杆供人扶著上下坡。  
We climbed northward; on the steepest sections there were iron handrails to grip while going up and down.  
順著垛口向外看，盡是重疊的山、乾枯的樹、雜亂的草，構成一片荒涼。  
Through the embrasures, all you saw were ranges of hills, withered trees, and scraggly grass — a vast desolation.  
每隔幾百公尺就有方形城台，兩層的叫敵樓，上層用來瞭望或攻擊，  
Every few hundred meters there was a square platform; the two-story ones were *dilou* — enemy towers — with the upper level for lookouts and attack,  
下層讓士兵休息或存放武器；一層的叫城台，四周有垛口供巡邏與攻擊。  
the lower for soldiers to rest or store weapons. The single-story ones were *chengtai*, ringed with crenellations for patrols and attack.  
高亮說現在叫的南四、南三、北三、北四樓等，都是敵樓。  
Gao Liang said the structures we kept naming — South Fourth, South Third, North Third, North Fourth — were all enemy towers.  
「我們要爬到八達嶺長城海拔最高的北八樓。」他說。  
"We're climbing to the North Eighth Tower — the highest point of Badaling," he said.  

暖暖畢竟是女孩子，體力較差，偶爾停下腳步扶著欄杆喘口氣。  
Nuannuan, being a girl, had less stamina, and now and then she'd stop, lean on a railing, and catch her breath.  
有時風吹得她搖搖晃晃，高亮說這裡是風口，風特大。  
Sometimes the wind made her sway. Gao Liang said this was a wind gap, where gusts ran especially strong.  
「如果是秋冬之際，風特強、天特冷。那時爬長城特有感受。」他說。  
"In autumn or winter the wind is fierce and the cold cuts deep. Climbing then has a feeling all its own," he said.  
我們現在一身輕裝，頂多帶瓶水，還得靠欄杆幫我們上上下下；  
We were dressed light, with maybe a bottle of water, and even so we needed the rails to get up and down;  
而古代守城將士卻是一身盔甲、手持兵器，頂著狂風在這跑上跑下。  
Yet the ancient garrison soldiers had done it in full armor, weapons in hand, against howling wind.  
每天望向關外的荒涼，除同袍外看不見半個人，該是何等孤獨與寂寞。  
Day after day they had stared out at the wasteland and seen no one but their comrades — what loneliness that must have been.  
想看到人又怕看到人，因為一旦看到人影，可能意味著戰事的開端，  
Longing to see another person, dreading to see another person, because the appearance of a figure could mean the start of war —  
這又是怎樣的矛盾心情？  
what a contradiction of feeling that must have been.  

『如果……』  
"If—"  
「如果世上的男女都能以純真的心對待彼此，」暖暖打斷我，接著說：  
"*If every man and woman in the world treated each other with sincere hearts*," Nuannuan cut in, finishing for me,  
「到那時長城就可以含笑而塌了。你是不是想這樣說？」  
"*then the Great Wall could smile and collapse*. Were you about to say that?"  
『嘿。』我笑了笑，『妳休息夠了？』  
"Heh." I smiled. "Rested enough?"  
「嗯。」暖暖點點頭。  
"Mm." Nuannuan nodded.  

高亮體力好，總是拿著一台像砲似的照相機東拍西拍，不曾歇腿。  
Gao Liang, with his stamina, was always wielding a cannon-sized camera, snapping shots in every direction without stopping.  
我和暖暖每到一座敵樓便坐下來歇息喝口水，四處張望。  
At every tower Nuannuan and I sat down to rest, sip water, and look around.  
城牆上常看見遊客題上「到此一遊」，台灣的風景名勝也常見到此一遊。  
Visitors had scrawled "I was here" all over the parapets — the same line you find at scenic spots in Taiwan.  
看來《西遊記》裡的孫悟空真是害人不淺。  
Sun Wukong from *Journey to the West* really had a lot to answer for.  
記得大學時去過的民雄鬼屋，那裡竟然也到處被寫上到此一遊。  
I remember the haunted house at Minxiong I'd been to in college — even there, "I was here" had been scribbled everywhere.  
有的同學比較狠，簽下到此一遊後，還順便寫上老師的地址和聯絡電話。  
Some classmates went further: after signing "I was here," they'd add a teacher's home address and phone number.  
「看你還敢不敢隨便當人。」寫完後，他說。  
"Try failing students at random again," they'd say after writing.  

我起身看看牆上還題些什麼字。  
I stood up to see what else had been written on the wall.  
「我到長城是好漢！」  
*Climbing the Great Wall makes me a hero!*  
這個俗，搞不好有八千塊磚上這樣寫。  
That one's so common there must be eight thousand bricks like it.  
「我要學長城堅強屹立千年！」  
*I will be strong like the Great Wall and stand a thousand years!*  
堅強是好事，但要有公德心。沒公德心而屹立千年，就叫禍害遺千年。  
Being strong is fine, but you need civic spirit. Standing a thousand years without it just means a thousand years of nuisance.  
「小紅！我對妳綿延的愛就像長城！」  
*Xiao Hong! My love for you is unbroken like the Great Wall!*  
被愛沖昏頭所做的糊塗事，可以理解。小紅幫個忙，甩了他吧。  
Stupid things done in love are forgivable. Xiao Hong, do us a favor — dump him.  
「我的XX比長城長！」  
*My XX is longer than the Great Wall!*  
『馬的！』我不禁脫口而出。  
"Damn it!" I blurted out before I could help it.  

『咳咳……』瞥見暖暖正瞧著我，臉上一紅，『我失態了。』  
"Ahem—" I caught Nuannuan watching me and reddened. "Forgive my outburst."  
「沒事。」暖暖說，「你罵得好。」  
"No, no," she said. "Well cursed."  
『我還可以罵得更難聽喔。』  
"I can curse much worse."  
「罵來聽聽。」  
"Then let's hear it."  
我張開嘴巴，始終吐不出話，最後說：『我們還是繼續上路吧。』  
I opened my mouth and nothing came out. In the end I said, "Let's get moving again."  

再往上爬了一會，終於來到烽火台，這裡地勢既高且險、視野又開闊，  
After more climbing, we finally reached a beacon tower — high, exposed, and with wide views,  
如此才能達到燃放煙火示警的目的。  
exactly what was needed for sending warning fires.  
大約有二十多個學生已經坐著聊天，徐馳看見我便說：  
About twenty students were already there chatting. Xu Chi spotted me and said,  
「老蔡，您的腿還是自個兒的嗎？」  
"Old Cai, are those still your own legs?"  
經他一說，我才發覺腿有些軟。  
Now that he mentioned it, my legs were a little wobbly.  

四個老師到了三個，北京李老師特地壓後，他到了表示全都到了。  
Three of the four teachers had arrived; Teacher Li from Beijing always brought up the rear, so when he showed up, the group was complete.  
過了十幾分鐘，李老師終於到了。  
After ten-odd more minutes, Teacher Li finally arrived.  
他喘口氣，點齊了人數，清了清喉嚨後，開口說：  
He caught his breath, counted heads, cleared his throat, and began:  

大家都聽過「不到長城非好漢」，但一定得爬長城來證明自己是好漢嗎？  
You've all heard the saying *one who hasn't climbed the Great Wall is no true hero*. But do you really need to climb the Wall to prove you're a hero?  
你試試挑座險要的山，從山腳登上頂，誰敢說你不是好漢？或者你繞著北京走上一圈，  
Take any steep mountain, walk it from foot to peak — who'd dare say you weren't a hero? Or walk all the way around Beijing,  
中途不歇息不叫救護車不哭爹喊娘，這不是好漢嗎？爬長城的目的不只在證明自己是好漢，  
without resting, without calling an ambulance, without crying for your parents — wouldn't that make you a hero? The point of climbing the Wall isn't just to prove you're a hero.  
看看腳下，你正踏著歷史的動脈。有了長城，秦國才能騰出手來滅六國、統一中原；  
Look down: you're treading on the artery of history. With this Wall, Qin could free its hands to crush the six kingdoms and unify the Central Plain;  
若沒長城，歷史完全變了樣。你常在書上讀到詠嘆長城和邊塞將士的詩詞，那是文學的美；  
without it, history would have gone differently. The poems you've read about the Wall and the frontier soldiers are the beauty of literature;  
你今天爬上一遭，對文學的美更有深刻感受，同時你也能感受歷史的真。  
climb it once today and you'll feel that beauty more deeply, and at the same time touch the truth of history.  
歷史就是人類走過千年所留下的腳印，你現在的腳印將來也會成歷史啊。  
History is the trail of footprints humanity has left over a thousand years; the prints you leave today will be history too.  
看看四周，地勢越險要，越彰顯長城的雄偉，長城若建在平原上，那不就一道牆唄。  
Look around: the more treacherous the terrain, the more the Wall's grandeur shows. Built on a plain, it would be just another wall.  
人生也一樣，越是困頓波折，越能彰顯你的價值，越能激勵你向上，瞭解這層道理，你才是真好漢。  
Life works the same way: the harder the road, the more your worth shows, the more it pushes you upward. Understand that, and *then* you're a real hero.  

他說完後大夥拍拍手，李老師確實說得好。但是，太感性了吧？  
When he finished, everyone clapped. Teacher Li had spoken well — but a bit *too* heartfelt, no?  
北京張老師站起身，也清了清喉嚨說：  
Teacher Zhang from Beijing stood up, cleared his throat, and added,  
「我們待會一起在烽火台下合個影。合影的同時，希望同學們在心裡默默  
"We'll all take a group photo at the foot of the beacon tower in a moment. While we're taking it, I hope each of you will silently pray in your heart  
祈禱：但願烽火台永遠不再燃起狼煙。」  
that the beacon tower never burns warning fires again."  
現在是怎樣？感性還會傳染喔。  
What was happening here? Sentimentality is contagious, apparently.

"I saw a movie once where the lead couple was sheltering under a coconut tree when a coconut fell.  
男的說：是椰子耶！女的回說：從椰子樹上掉下來的當然是椰子，難道  
The guy said, *It's a coconut!* The girl said, *Of course it's a coconut — what fell from a coconut tree, do you think — would it  
還會是芭樂嗎？』我笑了笑，『妳的幽默感跟女主角好像同一門派。』  
be a guava?*" I laughed. "Your humor's of the same school as that heroine's."  

「你愛看電影？」暖暖問。  
"You like movies?" Nuannuan asked.  
『嗯。』我點點頭，『什麼類型都看，但文藝片很少看。』  
"Mm." I nodded. "I'll watch anything, but rarely literary films."  
「咋說？」  
"Why?"  
『有次看到一部文藝片，裡面武松很深情的對著潘金蓮說：妳在我心中，  
"Once I watched a literary film where Wu Song says to Pan Jinlian, full of feeling, *In my heart  
永遠是青草地的小黃花。』我吃吃亂笑，『那瞬間，我崩潰了。』  
you will forever be a little yellow flower on a green meadow.*" I cracked up. "I lost it on the spot."  
「幹啥這樣笑？」  
"Why are you laughing like that?"  
『我那時就這樣笑，結果周遭投射來的目光好冰。從此不太敢看文藝片，  
"I laughed like this in the theater, and the looks I got around me were freezing. I've been wary of literary films ever since,  
怕又聽到這種經典對白。』  
afraid of running into another classic line like that."  
說完後，我又劈里啪啦一陣亂笑，不能自已。  
And I burst out laughing again, helplessly.  
「笑完了？」暖暖說，「嘴不酸嗎？」  
"Done?" Nuannuan said. "Doesn't your jaw hurt?"  
『唉。』我收起笑聲，說：『真是餘悸猶存。』  
"Sigh." I reined in the laughter. "The aftershocks are still with me."  

我突然發覺跟暖暖在一起時，我變得健談了。  
I suddenly noticed that around Nuannuan, I became talkative.  
這有兩種可能，一是她會讓我不由自主想說很多話；  
Two possibilities: one, she made me want to say a lot without thinking;  
二是我容易感受到她的聆聽，於是越講越多。  
two, I could easily feel her listening, so I kept going.  
以現在而言，她看來相當疲憊，卻打起精神聽我說些無聊的話。  
Right now, she looked exhausted, yet she was rallying to listen to my drivel.  
「真累了。」她低頭看著餐盤，「吃不完，咋辦？」  
"I'm really tired," she said, looking down at her tray. "Can't finish. What now?"  
『吃不完，』我說，『兜著走。』  
"Can't finish — *take it with you*," I said.  
「這句話不是這樣用的。」  
"That's not how that idiom is used."  
『在台灣就這麼用。』我嘿嘿笑了兩聲。  
"That's how we use it in Taiwan." I gave a couple of smug laughs.  

我和暖暖走出食堂，走了幾步，我突然停下腳步。  
We left the dining hall and walked a few steps; I suddenly stopped.  
『啊？差點忘了。』我說。  
"Oh — almost forgot," I said.  
「忘了啥？」  
"Forgot what?"  
『我才是北七。』我指著鼻子，『在長城跟妳開個玩笑，別介意。』  
"*I'm* the *bei qi*." I pointed at my nose. "I was just teasing you up on the Wall. Don't take it personally."  
暖暖想了一下，終於笑出聲，說：「以後別用我聽不懂的台灣話罵人。」  
Nuannuan thought for a moment, then laughed. "Don't use Taiwanese I can't understand to insult me."  
『是。』我說，『要罵妳一定用普通話罵，這樣妳才聽得懂。』  
"Right," I said. "If I'm going to insult you, I'll use Mandarin so you understand."  
「喂。」  
"Hey!"  
『開玩笑的。』  
"Joking."  

經過教室，發現大多數的同學都在裡面，教室充滿笑聲。  
Passing the classroom, we saw most of the students already inside, the room full of laughter.  
有的聊天；有的展示今天在長城買的紀念品；有的在看數位相機的圖檔。  
Some were chatting, some showing off souvenirs they'd bought on the Wall, some flicking through digital photos.  
我和暖暖也加入他們，徐馳朝我說：「老蔡，我偷拍了你一張。」  
We joined them. Xu Chi said to me, "Old Cai, I caught a candid of you."  
湊近一看，原來是我在烽火台上不支倒地的相片。  
I leaned in — a picture of me collapsing at the beacon tower.  
「你這次咋沒比V？」暖暖說。  
"How come you didn't flash a V this time?" Nuannuan said.  
『妳真是見樹不見林。』我說，『我的雙腳大開，不就構成了V字？』  
"You can't see the forest for the trees," I said. "My legs are spread wide — isn't that a V?"  
我很得意哈哈大笑，笑聲未歇，眼角瞥見學弟和王克坐在教室角落。  
I laughed proudly, and as my laugh trailed off, I caught my junior and Wang Ke sitting in a corner.  
我很好奇便走過去。  
Curious, I went over.  

王克正低頭畫畫，學弟坐她對面，也低頭看她畫畫。  
Wang Ke was bent over a drawing; my junior sat across from her, head bent watching.  
我在兩人之間插進頭，三個人的頭剛好形成正三角形。  
I poked my head in between them, the three heads forming an equilateral triangle.  
那是張素描，蜿蜒於山脊的長城像條龍，遊長城的人潮點綴成龍的鱗片。  
It was a sketch — the Wall winding along the ridges like a dragon, the crowds of visitors dotted in as its scales.  
『畫得很棒啊。』我發出感嘆。  
"That's beautiful," I said admiringly.  
王克抬起頭，靦腆地朝我笑了笑。  
Wang Ke looked up and gave me a shy smile.  
「學長。」學弟也抬起頭，神秘兮兮地說：「很亮。」  
"Senior," my junior looked up too and said, mysteriously, "Very *bright*."  
『OK。』我朝他點點頭，『我了解。』  
"OK." I nodded at him. "Got it."  

轉身欲離去時，發現王克的眼神有些困惑。  
As I turned to leave I noticed Wang Ke looking puzzled.  
『學弟的意思是說我是你們的電燈泡啦。』我對著王克說，  
"What he means is that I'm being a third wheel — a *light bulb* — between you two," I told her,  
『所謂的電燈泡就是……』  
"a *light bulb* meaning—"  
「學長！」學弟有些氣急敗壞。  
"*Senior!*" my junior burst out.  
王克聽懂了，臉上有些尷尬，又低頭作畫。  
Wang Ke understood, looked embarrassed, and bent her head back over the page.  
我帶著滿足的笑容離開。  
I walked away with a satisfied smile.  

「你這人賊壞。」暖暖說。  
"You're really *zei* mean," Nuannuan said.  
『賊壞？』我說，『什麼意思？』  
"*Zei* mean? What does that mean?"  
「賊在東北話裡面，是很、非常的意思。」  
"In Northeast dialect, *zei* means *very*, *extremely*."  
『喔。』我恍然大悟，『暖暖，妳這人賊靚。這樣說行嗎？』  
"Oh." It clicked. "Nuannuan, you're really *zei* pretty. Does that work?"  
「說法沒問題，」暖暖笑出聲，「但形容我並不貼切。」  
"The grammar's fine," she laughed, "but the description doesn't quite fit."  
『既然不貼切，幹嘛笑那麼開心？』  
"If it doesn't fit, why are you laughing so happily?"  
「涼涼！」暖暖叫了一聲。  
"Liangliang!" she yelled.  
我趕緊溜到徐馳旁邊假裝忙碌。  
I scurried over to Xu Chi and pretended to be busy.  

大夥在教室裡聊到很晚，直到老師們進來趕人。  
We talked in the classroom late into the night, until the teachers came in to chase us out.  
回到寢室，一跳上床，眼皮就重了。  
Back in the dorm, the moment I jumped onto my bunk, my eyelids felt heavy.  
「老蔡，下次你來北京，我帶你去爬司馬台長城。」高亮說。  
"Old Cai, next time you come to Beijing, I'll take you to the Simatai section of the Wall," Gao Liang said.  
高亮說那是野長城，遊客很少，而且多數是老外。  
He said it was *wild* Wall — few visitors, and most of them foreigners.  
他又說司馬台長城更為雄奇險峻，是探險家的天堂等等。  
He went on about how Simatai was even more dramatic and treacherous, a paradise for adventurers, and so on.  
我記不清了，因為他講到一半我就睡著了，睡著的人是不長記性的。  
I don't remember the rest, because halfway through I was asleep — and a sleeping person doesn't grow much memory.
