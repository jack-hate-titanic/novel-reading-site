# 《暖暖》第五章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

一早醒來，走到盥洗室時還迷迷糊糊糊。  
Waking up early, I staggered to the bathroom still half asleep.  
碰見學弟，他說：「學長，哈你個卵。」  
I ran into my junior. "Senior, *ha ni ge luan*," he said.  
我瞬間清醒，掐住他脖子，說：『一大早就討打。』  
I was instantly awake and grabbed him by the throat. "Looking for a beating first thing in the morning?"  
「是徐馳教我的。」學弟在斷氣前說。  
"Xu Chi taught me," my junior gasped before I choked him senseless.  

徐馳說這是他們家鄉話，問候打招呼用的。  
Xu Chi said it was a greeting from his hometown, just a way of saying hello.  
也不知道真的假的，但看徐馳的模樣又不像開玩笑。  
I couldn't tell if he was serious, but Xu Chi didn't look like he was joking.  
如果對女生講這句會被告性騷擾；碰上男生講這句，大概會被痛毆一頓。  
Say it to a girl and you'd be accused of harassment; say it to a guy and you'd probably get beaten to a pulp.  
但總比那男生真脫下褲子請你打招呼要好。  
But it's still better than the guy actually dropping his pants to greet you.  

在食堂門口，李老師跟張老師商量一會後，說：  
Outside the canteen, Teacher Li and Teacher Zhang talked for a moment, then Li announced:  
「咱們今天到外面喝豆汁去，感受一下老北京的飲食文化。」  
"We're going out today to drink *douzhi* and experience old Beijing's food culture."  
我問暖暖：『豆汁就是豆漿嗎？』  
I asked Nuannuan: "Is *douzhi* like soy milk?"  
「當然不是。」暖暖說，「豆漿是黃豆做的，豆汁則是綠豆。豆汁就只有  
"Of course not. Soy milk is made from soybeans; *douzhi* is made from mung beans. *Douzhi* is only  
北京有，別的地方是喝不到的。」  
found in Beijing — you can't get it anywhere else."  
『好喝嗎？』我又問。  
"Is it good?" I asked.  
「準保讓你印象深刻。」暖暖的表情透著古怪。  
"It'll definitely leave an impression," she said, with a strange look.  

我覺得奇怪，問了徐馳：『豆汁好喝嗎？』  
I thought that was odd, so I asked Xu Chi: "Is *douzhi* good?"  
「會讓你畢生難忘。」徐馳臉上的神情也很古怪。  
"It'll be unforgettable for the rest of your life." Xu Chi wore the same odd expression.  
我想高亮是個老實人，講話會比較直，便又問高亮：『豆汁好喝嗎？』  
I figured Gao Liang was straightforward and would give a straight answer. "Is *douzhi* good?"  
「嗯……」高亮沉吟一會，說：「我第一次喝了後，三月不知肉味。」  
"Mm…" Gao Liang mused for a moment. "The first time I had it, I couldn't taste meat for three months."  
印象深刻、畢生難忘、三月不知肉味，怎麼都是這種形容詞。  
"Leaves an impression," "unforgettable," "couldn't taste meat" — why all these indirect answers?  
回答好不好喝那麼難嗎？  
Was it that hard to say if it was good or not?  

如果你問：那女孩長得如何？  
If you ask: what does that girl look like?  
人家回答：很漂亮，保證讓你畢生難忘。  
And they answer: she's beautiful, you'll never forget her.  
你當然會很清楚知道，你將碰到一個絕世美女。  
You'd know for sure you're about to meet a stunning beauty.  
但如果人家只回答：保證讓你印象深刻、畢生難忘、三月不知肉味。  
But if they only say: "It'll leave an impression," "unforgettable," "three months without tasting meat" —  
你怎麼曉得那女孩漂不漂亮？碰到恐龍也是會印象深刻到畢生難忘，  
how would you know if she's beautiful? You'd be just as "impressed" and "unforgettable" if you met a dinosaur,  
於是三個月吃不下飯啊。  
and you'd lose your appetite for three months anyway.  

一走進豆汁店裡，馬上聞到一股酸溜溜的嗆鼻味道，讓人不太舒服。  
The moment I stepped into the *douzhi* shop, a sour, pungent smell hit my nose — not pleasant at all.  
濃稠的豆汁端上來了，顏色灰裡透綠；另外還有一盤鹹菜絲、一盤焦圈。  
The thick *douzhi* arrived, grayish-green in color, along with a plate of shredded pickles and a plate of *jiaoquan* — fried dough rings.  
細長的鹹菜絲灑上芝麻、辣椒油，焦圈則炸得金黃酥透。  
The fine pickle shreds were sprinkled with sesame and chili oil; the dough rings were fried golden and crisp.  
「這得趁熱喝。」暖暖告訴我，眼神似笑非笑。  
"Drink it while it's hot," Nuannuan told me, with a half-smile.  
我戰戰兢兢端起碗，嘴唇小心翼翼貼住碗邊，緩緩地啜了一小口。  
Nervously I lifted the bowl, pressed my lips carefully to the rim, and took a tiny sip.  
『哇！』  
"Whoa!"  
我慘叫一聲，豆汁不僅酸而且還帶著餿腐的怪味，令人作嘔。  
I yelped. The *douzhi* wasn't just sour — it had a rotten, rancid taste that made me want to gag.  

我擠眉弄眼、掐鼻抓耳、齜牙咧嘴，五官全用上了，還是甩不掉那怪味。  
I scrunched my face, pinched my nose, pulled my ears, bared my teeth — used every feature I had — but couldn't shake the taste.  
暖暖笑了，邊笑邊說：「快吃點鹹菜絲壓壓口。」  
Nuannuan laughed. "Quick — eat some pickles to mask it."  
我趕緊挾了一筷子鹹菜絲送入口中，胡亂嚼了幾口，果然有效。  
I grabbed a chopstick-full of pickles and chewed frantically — it worked.  
『豆汁的味道好怪。』我說。  
"The taste is so strange," I said.  
「那是幻覺。」暖暖說，「再試試？」  
"That's just in your head. Try again?"  
我又端起碗，深呼吸一次，重新武裝了心理，憋了氣再喝一口。  
I picked up the bowl again, took a deep breath, steeled myself, held my breath, and took another sip.  
這哪是幻覺？這是真實的怪味啊。豆汁滑進喉嚨時，我還差點噎著。  
That wasn't in my head — it was real. As it slid down my throat I nearly choked.  
氣順了後，放下碗，眼神空洞，望著暖暖。  
Once I'd caught my breath, I put down the bowl and stared blankly at Nuannuan.  

「要喝這豆汁兒，需佐以鹹菜絲和焦圈，三樣不能少一樣。」暖暖說，  
"To drink *douzhi*, you need pickles and *jiaoquan* — all three together, can't skip one," she said.  
「豆汁的酸、鹹菜絲的鹹與辣、焦圈的脆，在酸、鹹、辣、脆的夾擊中，  
"The sour of the *douzhi*, the salt and spice of the pickles, the crunch of the *jiaoquan* — amid the assault of sour, salt, spice, and crunch,  
口齒之間會緩緩透出一股綿延的香。」  
a lingering fragrance slowly emerges in your mouth."  
暖暖一口豆汁、一口鹹菜絲、一口焦圈，吃得津津有味，眉開眼笑。  
She alternated sips of *douzhi*, bites of pickle, and bites of *jiaoquan*, eating with relish, beaming.  
我越看越奇，簡直是不可思議。  
The more I watched, the more amazed I was — it was incredible.  
「意猶未盡呀。」暖暖說。  
"I still want more," she said.  
『請受小弟一拜。』我說。  
"Please accept my humble bow," I said.  

隔壁桌的學弟突然跑過來，蹲下身拉住我衣角，說：  
My junior from the next table suddenly ran over, crouched down, and tugged at my shirt:  
「學長，我不行了，快送我到醫院。」  
"Senior, I'm done — get me to a hospital."  
『你怎麼了？』  
"What happened?"  
「我把整碗豆汁都喝光了。」學弟說完便閉上雙眼。  
"I drank the whole bowl." He closed his eyes.  
『振作點！』我啪啪打了他兩耳光。  
"Buck up!" I slapped him twice.  
學弟睜開雙眼，站起身撫著臉頰，又回到他座位上。  
He opened his eyes, stood up rubbing his cheeks, and went back to his seat.  

「剛剛的耳光，你好像真打？」暖暖說。  
"That slap sounded real," Nuannuan said.  
『是啊。』我忍不住吃吃笑了起來，『我學弟愛玩，我也樂得配合演出。  
"Yeah." I couldn't help chuckling. "My junior loves to clown around, and I'm happy to play along.  
對了，剛說到哪？』  
Anyway, where were we?"  
「你說你想拜我。」  
"You said you wanted to bow to me."  
我立刻起身離開座位，單膝跪地、雙手抱拳，曰：『姑娘真神人也。』  
I immediately stood, left my seat, knelt on one knee, and clasped my hands: "Miss, you are truly a divine being."  
暖暖笑著拉我起身，說：「其實我第一次喝豆汁時，也忍受不了這怪味。  
She laughed and pulled me up. "Actually, the first time I drank *douzhi*, I couldn't stand the taste either.  
後來連續喝了大半個月，習慣後才喝出門道，甚至上了癮。」  
After half a month of drinking it every day, I got used to it, learned to appreciate it, even got addicted."  

『真是風情的哥哥啊。』我說。  
"Truly a brother of *fengqing*," I said.  
「啥？」暖暖問。  
"What?"  
『不解。』  
"*Bujie* — I don't get it."  
「呀？」  
"Huh?"  
『因為有句話叫不解風情，所以風情的哥哥，就叫不解。』  
"You know the phrase *bujie fengqing* — doesn't understand romance. So the brother of *fengqing* is *bujie*."  
「你喝豆汁喝傻了？」暖暖說，「我完全聽不懂你說的。」  
"Did the *douzhi* fry your brain?" she said. "I can't understand a word you're saying."  

『我的意思是，我很不解。』我說，『想請教您一件事。』  
"What I mean is, I don't get it," I said. "Let me ask you something."  
「說唄。」  
"Go on."  
『妳第一次喝豆汁時，反應跟我差不多？』  
"The first time you drank *douzhi*, you had about the same reaction as me?"  
「嗯。」暖暖點點頭，「可以這麼說。」  
"Mm." She nodded. "You could say that."  
『後來妳連續喝了半個多月才習慣，而且還上了癮？』  
"Then you drank it for half a month before you got used to it, and you got addicted?"  
「是呀。」暖暖笑了笑，「那時只要打聽到豆汁老店，再遠我都去。」  
"Yes. Back then, wherever I heard of an old *douzhi* shop, no matter how far, I'd go."  
『既然妳第一次喝豆汁時就覺得根本不能接受，』我歪著頭想了半天，  
"Since the first time you had it you found it completely unacceptable," I tilted my head and thought for a moment,  
『又怎麼會再連續喝半個多月呢？』  
"why would you keep drinking it for half a month?"  
暖暖睜大眼睛，沒有答話，陷入一種沉思狀態。  
She stared at me, speechless, lost in thought.  

「這還真是百思的弟弟。」過了許久，暖暖才開口。  
"This really is the younger brother of *baisi* — thinking a hundred times," she said after a long pause.  
『嗯？』我說。  
"Huh?"  
「也叫不解。」暖暖笑說，「因為百思不解。」  
"Also called *bujie* — can't understand. Because *baisi bujie* — a hundred thoughts can't solve it."  
『妳怎麼也這樣說話？』  
"Why are *you* talking like this now?"  
「這下你總該知道聽你說話的人有多痛苦了。」  
"Now you know how much pain you put your listeners through."  
『辛苦妳了。』我說。  
"You've worked hard," I said.  
「哪兒的話。」暖暖笑了笑。  
"Not at all," she said with a smile.  

「喝豆汁的文化，據說已有千年。所以味道再怪，我也要堅持下去。」  
"The culture of *douzhi* goes back a thousand years, they say. So no matter how strange the taste, I have to keep it up."  
暖暖似乎找到喝豆汁的理由，「總之，就是一股傻勁。」  
Nuannuan seemed to have found her reason. "Anyway — sheer stubbornness."  
『妳實在太強了。』我嘖嘖讚嘆著。  
"You're really something," I said in admiration.  
「涼涼。」暖暖指著我面前的碗，「還試嗎？」  
"Liangliang." She pointed at the bowl in front of me. "Want to try again?"  
我伸出手端起碗，卻始終沒勇氣送到嘴邊，嘆口氣，又放下碗。  
I reached out and picked up the bowl, but couldn't bring myself to lift it to my lips. I sighed and put it down.  
暖暖笑了笑，端起我的碗。我急忙說：『我喝過了。』  
Nuannuan smiled and picked up my bowl. "I've already drunk from it," I said hurriedly.  
「沒事。」暖暖說，「做豆汁很辛苦的，別浪費。」  
"It's fine. Making *douzhi* is hard work — shouldn't waste it."  

徐馳走過來，看到我面前的空碗，驚訝地說：「老蔡，你喝光了？」  
Xu Chi came over and saw the empty bowl in front of me. "Old Cai, you finished it?"  
『嘿嘿。』我說。  
"Heh heh," I said.  
「沒事吧？」徐馳看看我的眼，摸摸我的手，搖搖我身子。  
"You okay?" He peered into my eyes, felt my hand, shook me.  
『嘿嘿嘿。』我又說。  
"Heh heh heh," I said again.  
「真想不到。」徐馳說，「來！咱哥兒們再喝一碗！」  
"Never would've guessed. Come on, let's get another bowl!"  
『馳哥！』我急忙拉住他，『是暖暖幫我喝光的。』  
"Brother Chi!" I grabbed him. "Nuannuan finished it for me."  
徐馳哈哈大笑，暖暖也笑了，我笑得很尷尬。  
Xu Chi burst out laughing, Nuannuan laughed too, and I gave an awkward smile.  

我觀察一下所有學生的反應，台灣學生全都是驚魂未定的神情；  
I looked around at the students. The Taiwanese all looked shell-shocked;  
北京學生的反應則很多元，有像暖暖、徐馳那樣超愛喝豆汁的人，  
the Beijing students' reactions varied — some, like Nuannuan and Xu Chi, loved it;  
也有像高亮那樣勉強可以接受的人，當然更有避之唯恐不及的人。  
others, like Gao Liang, could barely tolerate it; and some avoided it at all costs.  
李老師擔心大家喝不慣豆汁以致於餓了肚子，還叫了些糖火燒、麒麟酥、  
Worried we'd go hungry from not being able to stomach the *douzhi*, Teacher Li also ordered *tanghuoshao*, *qilin su*,  
密三刀、鹹油酥之類的點心小吃。  
*mi san dao*, *xian you su* — all kinds of pastries and snacks.  

回學校的路上，暖暖感慨地說：「不知道啥原因，豆汁店越來越少了。」  
On the way back, Nuannuan said thoughtfully, "For some reason, *douzhi* shops are getting fewer and fewer."  
『我知道為什麼豆汁店越來越少的原因。』我說。  
"I know why," I said.  
「原因是啥？」暖暖說。  
"Why?"  
『現在早點的選擇那麼多，雖然豆汁別具風味，但有哪個年輕人願意忍受  
"With so many breakfast options these days, sure, *douzhi* has a unique flavor, but what young person is willing to suffer through  
喝餿水一段時間，直到餿水變瓊漿玉液呢？誰能忍受這段過程呢？』  
drinking something that tastes like sewage, waiting for it to turn into ambrosia? Who has the patience for that?"  
「涼涼。」暖暖意味深長地說：「你這話挺有哲理的。」  
"Liangliang," she said meaningfully, "that's actually quite philosophical."  
『是嗎？』  
"Is it?"  
「嗯。」暖暖點點頭，笑著說：「真難得唷。」  
"Mm." She nodded. "A rare occurrence."  

『如果世上的男女都能以純真的心對待彼此，』我看著遠方，說：  
"If every man and woman in the world treated each other with sincere hearts," I said, looking into the distance,  
『到那時豆汁就可以含笑而香了。』  
"then *douzhi* could smile and be fragrant."  
「含笑而香？」  
"Smile and be fragrant?"  
『如果人人都能純真，豆汁便不必以酸、餿、腐來偽裝自己和試煉別人，  
"If everyone were sincere, *douzhi* wouldn't need to disguise itself with sour, rancid, rotten flavors to test people —  
直接用它本質的香面對人們就可以了啊。』  
it could just face people with its natural fragrance."  
「你講的話跟豆汁一樣，」暖暖說，「得聽久了才會習慣。」  
"What you say is like *douzhi*," she said. "You have to listen for a while before you get used to it."  
『習慣後會上癮嗎？』  
"And once I get used to it, will I become addicted?"  
「不會上癮。」暖暖笑了笑，「會麻痺。」  
"Not addicted," she said with a smile. "Just numb."  

走進教室上課前，好多同學拼命漱口想沖淡口齒之間豆汁的怪味。  
Before class started, lots of students were frantically rinsing their mouths, trying to wash away the *douzhi* taste.  
我猜那怪味很難沖淡，因為已深植腦海且遍佈全身。  
I figured it'd be hard to get rid of — it was lodged in your brain and spread through your whole body.  
果然老師一走進教室，便問：「咋有股酸味？你們剛去喝豆汁兒了嗎？」  
Sure enough, the teacher walked in and asked, "What's that sour smell? You kids been drinking *douzhi*?"  
老師自顧自地說起豆汁的種種，神情像是想起初戀時的甜蜜。  
He launched into a talk about *douzhi*, with the look of someone remembering first love.  
「豆汁兒既營養滋味又獨特，我好陣子沒喝了，特懷念。」  
"*Douzhi* is nutritious and uniquely flavorful. I haven't had it in a while — I really miss it."  
老師，拜託別再提豆汁了，快上課吧。  
Teacher, please stop talking about *douzhi* and start the lesson.  

「昨天的床前明月光同學呢？」這是老師言歸正傳後的第一句話。  
"Where's yesterday's 'Bright Moonlight Before the Bed' student?" — that was the teacher's first question once he got down to business.  
大夥先楞了幾秒，然後學弟才緩緩舉起手。  
Everyone froze for a few seconds before my junior slowly raised his hand.  
「來。」老師笑了笑，拿出一卷軸，「這給你。」  
"Come here." The teacher smiled and produced a scroll. "This is for you."  
學弟走上台，解掉綁住卷軸的小繩，卷軸一攤開，快有半個人高度。  
My junior walked up, untied the small cord around the scroll, and unrolled it — it was nearly half a person's height.  
上面寫了兩個又黑又濃又大的毛筆字：「才子」，旁邊還落款。  
On it were two bold, thick, oversized brush characters: *cai zi* — *prodigy*, along with the artist's signature.  

學弟一臉白痴樣，頻頻傻笑，大夥起鬨要照相。  
My junior stood there grinning like an idiot. The crowd clamored for photos.  
學弟一會左手比V、右手拿卷軸；一會換左手拿卷軸、右手比V；  
He alternated poses — left hand in a V, right hand holding the scroll; then swapped;  
一會雙手各比個V，用剩餘的指頭扣著卷軸。  
then both hands in Vs, holding the scroll with whatever fingers were left.  
閃光燈閃啊閃，學弟只是傻笑，口中嘿嘿笑著。  
Flash after flash, and he just stood there grinning, chuckling to himself.  
真是白痴，他大概還不知道所有鏡頭的焦點都只對準那幅卷軸。  
What an idiot — he probably didn't realize every camera was focused on the scroll, not him.  

老師先簡略提起漢字從甲骨文、金文、篆書、隸書、楷書的演變過程，  
The teacher briefly covered the evolution of Chinese script — from oracle bone, bronze, seal, clerical, to regular script —  
最後提到繁體字與簡體字。  
then touched on traditional versus simplified characters.  
說完便給了我們一小本繁簡字對照表，方便我們以後使用，並說：  
He handed out a small booklet with traditional-simplified comparisons for future use, and added:  
「由繁入簡易、由簡入繁難。北京的同學要多用點心。」  
"Going from complex to simple is easy; going from simple to complex is hard. Beijing students should pay extra attention."  
老師接著講漢字簡化的歷史以及簡化的目的，然後是簡化的原則和方法。  
He went on to discuss the history of character simplification, its purpose, principles, and methods.  

我算是看得懂簡體字的台灣人，因為念研究所時讀了幾本簡體字教科書。  
I was a Taiwanese person who could read simplified characters, having used a few simplified textbooks in grad school.  
剛開始看時確實不太懂，看久了也就摸出一些門道。  
At first I really struggled, but over time I figured out some patterns.  
偶爾碰到不懂的字，但只要它跟它的兄弟連在一起，還是可以破解出來。  
Occasionally I'd run into an unrecognizable character, but put it together with its context and I could usually decode it.  
印象中只有「广」和「叶」，曾經困擾我一陣子。  
The only ones I recall giving me trouble were *guang* and *ye*.  
第一次看到广時，發覺一張桌子一隻腳，上頭擺了個東西，那還不塌嗎？  
The first time I saw *guang* — which is a table with one leg and something on top — wouldn't that table collapse?  
叶也是，十個人張口，該不會是吵吧？  
And *ye* — ten mouths open — wouldn't that mean a noisy argument?  
後來跟同學一起琢磨，還請教別人，終於知道分別是廣和葉。  
Later, puzzling it out with classmates and asking around, I learned they were simplified forms of *guang* — vast — and *ye* — leaf.  

老師提醒我們有兩種情形要特別注意：一是簡化後跟已有的字重複，  
The teacher warned us of two situations to watch for: first, when simplification creates overlap with existing characters —  
如後（后）、麵（面）、裡（里）、醜（丑）、隻（只）、雲（云）等。  
like *hou* (后), *mian* (面), *li* (里), *chou* (丑), *zhi* (只), *yun* (云).  
二是兩個字簡化後互相重複，如獲、穫簡化成获；幹、乾簡化成干；  
Second, when two different characters simplify to the same one — like *huo* and *huo* both become *获*; *gan* and *gan* both become *干*;  
髮、發簡化成发；鐘、鍾簡化成钟；復、複簡化成复等。  
*fa* and *fa* both become *发*; *zhong* and *zhong* both become *钟*; *fu* and *fu* both become *复*.  
「如果有個老爸將他四個女兒分別叫劉雲雲、劉云云、劉雲云、劉雲云，  
"If a father named his four daughters Liu Yunyun, Liu Yunyun, Liu Yunyun, and Liu Yunyun — mixing traditional and simplified —  
那這四個女孩的名字簡化後都叫刘云云。」老師笑了笑，  
"after simplification, all four would be called Liu Yunyun." The teacher smiled.  
「這也是簡化漢字的好處，人變少了，反正中國人口太多。」  
"That's another benefit of simplification — fewer people. China has too many anyway."  

我看著黑板上寫的髮和發，簡化後都是发，這讓我很納悶。  
I stared at the characters *fa* (hair) and *fa* (to emit) on the board — both simplified to *发*. That bothered me.  
『暖暖。』我轉頭說，『我頭发白了。』  
"Nuannuan," I said, turning to her. "My *tou fa* turned white."  
暖暖仔細打量我頭髮，然後說：「沒看見白頭发呀。」  
She looked carefully at my hair. "I don't see any white *tou fa*."  
『我的意思是：頭“发白”了。』  
"I meant: my head *fa bai* — turned white."  
「頭咋會发白？」  
"How can a head *fa bai*?"  
『頭本來是黑色的，理了光頭就變白了。』  
"Originally black, but if you shave it, it turns white."  
「無聊。」暖暖瞪我一眼。  
"Boring," she said, shooting me a look.  
『而且頭發白是驚嚇的最高境界，比臉發白還嚴重。』我說。  
"And *tou fa bai* — hair turning white — is the ultimate level of shock, worse than a pale face," I said.  
暖暖轉過頭去，不想理我。  
She turned away, ignoring me.  

「隻」簡化變「只」，如果有人說：「我養的豬只會吃青菜。」  
When *zhi* (only) and *zhi* (single) both simplify to *只*: if someone says "My pig *zhi hui* eats greens,"  
是豬也會吃青菜的意思？還是牠是具有佛性的豬，於是只吃青菜？  
does it mean the pig *also* eats greens? Or is it an enlightened pig that eats *only* greens?  
「幹」、「乾」簡化後都是「干」，如果有天我當了書店員工，  
When *gan* (do) and *gan* (dry) both become *干*: if I worked in a bookstore someday and  
看到一本小說叫《我干妹妹的故事》，干是動詞？還是形容詞？  
saw a novel called *My Gan Younger Sister's Story*, is *gan* a verb or an adjective?  
我怎麼知道要把它擺進情色文學區？還是青春小說區？  
How would I know whether to put it in Erotica or Young Adult?  

「麵」簡化變「面」，如果我不小心英雄救美，美人不好意思開口道謝，  
When *mian* (noodles) and *mian* (face) both become *面*: if I heroically save a damsel and she's too shy to thank me aloud,  
於是她用簡體字寫了紙條：「为了感谢你，我下面给你吃。」  
and writes me a note in simplified characters: *"To thank you, I'll xia mian for you to eat"* —  
我實在分不出來她是親切還是淫蕩？萬一我會錯意就完了。  
I can't tell if she's being kind or obscene. If I misunderstand, I'm done for.  
雖然看來似乎很恐怖，但對寫簡體字小說的人反而是好事。  
As terrifying as it sounds, this is actually a boon for writers using simplified characters —  
因為充滿了很多雙關語，必然為小說帶來更高的精彩度，  
the abundance of puns inevitably adds richness to fiction,  
這是寫繁體字小說者無法享受的特權。  
a privilege writers of traditional characters can't enjoy.  

快下課前，老師說他以前跟台灣朋友常用電子郵件通信，  
Just before class ended, the teacher mentioned he used to exchange emails with Taiwanese friends.  
那時繁簡字電腦編碼的轉換技術還不成熟，往往只能用英文溝通。  
Back then, encoding conversion between traditional and simplified was primitive — they often had to communicate in English.  
「沒想到都用中文的人竟然得靠英文溝通。」老師感慨地說，  
"Two people who both speak Chinese, reduced to communicating in English," he said with emotion.  
「結果大家的英文都變好了，中文卻變差了。」  
"End result: everyone's English improved while their Chinese got worse."  
老師說完後頓了頓，意味深長地看了全體學生一眼，然後說：  
He paused, gave the class a meaningful look, and added:  
「希望你們以後不會出現這種遺憾。」  
"I hope you never suffer that same regret."  

下了課，李老師急著催我們到食堂吃飯；到了食堂，又催我們吃快點。  
After class, Teacher Li hurried us to the canteen; once there, he hurried us to eat quickly.  
「抓緊時間。」李老師說，「去天壇一定要人最少的時候去。」  
"Time's tight," he said. "You have to visit the Temple of Heaven when it's least crowded."  
『為什麼要挑人最少的時候去天壇？』我問暖暖。  
"Why go when there are the fewest people?" I asked Nuannuan.  
「別問我。」暖暖說，「我也不知道。」  
"Don't ask me," she said. "I don't know either."  
『為什麼現在去天壇，人最少？』我又問。  
"Why are there the fewest people at the Temple of Heaven right now?"  
「現在是大熱天，又正值中午，誰會出門亂晃？」暖暖回答。  
"It's the middle of a hot day at noon — who'd be out wandering?" she replied.  
『為什麼……』  
"Why—"  
「別再問為什麼了。」暖暖打斷我，「再問我就收錢了。」  
"Stop asking *why*." She cut me off. "One more *why* and I'm charging you."  
我掏出一塊人民幣放到暖暖面前，問：『為什麼妳長得特別漂亮？』  
I pulled out one yuan and placed it in front of her. "Why are you especially beautiful?"  
「這題不用錢。」暖暖笑了，「因為天生麗質。」  
"This one's free." She smiled. "Because I was born with natural beauty."  

大夥從南天門進入天壇，果然天氣熱又逢正午，幾乎沒別的遊客。  
We entered the Temple of Heaven through the South Heaven Gate. Just as predicted, it was hot, it was noon, and there were almost no other visitors.  
進門就看到一座露天的上、中、下三層圓形石壇，李老師說這叫圜丘壇。  
Inside the gate stood an open-air, three-tiered circular stone altar. Teacher Li called it the Circular Mound Altar.  
圜丘壇被兩重矮牆圍著，外面是正方形、裡面是圓形，象徵著天圓地方。  
It was enclosed by two low walls — the outer square, the inner circle, symbolizing heaven round and earth square.  
這裡是皇帝冬至祭天的地方。  
This was where the emperor performed the winter solstice ritual to honor heaven.  
「先繼續往北走，待會再折回來。」李老師說。  
"Let's keep heading north first; we'll come back later," Teacher Li said.  

我們沒登上圜丘壇，沿著下層石壇邊緣走弧線，走到正北再轉直線前進。  
We didn't climb the altar but walked an arc along its lower rim, then turned straight north.  
一出圜丘壇，便看到一座具藍色琉璃瓦單簷尖頂的殿宇。  
Exiting the Circular Mound, we saw a hall with a single-eave, pointed roof of blue glazed tiles.  
「這是皇穹宇，是供奉皇天上帝和皇帝祖先牌位的地方。」  
"This is the Imperial Vault of Heaven, where tablets of the Supreme Ruler of Heaven and the imperial ancestors are kept."  
同學們一聽，便想往殿內走去。李老師說等等，先往旁走。  
Hearing this, everyone wanted to go inside. Teacher Li said to wait, and led us aside instead.  
「太好了，這時候果然沒人。」李老師在圓形圍牆旁停下腳步，說：  
"Perfect — just as I hoped, no one's here." He stopped by a circular wall.  
「這裡是回音壁。待會兩人一組，各站在圓形直徑的兩端，對著牆說話，  
"This is the Echo Wall. Pair up, stand at opposite ends of the diameter, and speak toward the wall.  
聲音不必大，也不用緊貼著牆。大家試試能不能聽出回音。」  
Keep your voice low; you don't need to press close. See if you can hear each other's echo."  

回音壁直徑61.5公尺、高3.7公尺、厚0.9公尺，是皇穹宇的圍牆。  
The Echo Wall — 61.5 meters in diameter, 3.7 meters tall, 0.9 meters thick — encloses the Imperial Vault.  
牆身為淡灰色城磚，磨磚對縫、光滑嚴密，牆頂為藍色琉璃瓦簷。  
Made of pale gray bricks, ground smooth and fitted tight, with a blue glazed tile cornice.  
奇怪的是，現在氣溫超過30度，但沿著圓牆走，卻是清涼無比。  
Strangely, though the temperature was above thirty degrees, walking along the circular wall felt wonderfully cool.  
我走到定位，耳朵靠近牆，隱約聽到風聲，還有一些破碎的聲音。  
I reached my position and put my ear to the wall. I could faintly hear wind and fragmentary sounds.  
「涼涼。」  
"Liangliang."  
我聽到了，是暖暖的聲音，但聲音似乎被冰過，比暖暖的原音更冷更低。  
I heard it — Nuannuan's voice, but it sounded chilled, colder and lower than her natural tone.  
『妳是人還是鬼？』我對著牆說。  
"Are you a person or a ghost?" I said toward the wall.  
暖暖笑了，笑聲細細碎碎，有點像鳥叫聲。  
She laughed, a thin, fragmented sound like birdsong.  

「我聽到了。」暖暖的聲音。  
"I heard you," Nuannuan's voice came.  
『我也聽到了。』我說。  
"I heard you too," I said.  
「你吃飽了嗎？」暖暖的聲音。  
"Have you eaten enough?"  
『我吃飽了。』我說。  
"I've eaten enough."  
「涼涼。」  
"Liangliang."  
『暖暖。』  
"Nuannuan."  
「我不知道該說啥了。」暖暖的聲音。  
"I don't know what else to say," she said.  
『我也是耶。』我說。  
"Me neither," I said.  

暖暖和我都很興奮，興奮過了頭，反而不知道該說什麼？  
We were both so excited that we forgot what to say.  
以前都是看著對方說話，現在對牆壁說話、從牆壁聽到回答，真不習慣。  
We'd always talked face to face; speaking to a wall and hearing an answer from a wall felt strange.  
我們隨便說些不著邊際的話，反正話不是重點，重點只是發出聲音。  
We rambled about nothing — the words weren't the point, just making sounds.  
我學狗叫，暖暖學貓叫；我再學被車撞到的狗，暖暖便學被狗嚇到的貓。  
I barked like a dog; she meowed like a cat. I did a dog hit by a car; she did a cat scared by a dog.  
我試著說英文，也許回音壁有靈性，搞不好不屑英文，但暖暖還是聽到。  
I tried English — maybe the Echo Wall was spiritual and looked down on English — but she heard it anyway.  
「我是才子啊，佳人在哪？」學弟的聲音。  
"I'm the *caizi* — where's the beauty?" That was my junior's voice.  
轉頭看見王克在我五步外，她瞥見我的神情，有些不好意思便走開了些。  
I turned and saw Wang Ke about five steps away. She caught my expression, looked embarrassed, and moved off.  

「我要去暖暖！」暖暖的聲音。  
"I want to go to Nuannuan!" Nuannuan's voice.  
我吃了一驚，決定裝死。  
I was startled and decided to play dead.  
『聽不清楚啊。』我說。  
"Can't hear clearly!" I said.  
「別裝樣了，你明明聽到了。」  
"Stop pretending — you clearly heard me."  
『我沒裝樣啊。』我說完就發現露底了。  
"I'm not pretending," I said, and immediately realized I'd given myself away.  
果然暖暖笑了，還笑得又細又長，似乎想讓我覺得不好意思。  
Sure enough, she laughed — a long, thin laugh, as if trying to make me feel embarrassed.  

暖暖笑著的同時，我彷彿聽見心裡的聲音，也許那聲音一直在心裡亂竄，  
As she laughed, I seemed to hear a voice inside me — perhaps it had been rattling around in there all along,  
直到此刻遇見回音壁，才清晰湧現。  
only now made clear by the Echo Wall.  
『暖暖，我……』我說。  
"Nuannuan, I—" I said.  
「後面聽不清楚。」暖暖的聲音。  
"Can't hear the rest," she said.  
『暖暖。』說完後，我把頭往後仰，把聲音降到最低最輕最小，說：  
"Nuannuan." I tilted my head back and lowered my voice to the faintest whisper.  
『我喜歡妳。』  
"I like you."  
「後面還是聽不清楚呀。」  
"Still can't hear the rest!"  
『別裝樣了。』我說。  
"Stop pretending," I said.  
「我沒裝樣呀。」暖暖似乎急了。  
"I'm not pretending!" She sounded anxious.  

暖暖，我知道妳沒聽見，但總之我說了。  
Nuannuan, I know you didn't hear me. But I said it.  
這是我心裡的回音。  
That was the echo of my heart.  
這種回音不需要被回應，它只想傳遞。  
That kind of echo doesn't need an answer. It only wants to be sent.  

李老師讓大夥玩了20分鐘，才簡略說出回音壁的原理。  
Teacher Li let us play for twenty minutes before briefly explaining the Echo Wall's principle.  
這道理不難懂，聲波在圓形的凹面內，藉由連續反射而傳播。  
It wasn't complicated: sound waves within a concave circular surface propagate through continuous reflection.  
牆面堅硬又光滑，讓聲波的逸散減到最小，才能聽到幾十公尺外的回音。  
The hard, smooth wall minimized sound dispersion, making it possible to hear echoes tens of meters away.  
道理說來簡單，但建築時的精確計算、建材的選擇、施工的細密，  
Simple in theory, but the precision of the calculations, choice of materials, and care in construction  
才是這幾百年前興建的回音壁不可思議之處。  
were what made this centuries-old Echo Wall truly incredible.  

我這時才知道李老師為什麼一定要挑人最少的時候來，  
Now I understood why Teacher Li insisted on coming when it was least crowded —  
因為一旦遊客多，所有人七嘴八舌亂喊亂叫：  
because when there are lots of tourists, everyone's shouting and yelling,  
ㄚ頭、老爸、妹子唷、哥哥呀、我想放屁、吃屎吧你……  
"Hey babe!" "Dad!" "Sis!" "Bro!" "I gotta fart!" "Eat shit!" —  
你能聽出什麼？  
what could you possibly hear?  
別說幾十公尺外的回音了，有人在附近高喊救命你也未必聽得見。  
Forget echoes tens of meters away — you might not even hear someone screaming for help right next to you.  

李老師帶領大夥走回皇穹宇的大殿前，當我們又想走進殿內時，  
Teacher Li led us back to the front of the Imperial Vault. When we tried to go inside again,  
「再等等。」李老師笑了。  
"Not yet," he smiled.  
李老師在皇穹宇前自北向南的甬道上跨了三大步，停在第三塊石板上。  
He took three large strides on the north-south walkway in front of the hall and stopped on the third stone slab.  
「這是三音石。大家輪流在此擊掌，試試能不能聽到三個回聲。」他說。  
"This is the Triple Sound Stone. Take turns clapping here and see if you can hear three echoes."  
大夥一個一個輪流站在第三塊石板上用力擊掌，每個人都擊完掌後，  
One by one we stood on the third slab and clapped loudly. After everyone had a turn,  
便圍在一起詢問彼此聽到的回音狀況，然後討論起原理。  
we gathered to compare what we'd heard and discuss the principle.  

這第三塊石板剛好是回音壁的圓心，聲音向四周傳播，碰到回音壁反射，  
The third slab is exactly at the Echo Wall's center. Sound travels outward, hits the wall, reflects back,  
回到圓心聚集；然後繼續前進，碰回音壁，再反射，又回到圓心。  
converges at the center; then travels out again, reflects again, returns again.  
只不過聲音終究會損失，所以聽到的回聲會越來越弱。  
But sound loses energy, so the echoes get progressively fainter.  
在環境極度安靜、擊掌力道夠強、耳朵內沒耳屎的條件下，  
In perfect silence, with a strong clap and clean ears,  
搞不好可以隱約聽到第四個回聲。  
you might even faintly hear a fourth echo.  

「你們好厲害。」李老師拍拍手。  
"You're amazing." Teacher Li clapped.  
「老師應該站在第三塊石板上拍手，這樣我們會覺得更厲害。」學弟說。  
"You should stand on the third slab and clap, Teacher — then we'd think *you* were amazing," my junior said.  
李老師笑了笑，站在三音石上用力拍手十幾聲，我們也都笑了。  
Teacher Li smiled, stood on the Triple Sound Stone, and clapped loudly a dozen times. We all laughed.  
這其實不算什麼，畢竟我們這群學生當中，不管來自台灣或北京，  
It was nothing, really — among our group, whether from Taiwan or Beijing,  
起碼有一半唸理工。  
at least half studied science or engineering.  

走回三層的圜丘壇，我們直接爬到最上層，壇面除中心石是圓形外，  
Back at the three-tiered Circular Mound, we climbed straight to the top. Apart from the circular center stone,  
外圍各圈的石頭均為扇形。  
all the surrounding stones were fan-shaped.  
「這塊叫天心石。」李老師指著中心那塊圓石，「據說站在那兒即使小聲  
"This is the Heaven's Heart Stone," Teacher Li said, pointing at the center. "It's said that even a whisper from that spot  
說話，回音卻很洪亮，而且好像是從天外飛來的回音。原理你們比老師  
produces a booming echo, as if it came from beyond the heavens. You all know the principle better than I do —  
內行，說給我聽聽？」  
explain it to me?"  

這個原理跟三音石差不多，天心石正好在圓心，圓周是漢白玉石欄板。  
The principle is similar to the Triple Sound Stone. The Heaven's Heart Stone sits at the center; the circumference is ringed by white marble balustrades.  
聲波向四周傳播，碰到堅固圓弧形欄板後，反射回到圓心集中。  
Sound radiates outward, hits the curved balustrades, and reflects back to the center.  
與三音石不同的是，圜丘壇面光滑、壇內無任何障礙物，且圓半徑較小，  
Different from the Triple Sound Stone, the altar's surface is smooth, has no obstacles, and has a smaller radius —  
因此發出聲音後，回音以極快速度傳回，讓人幾乎無法分辨回音與原音。  
so the echo returns so fast you can barely distinguish it from the original sound.  
原音與回音疊加的結果，聲音聽起來便更加響亮且有共鳴感。  
The superposition makes the sound louder and more resonant.  
又因為聲波由四面八方反射傳回，根本搞不清楚回音的方向，  
And since the sound reflects from all directions, you can't tell where the echo is coming from,  
便會有回音是從天外飛來的錯覺。  
creating the illusion that it comes from beyond the sky.  

「古時候皇帝在這裡祭天，只要輕喊一聲，四面八方立刻傳來洪亮回聲，  
"When ancient emperors performed the heaven-worshipping ceremony here, a single soft utterance would be met with a booming echo from all directions —  
就像上天的神諭一般，加上祭禮時的莊嚴肅穆，氣氛更顯得神秘。」  
like a divine oracle — and with the solemnity of the ritual, the atmosphere would be all the more mystical."  
李老師又說環繞天心石的扇形石是艾青石，上、中、下層各九環，  
He added that the fan-shaped stones around the center were *aiqing* stone, arranged in nine rings per tier,  
越外環扇形石越多，但數目都是九的倍數。  
with more stones on each outer ring, but always in multiples of nine.  
層與層間的階梯各九級，上層石欄板72塊、中層108塊、下層180塊，  
The stairs between tiers each had nine steps; the top tier had 72 balustrade panels, the middle 108, the bottom 180 —  
不僅都是九的倍數，而且加起來共360塊，剛好符合360周天度數。  
all multiples of nine, and together totaling 360, matching the 360 degrees of the celestial circle.  
藉由反複使用九和九的倍數以呼應「九重天」，並強調天的至高無上。  
By repeating nine and its multiples, the design echoed the nine layers of heaven and emphasized heaven's supremacy.  

李老師要我們輪流站上天心石試試，可惜現在已出現一些遊客，  
Teacher Li had us each take a turn on the Heaven's Heart Stone. Unfortunately, a few tourists had appeared,  
在人聲略微吵雜的環境中，回音效果恐怕不會太好。  
and with the background noise, the echo effect wouldn't be great.  
還有個小女孩拉著她老爸放聲大哭，我幾乎脫口而出叫所有人都閉嘴，  
A little girl was holding her dad's hand and bawling. I nearly shouted for everyone to shut up —  
就讓她坐在天心石上大哭，看看會不會哭聲震天，讓老天不爽打起雷來。  
just let her sit on the Heaven's Heart Stone and wail, see if her crying would shake the heavens and make the gods angry enough to send thunder.  

輪到我站上天心石時，我仰望著天，說：『謝謝啦。』  
When it was my turn, I looked up at the sky and said, "Thanks."  
可能是心理作用，我覺得聲音確實變大了，隱約也聽到回聲。  
Maybe it was psychological, but my voice did seem louder, and I thought I heard an echo.  
「你說啥呀。」暖暖說。  
"What'd you say?" Nuannuan said.  
我告訴暖暖，中學時唸過一篇叫《謝天》的課文，陳之藩寫的。  
I told her about a text I'd read in middle school called *Thanking Heaven* by Chen Zhifan.  
裡頭有句：「因為需要感謝的人太多了，就感謝天吧。」  
It had a line: "When there are too many people to thank, just thank heaven instead."  
那時感動得一塌糊塗，現在終於可以直接向老天表達感謝之意。  
It had moved me deeply back then, and now I finally had the chance to express my thanks directly.  

『我還聽到回聲喔。』我說，『而且不只一個。』  
"I even heard echoes," I said. "More than one."  
「真的嗎？」暖暖很好奇。  
"Really?" She was curious.  
『嗯。』我點點頭，『我一共聽到九個回聲，第一個回聲是：不客氣。』  
"Mm." I nodded. "I heard nine echoes total. The first one was: *You're welcome*."  
「…………」  
"…"  
『第二個回聲是……』  
"The second was—"  
「你別說。」暖暖打斷我，「因為我沒問。」  
"Don't tell me," she cut in. "Because I didn't ask."  
『讓我說嘛。』  
"Let me say it."  

暖暖不理我，加快腳步往前走。  
She ignored me and walked faster.  
我在後頭自言自語，依序說出第二個到第八個回聲：你辛苦了、  
I muttered to myself behind her, listing the second through eighth echoes: *You've worked hard*,  
你真是客氣的人、現在很少看到像你這樣知恩圖報的人、北京好玩嗎、  
*You're such a polite person*, *It's rare to see someone so grateful these days*, *Having fun in Beijing?*,  
還習慣嗎、累不累、有沒有認識新朋友。  
*Getting used to it?*, *Tired?*, *Made any new friends?*  
『第九個回聲最重要，因為是九。』我說，『第九個回聲聽起來最清晰，  
"The ninth echo was the most important, because it's nine." I said. "It was the clearest too.  
祂說：嗯，暖暖確實是個好女孩。』  
It said: *Yes, Nuannuan really is a good girl.*"  
暖暖停下腳步，說：「為什麼第九個回聲會提到我？」  
Nuannuan stopped. "Why did the ninth echo mention me?"  

『當第八個回聲說有沒有認識新朋友？我便在心裡回答：有，她叫暖暖，  
"When the eighth echo asked if I'd made any new friends, I answered in my heart: *Yes, her name is Nuannuan,*  
她是個好女孩。』我說，『於是祂便給了第九個回聲。』  
*she's a good girl.* Then heaven gave the ninth echo."  
暖暖轉過身面對著我，停了幾秒後，說：「瞎說了這麼久，渴了吧？」  
She turned and faced me. After a few seconds, she said, "All that nonsense must've made you thirsty."  
『嗯。』我點點頭。  
"Mm." I nodded.  
「待會買瓶酸奶喝。」暖暖笑了。  
"I'll buy you a yogurt drink later." She smiled.  
『好啊。』我也笑了。  
"Sounds good." I smiled too.  

我和暖暖並肩走著，她說：「想知道剛剛我在天心石上說啥嗎？」  
We walked side by side. "Want to know what *I* said on the Heaven's Heart Stone?" she said.  
『妳在天心石上說什麼？』我問。  
"What did you say?" I asked.  
「我想去暖暖。」暖暖說，「而且我也聽到回音呢。」  
"I want to go to Nuannuan. And I heard echoes too."  
『妳別說。因為我沒問。』我說。  
"Don't tell me — because I didn't ask." I said.  
「嘿嘿，我也聽到九個回聲。」暖暖笑了，「前面八個回聲是：挺好呀、  
"Heh heh, I heard nine echoes too." She laughed. "The first eight were: *Go for it*,  
就去唄、一定要去、非去不可、不可不去、不去不行、不去我就打雷、  
*Just go*, *Absolutely must go*, *Have to go*, *Cannot not go*, *If you don't go I'll send thunder*,  
打雷了妳還是得去。」  
*Even with thunder you still have to go*."  

我加快腳步跑走，暖暖立刻跟上來；我東閃西閃，暖暖還是緊跟在旁。  
I quickened my pace and ran. She caught up immediately. I dodged left and right, but she stayed right beside me.  
「第九個回聲最重要，祂說：這是暖暖和涼涼的約定。』暖暖對著我說。  
"The ninth echo was the most important. It said: *This is Nuannuan and Liangliang's promise*," she said to me.  
『還好妳只是瞎說。』我說。  
"Good thing you're just making things up," I said.  
「反正你聽到了。」暖暖聳聳肩。  
"You heard me anyway," she shrugged.  

又來到了皇穹宇，這次終於可以走進殿內了。  
We arrived back at the Imperial Vault. Finally, we could go inside.  
總共三次經過皇穹宇門口卻沒走進去，我們好像都成了大禹了。  
After three passes at the entrance without entering, we were practically like Yu the Great — three times past his own door without going in.  
殿內正北有個圓形石座，位於最高處的神龕內供奉著皇天上帝的神位。  
Inside, a circular stone pedestal stood at the north end; at the highest point, a shrine held the tablet of the Supreme Ruler of Heaven.  
殿內東西兩廂各排列四個神位，供奉清朝前八位皇帝，  
On either side, east and west, eight tablets were arranged — the first eight Qing emperors:  
分別是努爾哈赤、皇太極、順治、康熙、雍正、乾隆、嘉慶、道光。  
Nurhaci, Hong Taiji, Shunzhi, Kangxi, Yongzheng, Qianlong, Jiaqing, Daoguang.  
『我記得清朝共有十二個皇帝。』我問暖暖：『咸豐、同治、光緒、宣統  
"I thought there were twelve Qing emperors," I said to Nuannuan. "Where are Xianfeng, Tongzhi, Guangxu, and Puyi?"  
的神位呢？』  
「興許他們覺得把中國搞得烏煙瘴氣，便不好意思住進來了。」暖暖說。  
"Maybe they feel they made such a mess of China that they're too ashamed to be here," she said.  

離開皇穹宇繼續朝北走，走在長長的丹陛橋上，兩旁都是柏樹。  
Leaving the Imperial Vault, we headed north on the long Danbi Bridge, lined on both sides with cypress trees.  
李老師說天壇內有六萬多株柏樹，密植的柏樹讓天壇顯得更肅穆。  
Teacher Li said the Temple of Heaven grounds held over sixty thousand cypresses, their density adding to the solemnity.  
丹陛橋由南向北，逐漸緩慢升高，並明顯被縱向劃分為左、中、右三條。  
The Danbi Bridge rose gently from south to north, clearly divided into three lanes: left, center, right.  
中間是神走的神道；右邊是皇帝走的皇道；左邊是王公大臣走的王道。  
The center was the sacred path for the gods; the right, the imperial path for the emperor; the left, the royal path for nobles and officials.  
李老師話剛說完，所有同學不約而同都走到中間的神道。  
The moment Teacher Li finished, every student without exception stepped onto the sacred middle path.  
『神道根本沒必要建造。』我說，『既然是神，難道還會用走的嗎？』  
"The sacred path is totally unnecessary," I said. "If they're gods, do they even *walk*?"  
暖暖睜大眼睛，過一會笑出來，說：「你這問題，還真讓人答不上來。」  
Nuannuan's eyes widened; she laughed after a moment. "That question is genuinely unanswerable."  

有同學問：這明明是條路，為何要叫橋？  
A student asked: "This is clearly a road — why is it called a bridge?"  
李老師回答：下面有條東西向通道，與丹陛橋成立體交叉，所以叫橋。  
Teacher Li replied: "There's an east-west passage underneath, intersecting with it at different levels — so it's a bridge."  
「那條通道是給牛羊等牲畜走的，牠們會走到幾百米外的宰牲亭被宰殺，  
"That passage is for livestock like cattle and sheep, which walked hundreds of meters to the slaughter pavilion to be butchered  
然後製成祭品。所以那條通道被叫做鬼門關，哪位同學想走走看？」  
and turned into sacrifices. That's why it's called the Gate of Hell. Any student want to take a walk down it?"  
大夥很正常，一個想走的人也沒。  
Everyone, being perfectly normal, declined.  

終於來到天壇的代表建築祈年殿，這是座有鎏金寶頂的三重簷圓形大殿，  
We finally reached the Temple of Heaven's iconic building — the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, a triple-eaved circular hall with a gilded gold spire.  
殿簷是深藍色，用藍色琉璃瓦鋪砌成。藍色和圓，都是代表天。  
The eaves were deep blue, covered in blue glazed tiles. Blue and circle both represented heaven.  
皇帝在這裡舉行儀式，祈求風調雨順、五穀豐登。  
Here the emperor performed ceremonies praying for favorable weather and bountiful harvests.  
殿高九丈九（約32米），全部採用木結構，以28根木柱支撐殿頂重量。  
The hall stood nine *zhang* nine *chi* tall — about 32 meters — entirely of timber, supported by 28 wooden columns.  
28根木柱分三圈，內圈4柱代表四季；中圈12柱代表十二個月；  
The columns formed three rings: the inner four represented the four seasons; the middle twelve represented the twelve months;  
外圈12柱代表十二個時辰；中外圈相加為24，代表一年二十四節氣；  
the outer twelve represented the twelve two-hour periods of the day; the middle and outer together made 24, representing the 24 solar terms;  
三圈相加為28，代表二十八星宿。  
all three rings together made 28, representing the 28 lunar mansions.  

祈年殿坐落在三層圓形漢白玉石台基上，每層都有雕花的漢白玉石欄板。  
The hall sat on a three-tiered circular white marble platform, each tier with carved marble balustrades.  
遠遠望去，深藍色的殿簷、純白色的漢白玉、赭色的木門和木柱、  
From a distance — deep blue eaves, pure white marble, ochre wooden doors and pillars,  
和璽彩繪的青、綠、紅、金，整體建築的色彩對比強烈卻不失和諧。  
the blue, green, red, and gold of the painted ornament — the building's contrast was striking yet harmonious.  
我和暖暖在祈年殿大門往南遠眺，丹陛橋以極小的坡降筆直向南延伸，  
Standing at the hall's entrance and looking south, the Danbi Bridge stretched straight ahead with an almost imperceptible slope,  
兩旁古柏翠綠蒼勁，偶見幾座門廊殿宇，視野似乎沒有盡頭。  
ancient cypresses in vivid green on either side, with occasional gates and halls in view — the vista seemed endless.  
這令人有種正從天上緩慢滑下來的錯覺。  
It felt like slowly gliding down from heaven.  
暖暖買來了酸奶，我們便享受一面滑行、一面喝酸奶的快感。  
Nuannuan bought yogurt drinks, and we enjoyed the pleasure of gliding while sipping.  

大夥從北天門離開天壇，李老師說要讓我們去前門大石辣兒逛逛。  
We exited through the North Heaven Gate, and Teacher Li said we'd visit *Da Shi La'r* at Qianmen.  
大石辣兒離天壇不遠，一下子就到了。  
*Da Shi La'r* wasn't far from the Temple of Heaven — we arrived quickly.  
「大石辣兒是北京最古老、也曾是最繁華的商業區，是北京老字號最密集  
"*Da Shi La'r* is Beijing's oldest and once most prosperous commercial district, with the highest concentration of Beijing's time-honored brands:  
的地方。經營中藥的同仁堂、經營布匹的瑞蚨祥、經營帽子的馬聚源、  
Tongrentang for traditional medicine, Ruifuxiang for fabrics, Majuyuan for hats,  
經營布鞋的內聯陞、經營茶葉的張一元等，都是響噹噹的百年老店。」  
Neiliansheng for cloth shoes, Zhangyiyuan for tea — all renowned century-old shops."  
李老師說著說著已走到街口，約兩層樓高的鐵製鏤空柵欄上頭，  
As he spoke, we reached the street entrance, where an iron arch about two stories high, with an intricate cut-out pattern,  
題了三個大金字：大柵欄。  
bore three large gold characters: *Da Zha Lan*.  

『這……』我有些激動，問暖暖：『難道這就是……』  
"This—" I was a little excited. "Is this—"  
「大石辣兒。」暖暖笑了。  
"*Da Shi La'r*," Nuannuan said, smiling.  
『柵欄可以唸成石辣嗎？』  
"Can *zha lan* be pronounced *shi la*?"  
「我查過字典。」暖暖說，「不行。」  
"I checked the dictionary," she said. "No."  
『那……』  
"Then—"  
「別問了。」暖暖說，「就跟著叫唄。」  
"Don't ask," she said. "Just follow along and call it that."  

據說明孝宗時，為防止京城內日益猖獗的盜賊，便在街巷口設立柵欄，  
It's said that during the Ming Hongzhi era, to combat worsening banditry in the capital, gates were set up at street and alley entrances —  
夜間關閉，重要的柵欄夜間還有士兵看守。  
closed at night, with guards posted at the important ones.  
由於這裡商店集中，柵欄建得又大又好，因此人們就叫這裡「大柵欄」。  
Because this area was dense with shops, the gates here were built big and sturdy, so people called it *Da Zha Lan* — Big Gate.  
清初有禁令：「內城逼近宮闕，嚴禁喧嘩」，因為這裡剛好在警戒線外，  
An early Qing ban read: "The inner city is near the palace — all noise is strictly forbidden." Since this area fell just outside the security zone,  
大家便來這裡找樂子，現存的慶樂園、廣德樓、廣和園等戲園子，  
everyone came here for entertainment. The surviving theaters — Qingle Yuan, Guangde Lou, Guanghe Yuan —  
當時都是夜夜笙歌的場所。  
were all places of nightly revelry.  
這裡也成為老北京人喝茶、看戲、購物的地方，是生活中的一部份。  
This became where old Beijingers drank tea, watched opera, and shopped — part of daily life.  

我和暖暖沿街閒逛，先被一座像是戲園子建築的大觀樓吸引住目光，  
Nuannuan and I strolled along the street. A theater-like building called Daguang Lou caught my eye,  
上頭還有「中國電影誕生地」的牌匾。  
with a plaque declaring it the "Birthplace of Chinese Cinema."  
裡頭是上下兩層環形建築，有大量歷史照片和畫冊掛在四周牆壁上。  
Inside, it was a two-tiered circular building, with historical photos and albums lining the walls.  
原來這是座電影院，1905年中國第一部電影《定軍山》就在這放映。  
It was a cinema — the first Chinese film, *Dingjun Mountain*, was screened here in 1905.  
看到陳列的舊時電影放映器材，我告訴暖暖我想起小時候看的露天電影。  
Seeing the old projectors on display, I told her it reminded me of the open-air movies I'd watched as a child.  
那時只要有慶典，廟口空地總是拉起長長的白幕，夜間便放映電影。  
Back then, whenever there was a festival, a long white screen would be set up in the temple square for night screenings.  
我總喜歡待在放映師旁，看他慢慢捲動電影膠帶。  
I always liked to sit by the projectionist and watch him slowly wind the film reels.  
暖暖說她小時候也特愛看露天電影。  
Nuannuan said she'd loved open-air movies as a kid too.  

走出大觀樓，心裡裝滿舊時回憶，彷彿自己已變回活蹦亂跳的小孩。  
We stepped out of Daguang Lou, hearts full of nostalgia, as if we'd turned back into lively children.  
大柵欄是步行街，沒有車輛進入，商家老字號牌匾更襯托出街景的古老。  
Da Zha Lan was a pedestrian street — no vehicles — and the old shop signs made the street look even more ancient.  
暖暖說有些街景她似乎曾在電視的清裝劇上看過。  
Nuannuan said some of the street scenes looked like they were from Qing dynasty TV dramas.  
大柵欄裡都是商店，但我口袋不滿，因此購買欲不高。  
The street was full of shops, but my pockets weren't full, so my desire to buy was low.  
服務態度還算不錯，有時見顧客買了東西，店員常會說：  
Service was decent. When a customer bought something, the staff would often say:  
「這是您——買的東西，這是您——要的發票，我把發票放在這袋子裡，  
"These are the things *you* — bought, this is the receipt *you* — need, I'm putting the receipt in the bag for *you* —  
您——比較好拿。」  
so *you* — can carry it more easily."  
說到「您」字總是拉長尾音，挺有趣的。  
They always drew out the *nin* — polite *you* — making it amusing.  

當看到商品標示的價錢時，我第一反應便是換算成台幣，價錢果然便宜。  
When I saw the prices, my first instinct was to convert to Taiwanese dollars — they really were cheap.  
「人民幣和台幣咋換算？」暖暖問。  
"How do you convert RMB and Taiwan dollars?" Nuannuan asked.  
『大約一比四。』我說，『一塊人民幣可換四塊台幣。』  
"About one to four," I said. "One RMB equals four Taiwan dollars."  
「嗯。」暖暖點頭表示理解，然後指著一個標著兩百塊的花瓶，  
"Mm." She nodded in understanding, then pointed at a vase marked 200 RMB.  
「所以這是五十塊台幣？」  
"So that's 50 Taiwan dollars?"  
『是八百塊台幣啦！』我瞪大眼睛不敢置信。  
"That's 800 Taiwan dollars!" My eyes went wide in disbelief.  
暖暖吐了吐舌頭，說：「我算術一向不好。」  
Nuannuan stuck out her tongue. "I've never been good at math."  
『這哪叫不好？』我說，『這叫很糟。』  
"This isn't *not good*," I said. "This is *terrible*."  

我從皮夾掏出一張自從來北京後就沒有出來曬太陽的百元台幣，說：  
I pulled out a 100 Taiwan dollar bill from my wallet — one that hadn't seen the sun since I arrived in Beijing.  
『跟妳換一百塊人民幣。』  
"Trade you for a 100 RMB note."  
「你想得美！」暖暖說。  
"Dream on!"  
『還好。』我笑了笑，『妳算術還不到無可救藥。』  
"At least," I laughed, "your math isn't completely hopeless."  
暖暖似乎對我手中的紅色鈔票感到好奇，我便遞給她。  
She seemed curious about the red bill in my hand, so I handed it to her.  
「這是孫中山嘛。」暖暖看了看後，說。  
"This is Sun Yat-sen," she said, looking at it.  
『妳也認得啊。』我說，『好厲害。』  
"You recognize him too?" I said. "Impressive."  
「誰不認得。」暖暖白了我一眼。  
"Who wouldn't?" She rolled her eyes at me.  

我看暖暖對台幣的興致很高，便又從皮夾掏出一張藍色千元鈔票遞給她。  
Seeing her interest, I pulled out a blue 1000-dollar bill and handed it over.  
「咋是小孩？」暖暖的表情顯得疑惑，「我以為會看到蔣介石呢。」  
"Why is there a kid on it?" She looked puzzled. "I thought it'd be Chiang Kai-shek."  
『以前確實是，前些年剛換。』  
"It used to be. They changed it a few years back."  
「我果然沒猜錯，你們應該會印上蔣介石……」暖暖突然停住不說。  
"I guessed right — I figured you'd have Chiang Kai-shek on your money…" She stopped abruptly.  
『怎麼了？』我問。  
"What's wrong?"  
「我直接叫蔣介石，你不介意嗎？」暖暖問。  
"Do you mind me saying Chiang Kai-shek directly?" she asked.  
『為什麼要介意？』我很好奇。  
"Why would I mind?" I was curious.  
「蔣——介——石。」暖暖一字一字說，「當真不介意？」  
"Chiang — Kai — shek." She said it syllable by syllable. "Really don't mind?"  
『當然不會啊。』我說，『妳叫他介石哥我才會介意。』  
"Of course not. I'd only mind if you called him *Brother Jieshi*."  
「你有毛病。」暖暖又瞪了我一眼。  
"There's something wrong with you." She shot me another look.  

我突然醒悟，這些天愉快而自然的相處，讓我們言語投機無話不談，  
It suddenly hit me — our easy, natural time together had made us forget there were certain differences between us,  
卻忘了彼此之間還存在著某些差異，甚至是禁忌。  
even taboos.  
『如果十年前妳直接叫蔣介石，也許我真會介意。但現在已經不會了。』  
"If you'd said Chiang Kai-shek directly ten years ago, I might have minded. But not anymore."  
「為什麼？」  
"Why?"  
『在台灣，蔣介石從神到寇最後到魔，也不過花了十多年時間。』  
"In Taiwan, Chiang Kai-shek went from god to bandit to demon in just over a decade."  
暖暖欲言又止，似乎也突然想起我們之間的禁忌，於是簡單笑了笑。  
She seemed about to speak, but stopped — as if she too had remembered the things between us — and simply smiled.  

暖暖應該不知道我說這些話時的心情。  
Nuannuan probably didn't understand how I felt saying those words.  
對我們這一代的台灣學生而言，我們曾經天真但那是因為熱情。  
For my generation of Taiwanese students, we were once naive, but that was out of passion.  
在某段期間堅信的真理與信仰，往往不到幾年就被輕易粉碎；  
The truths and beliefs we held firmly were often shattered within a few years;  
而重新建立起的價值觀，也不知道何時又會粉碎？  
and the values we rebuilt — who knew when they'd be shattered again?  
我們不是不相信歷史，只是不知道該相信誰？  
It wasn't that we didn't believe in history; it was that we didn't know who to believe.  
所以我們不再相信，也不再熱情。  
So we stopped believing. And we stopped being passionate.  
如果我說給暖暖聽，她大概無法理解吧？  
If I tried to explain this to Nuannuan, she probably wouldn't understand.  

我試著轉移話題，從口袋掏出一張紅色百元人民幣，上頭是毛澤東肖像。  
I tried to change the subject. I pulled out a red 100 RMB note from my pocket, bearing Mao Zedong's portrait.  
這是我在台灣先以台幣換成美金，到北京後再用美金換成的人民幣。  
This was money I'd first converted from Taiwan dollars to US dollars in Taiwan, then from US dollars to RMB in Beijing.  
我不想告訴暖暖這複雜的過程，指著手中三張鈔票說：  
I didn't want to explain this convoluted process. Pointing at the three notes in my hand, I said,  
『妳照樣把千元台幣當成蔣介石，把百元人民幣當成毛澤東、把百元台幣  
"You can think of the 1000-dollar bill as Chiang Kai-shek, the 100 RMB as Mao Zedong, and the 100-dollar Taiwan bill  
當成孫中山。所以一個蔣介石可以換兩個半毛澤東；一個毛澤東可以換  
as Sun Yat-sen. So one Chiang Kai-shek equals two and a half Mao Zedongs; one Mao Zedong equals  
四個孫中山。明白了嗎？』  
four Sun Yat-sens. Get it?"  
暖暖覺得好玩，便笑了笑、點點頭。  
She found this amusing and smiled, nodding.  

『對了。』我說，『我剛剛直接叫毛澤東，妳不介意嗎？』  
"Oh, by the way," I said. "I just said Mao Zedong directly. Do you mind?"  
「毛澤東一向跟群眾站在一起，直接叫名字有啥不對？」  
"Mao Zedong always stood with the masses. What's wrong with using his name directly?"  
『毛——澤——東。』我一字一字說，『當真不介意？』  
"Mao — Ze — Dong." I said it syllable by syllable. "Really don't mind?"  
「你挺無聊的。」  
"You're so boring."  
暖暖話才說完，隨即想起自己剛剛也有這種反應，便笑了起來。  
As soon as she said it, she remembered she'd just had the same reaction, and laughed.  

『從台灣飛到香港再飛到北京，我大約花了10個蔣介石。』我問暖暖，  
"Flying from Taiwan to Hong Kong to Beijing cost me about 10 Chiang Kai-sheks," I asked her.  
『請問這等於多少個孫中山？』  
"How many Sun Yat-sens is that?"  
「這簡單。」暖暖說，「100個孫中山。」  
"That's easy," she said. "100 Sun Yat-sens."  
『那等於多少個毛澤東？』我又問。  
"And how many Mao Zedongs?"  
「25個呀。」暖暖笑著說。  
"25!" She smiled.  
『接下來是深奧的問題。』我說，『如果我花了2個蔣介石、3個毛澤東、  
"Now for a deep question," I said. "If I spent 2 Chiang Kai-sheks, 3 Mao Zedongs, and  
4個孫中山，請問這等於多少個毛澤東？』  
4 Sun Yat-sens, how many Mao Zedongs is that?"  
「呀？」暖暖楞住了。  
"Huh?" She was stumped.  

我們走進瑞蚨祥，裡面陳列各式各樣綢緞布匹，令人眼花撩亂。  
We stepped into Ruifuxiang, which displayed a dazzling array of silks and fabrics.  
還有個製衣櫃台，客人挑選好布料，裁縫師傅便可以為他量身訂作衣服。  
There was also a tailoring counter — pick your fabric and a master tailor would take your measurements and make the garment.  
旗袍也可訂製，量完身選好布料，快一點的話隔天就可以交貨；  
Cheongsams could be custom-made too; quick orders could be ready the next day.  
如果是外地的觀光客，店家還會幫你把作好的旗袍送到飯店。  
For out-of-town tourists, they'd even deliver the finished dress to your hotel.  
「9個毛澤東！」暖暖突然說。  
"9 Mao Zedongs!" Nuannuan said suddenly.  
我嚇了一跳，店內的人似乎也嚇了一跳，紛紛投射過來異樣的眼光。  
I jumped; people in the shop jumped too, casting odd looks our way.  
「這是剛剛問題的答案。」暖暖有些不好意思，降低了音量。  
"That's the answer to your question." She looked a bit embarrassed and lowered her voice.  

離開瑞蚨祥，走進內聯陞，看見「中國布鞋第一家」的匾額。  
Leaving Ruifuxiang, we went into Neiliansheng, where a plaque proclaimed "China's First Cloth Shoe Store."  
『暖暖，妳的腳借我試試。』我說。  
"Nuannuan, let me borrow your foot for a try," I said.  
「想給愛人買鞋？」  
"Buying shoes for your sweetheart?"  
『我沒愛人。』我說。  
"I don't have a sweetheart," I said.  
暖暖笑了笑，彎下身解鞋帶。  
She smiled and bent down to untie her laces.  
『不過女朋友倒有好幾個，得買好幾雙。』我又說。  
"But I do have several girlfriends — need to buy a few pairs," I added.  
暖暖手一停，然後把鞋帶繫上，站起身。  
Her hands stopped; she retied her laces and stood up.  
『開玩笑的。』我趕緊笑了笑，『我想買鞋給我媽。』  
"Just kidding." I laughed quickly. "I want to buy shoes for my mom."  
暖暖瞪我一眼，又彎身解鞋帶。  
She shot me a look, then bent down and untied her laces again.  

「你知道你媽腳的尺寸嗎？」暖暖問。  
"Do you know your mom's shoe size?" she asked.  
『大概知道。』  
"More or less."  
「當真？」  
"Really?"  
『小時候常挨打，我總是跪在地上抱著我媽小腿哭喊：媽，我錯了！』  
"When I was little and got spanked, I'd kneel on the ground, hug my mom's leg, and cry: *Mom, I was wrong!*"  
我笑著說：『看得久了，她腳的尺寸便深印在腦海。』  
I laughed. "After seeing her feet that many times, the size got burned into my brain."  
「淨瞎說。」暖暖也笑了。  
"Pure nonsense." She laughed too.  

暖暖幫我挑了雙手工納底的布鞋，黑色鞋面上繡著幾朵紅色小花。  
Nuannuan helped me pick a pair of hand-stitched cloth shoes — black uppers embroidered with little red flowers.  
這是特價品，賣88塊人民幣，我拿了張紅色百元人民幣，把暖暖叫來。  
They were on sale for 88 RMB. I took out a red 100 RMB bill and called Nuannuan over.  
『來，我們一起跟毛主席說聲再見。』我說。  
"Come on, let's say goodbye to Chairman Mao together."  
暖暖不想理我，便走開。  
She ignored me and walked away.  
店員找給我一張十元人民幣和兩個一元硬幣。  
The clerk gave me change: a 10 RMB bill and two 1 RMB coins.  
『妳看。』我走到暖暖身邊，指著十元人民幣上的毛澤東肖像，說：  
"Look." I went over to her and pointed at Mao's portrait on the 10 RMB note.  
『毛主席捨不得我們，換件衣服後又回來了。』  
"Chairman Mao couldn't bear to leave us — he just changed his outfit and came back."  
「北七。」暖暖說。  
"*Bei qi*," she said.  
『罵得好。』我說，『這句就是這樣用。』  
"Good one," I said. "That's how you use it."  

走出內聯陞，暖暖說她要去買個東西，十分鐘後回來碰頭，說完就跑掉。  
We stepped out of Neiliansheng. Nuannuan said she needed to buy something and would be back in ten minutes; she ran off.  
等不到五分鐘，我便覺得無聊，買了根棒棒糖，蹲在牆角畫圈圈。  
Not even five minutes passed before I got bored. I bought a lollipop, squatted by the wall, and drew circles on the ground.  
「買好了。」暖暖又跑回來，問：「你在作啥？」  
"All done." She ran back. "What are you doing?"  
『我在扮演被媽媽遺棄的小孩。』我站起身。  
"I'm playing the part of a child abandoned by his mother." I stood up.  
「真丟人。」暖暖說。  
"So embarrassing."  
『妳買了什麼？』我問。  
"What did you buy?"  
「過幾天你就知道了。」暖暖賣了個關子。  
"You'll find out in a few days." She kept it a mystery.  

大柵欄步行街從東到西不到三百公尺，但我和暖暖還是逛到兩腿發酸。  
The Da Zha Lan pedestrian street was less than 300 meters from east to west, but Nuannuan and I walked until our legs ached.  
剛好同仁堂前有可供坐著的地方，我們便坐下歇歇腿。  
There happened to be a place to sit in front of Tongrentang, so we sat down to rest.  
『這裡真好，可以讓人坐著。』我說，  
"This is nice — they let you sit here," I said.  
『如果天氣熱逛到中暑，就直接進裡頭看醫生抓藥。』  
"If you get heatstroke from shopping in this weather, you can just go straight in to see a doctor."  
「是呀。」暖暖擦擦汗，遞了瓶酸奶給我。  
"Yeah." She wiped her sweat and handed me a yogurt drink.  
我發覺夏天的北京好像缺少不了冰涼的酸奶。  
I'd realized that summer in Beijing couldn't do without cold yogurt drinks.  

「常在報上看見大柵欄的新聞，今天倒是第一次來逛。」暖暖說。  
"I often see Da Zha Lan in the news, but today's my first time visiting," she said.  
『都是些什麼樣的新聞？』我問。  
"What kind of news?"  
「大概都是關於百年老店的介紹，偶爾會有拆除改建的消息。」  
"Usually features on the century-old shops; occasionally news about demolitions and redevelopment."  
『真會拆嗎？』  
"Will they really tear it down?"  
「應該會改建。但改建後京味兒還在不在，就不得而知了。」暖暖說，  
"They'll probably renovate. But whether the Beijing flavor survives the renovation is anyone's guess."  
「這年頭，純粹的東西總是死得太快。」  
"These days, pure things always die too fast."  

暖暖看了看夕陽，過一會又說：  
She looked at the setting sun, then said after a moment:  
「夕陽下女孩在大柵欄裡喝酸奶的背影，興許以後再也見不著了。」  
"The silhouette of a girl drinking yogurt in Da Zha Lan at sunset — maybe you won't see this again in the future."  
『但妳的精神卻永遠長存。』我說。  
"But your spirit will live forever," I said.  
「說啥呀。」暖暖笑出聲。  
"What are you talking about?" She laughed.  

時間差不多了，大夥慢慢往東邊前門大街口聚集。  
Time was about up. Everyone gathered slowly at the Qianmen Street intersection to the east.  
我看見對面「全聚德」的招牌，興奮地對暖暖說：『是全聚德耶！』  
I spotted the Quanjude sign across the street and said excitedly, "It's Quanjude!"  
「想吃烤鴨嗎？」暖暖說。  
"Want roast duck?"  
『嗯。』我點點頭，『今天好像有免費招待。』  
"Mm." I nodded. "Looks like they're giving out free samples today."  
「是嗎？」暖暖嚇了一跳，「咋可能呢？」  
"Really?" She was surprised. "How's that possible?"  
『我剛看到店門口擺了些板凳，應該是免費招待看人吃烤鴨。』  
"I saw some stools by the door — must be free seating for watching people eat roast duck."  
「你……」暖暖接不下話，索性轉過身不理我。  
"You—" She was speechless and just turned away.  
我雙眼還是緊盯著對面的全聚德烤鴨店。  
I kept my eyes fixed on the Quanjude roast duck restaurant across the street.  

「涼涼。」暖暖說，「想吃的話，下次你來北京我請你吃。」  
"Liangliang," she said. "If you want to eat it, next time you come to Beijing, I'll treat you."  
『這是風中的承諾嗎？』  
"Is this a promise in the wind?"  
「嗯？」  
"Huh?"  
『風起時不能下承諾，這樣承諾會隨風而逝的。』  
"When the wind rises, you can't make a promise — it'll just blow away with the wind."  
「我才不像你呢。」暖暖說，「我說要去暖暖，你連像樣的承諾也沒。」  
"I'm not like you," she said. "I said I want to go to Nuannuan, and you haven't even given me a proper promise."  
『車來了。』我說。  
"The bus is here," I said.  
「又耍賴。」暖暖輕輕哼了一聲。  
"Running away again." She gave a soft snort.  

回到學校吃完飯，大夥又聚在教室裡展示今天的戰利品。  
Back at school after dinner, everyone gathered in the classroom to show off today's haul.  
今天的戰利品特別豐富，看來很多同學的荷包都在大柵欄裡大失血。  
Today's spoils were especially rich — it seemed many wallets had suffered major casualties at Da Zha Lan.  
徐馳讓我看他在大柵欄拍的照片，有一張是我和暖暖並肩喝酸奶的背影。  
Xu Chi showed me photos he'd taken at Da Zha Lan. One was of Nuannuan and me side by side, backs to the camera, drinking yogurt.  
想起暖暖那時說的話：「這年頭，純粹的東西總是死得太快。」  
I remembered what she'd said: "These days, pure things always die too fast."  
不知道下次來北京時（如果還有下次的話），哪些純粹會先死去？  
I wondered — next time I came to Beijing, if there was a next time — which pure things would die first?  
又有哪些純粹依然很純粹呢？  
And which would still be pure?  

躺在床上閉上眼睛，隱約聽到一些聲音。  
I lay in bed, closed my eyes, and faintly heard some sounds.  
大概是受天壇回音壁的影響，暖暖的笑聲一直在心裡反射。  
Probably the influence of the Temple of Heaven's Echo Wall — Nuannuan's laughter kept echoing in my heart.
