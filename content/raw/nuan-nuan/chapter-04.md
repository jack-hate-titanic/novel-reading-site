# 《暖暖》第四章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

隔天起床，我從上鋪一躍而下，這是我從大學時代養成的習慣。  
The next morning I jumped down from the top bunk, a habit I'd kept since college.  
一方面可迅速清醒，以便趕得及上第一堂課；  
On one hand, it wakes you up fast so you make it to the first class on time;  
另一方面，萬一降落不成功，也會有充足的理由不去上課。  
on the other hand, if the landing doesn't go well, you've got plenty of reason to skip class.  
但今天雖降落成功，雙腳卻有一股濃烈的酸意。  
Today the landing was successful, but my legs were heavy with a deep, sour ache.  
腿好酸啊，我幾乎直不起身。  
My legs were so sore I could barely stand straight.  

幸好刷牙洗臉和吃早飯不必用到腳，但走到教室的路程就有些漫長了。  
Luckily, brushing my teeth, washing my face, and eating breakfast don't require legs — but the walk to the classroom was a long one.  
「給。」一走進教室，暖暖便遞了瓶東西給我。  
"Here." The moment I stepped into the room, Nuannuan handed me a bottle.  
我拿在手上仔細端詳，是雲南白藥噴劑。  
I looked at it carefully — Yunnan Baiyao spray.  
「挺有效的。」她又說。  
"It works pretty well," she said.  
捲起褲管，在左右小腿肚各噴三下，感覺很清涼，酸痛似乎也有些緩解。  
I rolled up my pant legs and gave each calf three sprays. It felt cool, and the soreness eased a little.  
我沉思幾秒後，立刻站起身跑出教室。  
After a few seconds of thought, I jumped up and bolted out of the classroom.  
「你去哪？」暖暖的聲音在身後響起，「要上課了。」  
"Where are you going?" Nuannuan called after me. "Class is starting."  
『大腿也得噴啊。』我頭也不回說。  
"My thighs need spraying too," I said without turning back.  

「真是。」我從廁所回來後，暖暖一看見我就說。  
"*Really*," Nuannuan said the moment I came back from the toilet.  
真是什麼？難道我可以在教室裡脫下褲子噴大腿嗎？  
*Really* what? Was I supposed to drop my pants in class to spray my thighs?  
今天聽說上課的是個大學教授，要上漢語的語言特色。  
Today's lecturer, we'd heard, was a university professor speaking on the features of the Chinese language.  
本以為應該是個老學究，這種人通常會兼具魔術師和催眠師的身份。  
I'd assumed he'd be one of those old pedants — types who double as magician and hypnotist.  
也就是說，會是個讓桌子有一股吸力，吸引你的臉貼住桌子的魔術師；  
A magician, in the sense that he gives the desk a mysterious pull that draws your face down onto it;  
也會是個講話的語調彷彿叫你睡吧睡吧的催眠師。  
a hypnotist, in the sense that his voice murmurs *sleep, sleep* in your ear.  
不過這位教授雖然六十多歲了，講話卻詼諧有趣，口吻輕鬆而不嚴肅。  
But this professor, though in his sixties, was witty and engaging — his tone light, not stern.  

因為我們這群學生來自不同科系，所以他並不講深奧的理論。  
Because we came from a mix of departments, he didn't dive into deep theory.  
他說中文一字一音，排列組合性強，句子斷法不同，意義也不同。  
Chinese, he said, is one syllable per character, highly combinatorial; cut a sentence differently and it means something different.  
甚至常見順著唸也行、倒著唸也可以的句子。  
You even see sentences that work read forward *or* backward.  
比方說「吃青菜的小孩不會變壞」這句，經排列組合後，可以變成：  
For instance, *A child who eats vegetables won't go bad*, rearranged, can become:  
「變壞的青菜小孩不會吃」、「變壞的小孩不會吃青菜」，各有意義。  
*Spoiled vegetables — children won't eat them*, or *Bad children won't eat their vegetables*, each with its own meaning.  
還可變成「吃小孩的青菜不會變壞」，不過這句只能出現在恐怖電影裡。  
You can even get *Vegetables that eat children won't spoil*, though that one only belongs in a horror film.  

英文有時式，是因為重視時間，所以是科學式語言；  
English has tenses because it values time — it's a scientific language;  
中文沒有時式，所以中國人不注重時間，沒有時間觀念。  
Chinese has none, so the Chinese pay no attention to time, lack a sense of time.  
「這是鬼扯。一個動詞三種文字，那叫沒事找事做。加個表示過去的時間  
"That's hogwash. Three forms of one verb? That's making work for the sake of it. Just add a word indicating past time  
不就得了，何苦執著分別。人生該學的事特多，別讓動詞給罣礙了。」  
and you're done — why be so obsessed with the distinction? Life has plenty worth learning; don't let verbs hold you back."  
他微微一笑，「這就是佛。」  
He smiled. "That, by the way, is Buddhism."  
英文說a book、a desk、a car、a tree、a man等都只是「a」，簡單；  
In English, *a book*, *a desk*, *a car*, *a tree*, *a man* — all just *a*. Simple.  
中文卻有一棵、一粒、一張、一個、一本、一輛、一件等說法，很麻煩。  
In Chinese you have *yi ke*, *yi li*, *yi zhang*, *yi ge*, *yi ben*, *yi liang*, *yi jian* — a whole pile of measure words. A nuisance.  
「那是因為中國人知道萬事與萬物都有獨特性，所以計量單位不同，表達  
"That's because the Chinese know every thing and every event is unique, so we use different measure words to express  
一種尊重。」他哈哈大笑，「這就是道啊。」  
respect." He laughed heartily. "*That*, by the way, is Daoism."  

中文的生命力很強，一個字可有多種意義跟詞性，特有彈性。  
Chinese is full of vitality — a single character can carry many meanings and parts of speech; it's deeply elastic.  
「哪位同學可舉個例？舉的有特色，我親手寫『才子』送你。」  
"Can anyone give me an example? If it's a good one, I'll write *caizi* — *prodigy* — by hand and give it to you."  
老師開玩笑說：「上網拍賣，大概還值幾個錢。」  
"Sell it online," he joked, "and you'll probably make a few bucks."  
「這老師的毛筆字寫得特好。」暖暖偷偷告訴我，「涼涼，試試？」  
"His brush calligraphy is really excellent," Nuannuan whispered. "Liangliang, give it a shot?"  
我朝暖暖搖搖頭。  
I shook my head.  
我是個低調的人，難道我才高八斗也要讓大家都知道嗎？  
I'm a low-key sort. Just because I have eight bushels of talent doesn't mean everyone has to know.  

學弟忽然舉手，我嚇一大跳，心想這小子瘋了。  
My junior suddenly raised his hand. I jolted — *the kid's lost his mind*.  
只見老師點點頭說：「請。」  
The professor nodded. "Go ahead."  
「床前明月光，美女來賞光；衣服脫光光，共度好時光。」  
"*Bright moonlight before the bed* — *guang*; *a beauty comes to grace us* — *guang*; *clothes stripped bare* — *guangguang*; *spending lovely times together* — *shiguang*."  
學弟起身說，「這四個『光』字，意義都不同。」  
He stood up. "These four *guangs* all mean different things."  

「這位同學是台灣來的？」老師問。  
"This student is from Taiwan?" the professor asked.  
「嗯。」學弟點點頭。  
"Mm." My junior nodded.  
「真有勇氣。」老師又哈哈大笑，「英雄出少年。」  
"What bravery." The professor laughed again. "Heroes really do come from the young."  
恥辱啊，真是恥辱。我抬不起頭了。  
A disgrace. A real disgrace. I couldn't lift my head.  
「老師待會是寫『才子』還是寫『英雄出少年』給我？」學弟小聲問我。  
"You think he'll write *caizi* for me, or *heroes from the young*?" my junior whispered.  
『你給我閉嘴。』我咬著牙說。  
"Shut your mouth," I said through gritted teeth.  

老師接著讓台灣學生和北京學生談談彼此說話的差異。  
Next the professor had Taiwanese and Beijing students compare differences in how they speak.  
有人說，台灣學生說話溫文儒雅，語調高低起伏小，經常帶有感嘆詞；  
Someone said Taiwanese students speak gently and refined, with little rise and fall, often peppered with sentence-final particles;  
北京學生說話豪氣，語調高亢、起伏明顯，用字也較精簡。  
Beijing students speak with bold energy, pitched high, with marked rises and falls, in fewer words.  
例如台灣學生說「你真的好漂亮喔！」，北京學生則說「你真漂亮」。  
For example, a Taiwanese student says, *"You're really super pretty, you know!"*; a Beijing student says, *"You're really pretty."*  
人家說謝謝，台灣學生說不客氣；人家說對不起，台灣學生說沒關係。  
When someone says *thank you*, a Taiwanese student says *you're welcome*; when someone says *sorry*, *no worries*.  
語調總是細而緩，拉平成線。  
Always soft and slow, flattened into a line.  
而不管人家說謝謝還是對不起，北京學生都說「沒事」。  
Whether thanked or apologized to, a Beijing student just says, *"No problem"* — *meishi*.  
語尾上揚且短促，頗有豪邁之感。  
The ending lifts and snaps off — there's a swagger to it.  

「咱們做個試驗來玩玩。」學生們七嘴八舌說完後，老師說。  
"Let's run a little experiment for fun," the professor said once the students had finished chiming in.  
老師假設一個情況：你要坐飛機到北京，想去逛故宮和爬長城，  
He set up a scenario: you're flying to Beijing, plan to tour the Forbidden City and climb the Wall;  
出門前跟媽媽說坐幾點飛機、幾點到北京、到北京後會打電話報平安。  
before leaving, you tell your mother what time your flight is, when you'll arrive, and that you'll call when you get there.  
大夥輪流用自然輕鬆的方式說完，每個細節都一樣。  
We took turns saying it naturally, all with the same details.  
結果發現這段約50個字的敘述中，有些說法上有差異。  
And in this roughly fifty-character speech, certain phrasings differed.  
例如台灣學生最後說「我會打電話回家」；  
A Taiwanese student would end with *"I'll make a phone call back home"*;  
北京學生則說「會給家裡打電話」。  
a Beijing student would say *"I'll give home a call."*  

「現在用手指頭數數你剛剛共說了幾個字？」老師說。  
"Now count on your fingers — how many characters did you say?" the professor said.  
經過計算平均後，台灣學生說了52.4個字；北京學生說了48.6個字。  
The averages: Taiwanese students used 52.4 characters; Beijing students, 48.6.  
為了客觀起見，老師又舉了三種情況，結果也類似：  
For fairness he tried three more scenarios, with similar results:  
在一段約50個字的敘述中，台灣學生平均多用了三至四個字。  
Across a stretch of about fifty characters, the Taiwanese consistently used three to four more.  
我不太服氣，跟暖暖說：『快到教室外面來。妳怎麼說？』  
Unconvinced, I said to Nuannuan, "How would you say *Quickly come outside the classroom*?"  
「快來教室外頭。」暖暖說。  
"*Kuai lai jiaoshi waitou.*"  
屈指一算，她比我少用一個字。  
I counted on my fingers — one character fewer than mine.  

『這件衣服不錯。』我說。  
"*Zhe jian yifu bucuo* — this piece of clothing is decent."  
「這衣服挺好。」暖暖回答。  
"*Zhe yifu ting hao.*"  
『這件衣服太好了。』  
"*Zhe jian yifu tai hao le.*"  
「這衣服特好。」  
"*Zhe yifu te hao.*"  
『這件衣服實在太棒了。』  
"*Zhe jian yifu shizai tai bang le.*"  
「這衣服特特好。」暖暖笑著說，「我用的字還是比你少。」  
"*Zhe yifu te-te hao.*" Nuannuan laughed. "Still fewer characters than you."  
『妳賴皮。哪有人說特特好。』  
"You cheated. Nobody says *te-te hao*."  
「在北京就這麼說。」暖暖嘿嘿笑了兩聲。  
"In Beijing we do." She gave a couple of smug little laughs.  

老師最後以武俠小說為例，結束今天上午的課程。  
To wrap up the morning, the professor used wuxia novels as an example.  
在武俠小說中，北京大俠一進客棧，便喊：拿酒來！  
In a wuxia novel, the Beijing-style hero enters the inn and shouts: *Bring the wine!*  
台灣大俠則會說：小二，給我一壺酒。  
The Taiwanese hero says: *Waiter, give me a pot of wine.*  
看出差別了嗎？  
See the difference?  
台灣大俠通常不會忽略句子中的主詞與受詞，也就是「我」與「小二」；  
The Taiwanese hero doesn't drop subject or object — *me* and *waiter*;  
而且計量單位也很明確，到底是一壺酒還是一罈酒？必須區別。  
and he specifies the measure: is it a pot of wine or a jar? It must be distinguished.  

北京大俠則簡單多了，管你是小二、小三還是掌櫃，拿酒來便是。  
The Beijing hero is far simpler. Whether you're waiter, clerk, or innkeeper, just *bring the wine*.  
酒這東西不會因為不同的人拿而有所差異。  
Wine doesn't change depending on who carries it.  
因為是我說話，當然拿給我，難不成叫你拿去澆花？  
I'm the one talking, of course it's for me — would I be telling you to water the flowers with it?  
至於計量單位，甭管用壺、罈、罐、盅、瓶、杯、碗、臉盆或痰盂裝，  
As for measure: pot, jar, jug, cup, bottle, glass, bowl, washbasin, or spittoon — whatever holds it,  
俺只管喝酒。  
I'm just here to drink.  
武功若練到最高境界，北京大俠會只說：「酒！」  
At the peak of his skill, the Beijing hero says only: *"Wine!"*  
而台灣大俠若練到最高境界，大概還是會說：「來壺酒。」  
At his peak, the Taiwanese hero would probably still say: *"Bring me a pot of wine."*  

當然也因為這樣，所以台灣大俠特別受到客棧歡迎。  
This is why innkeepers especially welcome Taiwanese heroes.  
因為台灣大俠的指令明確，不易讓人出錯。  
The Taiwanese hero's order is clear; mistakes are unlikely.  
北京大俠只說拿酒，但若小二拿一大罈酒給北京大俠，你猜怎麼著？  
The Beijing hero says only *bring wine*; what if the waiter brings a huge jar?  
「混帳東西！」北京大俠怒吼，「你想撐死人不償命？」  
*"Wretch!"* he roars. *"Trying to burst me and get away with murder?"*  
這時小二嘴裡肯定媽的王八羔子您老又沒說拿多少，直犯嘀咕。  
The waiter, *damn-it-all-you-old-bastard-you-didn't-say-how-much*, mutters under his breath.  
「造反了嗎？」北京大俠咻的一聲拔出腰刀。  
*"Mutiny?"* The hero whips out his sabre with a *swish*.  
所以武俠小說中客棧發生打鬥場面的，通常在北方。  
That's why inn-fight scenes in wuxia novels usually happen up north.  
自古燕趙多慷慨悲歌之士，常為了喝酒而打架，這還能不悲嗎？  
Yan and Zhao have long been lands of stout-hearted, sorrowful singers; they fight over wine — how could it not be tragic?  

「那台灣的客棧呢？」有個同學問。  
"What about Taiwanese inns?" a student asked.  
「台灣客棧當然愛情故事多。」老師笑了笑，「君不見台灣客棧拿酒的，  
"Taiwanese inns are all about romance," the professor said with a smile. "Haven't you noticed — the one bringing the wine in a Taiwanese inn  
通常是小姑娘。」  
is usually a young girl."  
老師說完後，笑得很曖昧。隨即收起笑容，拍了拍手。  
He gave an ambiguous smile, then put it away and clapped his hands.  
「不瞎扯了，咱們明早再上文字的部分。」老師說，「你們趕緊吃完飯，  
"Enough nonsense. We'll do the writing system tomorrow morning," he said. "Eat up,  
飯後去逛胡同。」  
because after lunch you're touring the *hutongs*."  

在學校食堂裡簡單用過午飯，大夥上車直達鼓樓，登樓可以俯瞰北京城。  
After a simple lunch at the canteen, the bus took us straight to the Drum Tower; from there you can look out over Beijing.  
登上鼓樓俯瞰北京舊城區和錯綜複雜的胡同，視野很好。  
Climbing the Drum Tower for a view over old Beijing and its tangle of alleys, the perspective was excellent.  
「咱們先到什剎海附近晃晃，感受一下。」下了鼓樓，北京李老師說：  
"Let's wander around Shichahai first, get a feel for it," Teacher Li said when we got back down.  
「待會坐三輪車逛胡同，別再用走的。」  
"After that we'll take pedicabs around the *hutongs* — no more walking."  
他一說完，全場歡聲雷動。  
At the word "pedicab," cheers exploded.  

我和暖暖來到什剎海前海與後海交接處的銀錠橋，這是座單孔石拱橋。  
Nuannuan and I came to the Yinding Bridge — Silver Ingot Bridge — where Front Sea meets Back Sea at Shichahai. A single-arch stone bridge.  
橋的長度不到十公尺，寬度約八公尺，橋下還有小船划過橋孔。  
Less than ten meters long, about eight wide, with little boats slipping through underneath.  
從銀錠橋往後海方向走，湖畔綠樹成蔭，萬綠叢中點綴幾處樓閣古剎。  
Walking from the bridge toward Back Sea, the lakeside was thick with green trees, the mass of foliage dotted with old pavilions and temples.  
湖平如鏡，遠處西山若隱若現，幾艘小船悠遊其中，像一幅山水畫卷。  
The lake was glass-smooth; the Western Hills loomed and faded in the distance; a few boats drifted across — like a scroll painting.  
我和暖暖沿著湖畔綠蔭行走，雖處盛夏，亦感清涼。  
We walked along the green-shaded shore; even in midsummer it felt cool.  

暖暖買了兩瓶酸奶，給我一瓶，我們席地而坐，望著湖面。  
Nuannuan bought two bottles of yogurt drink, handed me one, and we sat down on the ground watching the water.  
時間流動的速度似乎變慢了，幾近停止。  
Time seemed to flow more slowly, almost stopped.  
我喝了一口酸奶，味道不錯，感覺像台灣的優酪乳。  
I sipped the yogurt — not bad, much like Taiwanese drinking yogurt.  
「我在這兒滑過冰。」過了一會，暖暖說。  
"I've ice-skated here," Nuannuan said after a while.  
『滑冰？』眼前盡是碧綠的水，我不禁納悶：『滑冰場在哪？』  
"Skated?" All I could see was emerald water. "Where's the rink?"  
「冬天一到，湖面結冰，不就是個天然滑冰場？」暖暖笑了笑。  
"Once winter comes, the lake freezes — that's a natural rink, isn't it?" she said with a smile.  
『果然是夏蟲不可語冰。』我說，『對長在台灣的我而言，很難想像。』  
"Truly, you can't speak of ice to a summer insect," I said. "For someone who grew up in Taiwan, it's hard to picture."  

「你會滑冰嗎？」暖暖問。  
"Can you skate?"  
『我只會吃冰，不會滑冰。』我笑了笑，『連滑冰場都沒見過。』  
"I only eat ice — I don't skate," I laughed. "I've never even seen a rink."  
「有機會到我老家來，我教你滑。」  
"If you ever come to my hometown, I'll teach you."  
『好啊。妳得牽著我的手，然後說你好棒、你是天才的那種教法喔。』  
"Sure. But it has to be the kind of teaching where you hold my hand and say *you're amazing, you're a genius*."  
「想得美。我會推你下去不理你，又在旁罵你笨，這樣你很快就會了。」  
"Dream on. I'd shove you out, ignore you, and yell that you're stupid — that's how you'd learn fastest."  
『如果是這樣，那我就不學了。』  
"In that case, I'm not learning."  
「不成。你得學。」  
"No way. You have to."  
『為什麼？』  
"Why?"  
「我想看你摔。」暖暖說完後，笑個不停。  
"I want to watch you fall." She broke into laughter that wouldn't stop.  
『妳這人賊壞。』我說。  
"You're really *zei* mean."  
「這形容就貼切了。」暖暖還是笑著。  
"*That* description fits," she said, still laughing.  

我們又起身隨興漫步，在這裡散步真的很舒服。  
We got up and walked on. Strolling there really was lovely.  
「我待在北京五個冬天了，每年冬天都會到這兒滑冰。」暖暖開了口。  
"I've spent five winters in Beijing now, and each one I come here to skate," she said.  
『妳大學畢業了？』我問。  
"You've graduated already?"  
「嗯。」暖暖點點頭，『要升研二了，明年這時候就開始工作了。』  
"Mm." She nodded. "Going into my second year of grad school. About this time next year I'll be working."  
『在北京工作？還是回老家？』  
"Working in Beijing? Or back home?"  
「應該還是留在北京工作。」暖暖彷彿嘆了口氣，說：  
"Probably staying in Beijing." She seemed to sigh. "The longer you're away from home,  
「離家的時間越久，家的距離就更遠了。」  
the farther home gets."  

『如果妳在北京工作，我就來北京找妳。』我說。  
"If you're working in Beijing, I'll come visit," I said.  
「你說真格的嗎？」暖暖眼睛一亮。  
"You serious?" Her eyes lit up.  
『嗯。』我點點頭。  
"Mm." I nodded.  
「這太好了，北京還有很多好玩的東西呢，得讓你瞧瞧。」暖暖很興奮，  
"That'd be great. There's still loads of fun stuff to show you in Beijing." She was excited.  
「最好我們還可以再去吃些川菜渝菜之類的，把你辣暈，那肯定好玩。」  
"And we can go for more Sichuan or Yu food, knock you out with the heat — that'll be a riot."  
『如果是那樣，我馬上逃回台灣。』  
"If so, I'm fleeing back to Taiwan right then."  
「不成，我偏不讓你走。」  
"No way, I'm not letting you go."  

暖暖笑得很開心，剛剛從她眼前飄過的一絲鄉愁，瞬間消失無蹤。  
She laughed brightly; the trace of homesickness that had drifted across her face vanished.  
我心裡則想著下次在北京重逢，不知道會是什麼樣？  
But I was wondering what reunion in Beijing next time would feel like.  
也不知道是什麼時候？  
And when, exactly.  
而那時候的我們，還能像現在一樣單純嗎？  
And if, by then, we'd still be as simple as we were now.  

「嘿，如果我在老家工作，你就不來找我了嗎？」暖暖突然開口。  
"Hey — if I worked back home, you wouldn't come to see me?" Nuannuan said abruptly.  
『我不知道黑龍江是什麼樣的地方。』我想了一下，接著說：  
"I don't know what Heilongjiang is like." I thought for a moment.  
『也許要翻過好幾座雪山、跨過好幾條冰封的大江，搞不好走了半個多月  
"Maybe you'd have to cross snow-mountain after snow-mountain, frozen river after frozen river — you'd walk for half a month  
才看到一個人，而且那人還不會講普通話。重點是我不會打獵，不知道  
before seeing a single person, and he wouldn't speak Mandarin. The bigger problem is I can't hunt;  
該如何填飽肚子。』  
I'd starve."  
「瞧你把黑龍江想成什麼樣。」暖暖說，「黑龍江也挺進步的。」  
"Look at how you're imagining Heilongjiang," she said. "It's pretty developed, you know."  
看來我對黑龍江的印象，恐怕停留在清末，搞不好還更早。  
Apparently my image of Heilongjiang was stuck in the late Qing — probably earlier.  
「如果黑龍江真是你形容的這樣，那你還來嗎？」  
"If Heilongjiang really were like you describe — would you still come?"  
暖暖停下腳步，轉身面對著我。  
She stopped and turned to face me.  

『暖暖。』我也停下腳步。  
"Nuannuan." I stopped too.  
「嗯？」  
"Mm?"  
『我會耶。』我笑了笑。  
"Yeah, I would," I said with a smile.  
暖暖也笑了，笑容很燦爛，像冬天的太陽，明亮而溫暖。  
She smiled back — a brilliant smile, like winter sun: bright and warm.  
我天真地相信，為了看一眼暖暖燦爛的笑容，西伯利亞我也會去。  
I naively believed I'd go to Siberia for one glimpse of that smile.  

『不過妳得先教我打獵。』我說。  
"But you'll have to teach me to hunt first," I said.  
「才不呢。」暖暖說，「最好讓黑熊咬死你。」  
"Not a chance," she said. "Better to let a black bear eat you."  
『碰到黑熊就裝死啊，反正裝死我很在行。』  
"I'll just play dead. Playing dead is my specialty."  
「還有東北虎呢。」  
"There are Manchurian tigers, too."  
『嗯……』我說，『我還是不去好了。』  
"Hmm…" I said. "Maybe I shouldn't come."  
「不成，你剛答應要來的。」  
"No way, you just promised."  
『隨便說說不犯法吧。』  
"Saying something casually isn't a crime."  
「喂。」  
"Hey!"  

『好。我去。』我說，『萬一碰到東北虎，就跟牠曉以大義。』  
"Fine. I'll come." I said. "If I run into a Manchurian tiger, I'll appeal to its sense of righteousness."  
「東北虎可聽不懂人話。」  
"Manchurian tigers don't understand human speech."  
『為了見妳一面，我千里迢迢、跋山涉水，應該會感動老天。老天都深受  
"Crossing thousands of miles, fording mountains and rivers, just to see you — heaven itself would be moved. If heaven is moved,  
感動了，更何況東北虎。也許牠還會含著感動的淚水幫我指引方向。』  
how could a tiger fail to be? It might even guide me in the right direction with tears in its eyes."  
「那是因為牠餓慌了，突然看見大餐送上門，才會感動得流淚。」  
"That's because it's starving, and seeing a feast walk up to it would naturally make it weep."  
暖暖邊說邊笑，我覺得有趣，也跟著笑。  
She laughed as she spoke; I found it funny too and laughed along.  

我和暖暖一路說說笑笑，又走回銀錠橋。  
Talking and laughing, we made our way back to the Silver Ingot Bridge.  
李老師已經找好20多輛人力三輪車，每兩個學生一輛。  
Teacher Li had already lined up over twenty pedicabs, two students to each.  
他讓學生們先上車，然後一輛一輛交代事情，不知道說些什麼。  
He had us all board, then went around cab by cab giving instructions — what, I couldn't hear.  
他一來到我和暖暖坐的三輪車，先稱呼三輪車夫為板爺兒，然後交代：  
When he reached the cab Nuannuan and I were in, he addressed our driver as *banr-ye* — *Master Driver* — and said:  
終點是恭王府，沿路上如果我們喜歡可隨時下車走走，但別太久。  
The destination is Prince Gong's Mansion. Feel free to get off and walk along the way, but don't take too long.  
「慢慢逛，放鬆心情溜達溜達。」李老師對我們微微一笑。  
"Take your time, relax, just stroll," he smiled.  

三輪車剛起動，暖暖便說她來北京這麼久，坐三輪車逛胡同還是頭一遭。  
As the pedicab started up, Nuannuan said that despite all her time in Beijing, this was her first time touring the *hutongs* by pedicab.  
『跟大姑娘坐花轎一樣。』我說。  
"Like a maiden in a bridal sedan chair."  
「啥？」  
"What?"  
『都叫頭一遭。』  
"Both first-time experiences."  
「你挺無聊的。」暖暖瞪了我一眼。  
"You're being silly." She glared.  

「爺，聽您的口音，您是南方人？」板爺突然開口。  
"*Sir,* by your accent, you're from the south?" the driver asked suddenly.  
『請叫我小兄弟就好。』聽他叫爺，我實在受不起，『我是台灣來的。』  
"Just call me *little brother*," I said — *sir* was too much for me. "I'm from Taiwan."  
「難怪。」板爺說，「你們台灣來的特有禮貌、人都挺好。」  
"No wonder," he said. "You Taiwanese are especially polite, all really nice people."  
我靦腆笑了笑，然後轉頭跟暖暖說：『嘿，人家說我很有禮貌耶。』  
I smiled bashfully and turned to Nuannuan. "Hey, he says I'm polite."  
「那是客套。」暖暖淡淡地說。  
"He's just being courteous," she said dryly.  
「小姑娘，俺從不客套。」板爺笑了笑。  
"*Young miss*, I never just do courtesy," the driver said with a smile.  
『聽見沒？小姑娘。』我很得意。  
"Hear that — *young miss*?" I crowed.  
沒想到我是爺，暖暖只是小姑娘，一下子差了兩個輩份，這讓我很得意。  
I was a *sir* and she was just a *young miss* — two generations between us. I felt smug.  

「爺，我瞅您挺樂的。」板爺說。  
"*Sir*, you look pretty happy," the driver said.  
『因為今天的天氣實在太好了！』我意猶未盡，不禁伸直雙臂高喊：  
"Because the weather today is just *too good*!" I couldn't help spreading my arms and shouting,  
『實在太好了！』  
"*Too good!*"  
「幼稚。」暖暖說。  
"Childish," Nuannuan said.  
『小姑娘，您說啥？』我said。  
"*Young miss*, what was that?" I said.  
暖暖轉過頭不理我，但沒多久便笑了出來。  
She turned away to ignore me, but soon laughed.  
「真幼稚。」暖暖把頭轉回來，又說。  
"Really childish," she said, turning back.  

幾百公尺外摩天大樓林立，街上車聲鼎沸、霓虹燈閃爍；  
A few hundred meters away skyscrapers loomed, traffic roared, neon flickered;  
但一拐進胡同，卻回到幾百年前，見到北京居民的純樸生活。  
but turn into a *hutong* and you fall back centuries, into the simple lives of old Beijing residents.  
四合院前閉目休息的老太太，大雜院裡拉胡琴的老先生，  
An old lady dozing in front of a courtyard house; an old man drawing a *huqin* in a shared compound —  
這些人並沒有被時代的洪流推著走。  
these people hadn't been swept up by the tide of the times.  
從大街走進胡同，彷彿穿過時光隧道，看到兩個不同的時代。  
Going from the boulevard into a *hutong* was like stepping through a time tunnel and seeing two different eras.  

這裡沒有車聲，有的只是小販抑揚頓挫的吆喝叫賣聲。  
No traffic noise here — only the rising-and-falling cries of street vendors hawking their goods.  
青灰色的牆和屋瓦、朱紅斑駁的大門、掉了漆的金色門環、深陷的門墩，  
Gray-blue walls and roof tiles, peeling vermilion gates, gold door-knockers with the paint flaking off, deeply worn door piers —  
胡同裡到處古意盎然。  
the *hutongs* breathe antiquity from every corner.  
我和暖暖下車走進一大雜院，院裡的居民很親切的跟我們聊幾句。  
We got off and stepped into a shared compound; the residents chatted warmly with us for a while.  
樑上褪了色的彩繪、地上缺了角的青磚，都讓我們看得津津有味。  
The faded painted designs on the beams, the chipped gray bricks on the ground — we drank it all in.  

板爺跟我們說起胡同的種種，他說還有不到半米寬的胡同。  
The driver told us about the alleys, including ones less than half a meter wide.  
「胖一點的人，還擠不進去呢。」他笑著說。  
"A heftier person can't even squeeze through," he laughed.  
『如果兩人在胡同中相遇，怎麼辦？』我轉頭問暖暖。  
"What if two people meet in such a *hutong*?" I asked Nuannuan.  
「用輕功唄。」暖暖笑說，『咻的一聲，就越過去了。』  
"Use *qing gong* — light-weight kung fu," she laughed. "*Whoosh*, you fly right over."  
『萬一兩人都會輕功呢？』我說，『那不就咻咻兩聲再加個砰。』  
"And if both of them have *qing gong*?" I said. "Two *whooshes* and then a *bang*."  
「砰？」  
"*Bang*?"  
『兩人都咻一聲，共咻咻兩聲；然後在半空中相撞，又砰一聲。』  
"Each goes *whoosh*, that's two *whooshes*; then they collide in midair — *bang*."  
暖暖臉上一副又好氣又好笑的神情；  
Nuannuan's face was somewhere between exasperated and amused;  
板爺則放聲大笑，宏亮的笑聲縈繞在胡同間。  
the driver burst out laughing, his hearty laugh echoing through the alley.  

說說笑笑之際，我被路旁炸東西的香味吸引，暖暖也專注地看著。  
Mid-laugh, I was drawn by the smell of something frying by the roadside; Nuannuan was watching too.  
『妳想吃嗎？』我問暖暖。  
"Want some?" I asked.  
暖暖有些不好意思，點了點頭。  
A bit shy, she nodded.  
我讓板爺停下車，走近一看，油鍋旁有一大塊已攪拌揉勻好的麵團。  
I had the driver stop. Closer up: beside the oil pot was a big lump of dough, well kneaded.  
問起這東西，大嬸說是炸奶糕，然後捏下一小塊麵團，用手摁成圓餅，  
The auntie said it was *zha naigao* — fried milk cake. She pinched off a piece, pressed it into a round patty,  
下油鍋後當餅膨脹如球狀並呈金黃色時撈出，再滾上白糖。  
dropped it in the oil, fished it out when it puffed into a golden ball, and rolled it in sugar.  
我買了一些回車上，跟暖暖分著吃。  
I bought some back to the cab and shared them with Nuannuan.  
炸奶糕外脆裡嫩，柔而細滑，咬了一口，散發濃郁奶香。  
Crisp outside, tender within, soft and silky; one bite released a deep milky fragrance.  

板爺維持規律的節奏踩著車，偶爾嘴裡哼唱小曲。  
The driver pedaled in steady rhythm, occasionally humming a tune.  
我和暖暖邊吃邊聊，邊聊邊看。  
We ate and chatted, chatted and watched.  
在這樣的角落，很難察覺時間的流逝，心情容易沉澱。  
In a corner like this, time passes unnoticed; the heart settles easily.  
「恭王府到了。」板爺停下車。  
"Prince Gong's Mansion," the driver said, stopping.  

李老師在恭王府前清點人數，發現還少兩個人。  
Teacher Li counted heads at the gate and found two short.  
過了一會，一輛三輪車載著學弟和王克，板爺以最快的速度踩過來。  
After a while, a pedicab raced up at top speed bearing my junior and Wang Ke.  
我走過去敲了一下學弟的頭，他苦著臉說他並非忘了時間，只是迷了路。  
I went over and knocked on his head. He said pitifully that he hadn't lost track of time — they'd just gotten lost.  
原來他和王克下車走進胡同閒晃時，越走越遠、越遠越雜、越雜越亂，  
Apparently they'd left the cab to wander into a *hutong*, gone deeper, the alleys grew tangled, and tangled into chaos,  
結果讓穿梭複雜的胡同給困住，王克還急哭了。  
and they got trapped in the maze. Wang Ke had been so anxious she cried.  
幸好後來有個好心的老先生帶領他們走出來。  
Eventually a kind old man led them out.  

恭王府雖因咸豐將其賜於恭親王奕訢而得名，但真正讓它聲名大噪的，  
The mansion takes its name from Emperor Xianfeng's bestowing it on Prince Gong, Yixin, but what truly made it famous  
是因為它曾是乾隆寵臣和珅的宅邸。  
was that it was once the residence of Qianlong's favorite minister, He Shen.  
「王府文化是宮廷文化的延伸，恭王府又是現今保存最完整的一座王府。  
"Princely-mansion culture is an extension of palace culture, and Prince Gong's is the most completely preserved one today.  
因此有『一座恭王府，半部清代史』之稱。」李老師笑著說：  
That's why people say, *one Prince Gong's Mansion is half a history of the Qing*," Teacher Li said with a smile.  
「同學們，慢慢逛。有興趣聽點故事的，待會跟著我。」  
"Take your time, students. If you're interested in the stories, follow me."  
一聽李老師這樣說，所有學生都跟在他屁股後頭。  
At that, every student fell in behind him.  

一路走來，幽靜秀雅、春色盎然，府外明明溫度高，裡頭卻清涼無比。  
Through the grounds — quiet, refined, lush. Outside the mansion the heat was fierce; inside it was wonderfully cool.  
李老師說起各建築的種種，像花園門口歐式建築拱門，當時北京只有三座；  
He spoke about each building: a European-style arched gate at the garden entrance — only three of its kind in Beijing at the time;  
全用木頭建的大戲樓，一個鉚釘都沒用，多年來沒漏過雨，戲台下淘空且放置幾口大缸，  
a grand wooden opera house built without a single nail, never leaked a drop in all these years; under the stage was hollowed out and several great jars placed inside,  
增大共鳴空間並達到擴音的作用，因此不需音響設備；  
to enlarge the resonance and amplify sound — no speakers needed;  
屋簷上滿是佛教的「卍」和蝙蝠圖案（卍蝠的諧音，即為萬福），連外觀形狀都像蝙蝠展開雙翼的蝠廳；  
the eaves were covered with Buddhist *卍* and bat motifs (*wan-fu* sounds like *ten thousand blessings*), and even the building's outline mimicked bat wings spread — the Bat Hall;  
和珅與文人雅士飲酒的流杯亭，亭子下有彎彎曲曲的窄溝，杯子在水面漂，停在誰面前誰就得作詩，  
the *Liubei Ting* — Floating-Cup Pavilion — where He Shen drank with literati: a winding shallow channel runs under it; a cup floats along, and whoever it stops in front of must compose a poem,  
不作詩便罰酒；假山上的邀月台，取李白詩中「舉杯邀明月，對影成三人」的意境；  
or be made to drink. On the rockery sits the Inviting-the-Moon Terrace, named after Li Bai's line: *I raise my cup to invite the moon — with my shadow, we become three*;  
通往邀月台兩條坡度很陡的斜坡走廊叫「升官路」，和珅常走升官路，於是步步高陞。  
the two steeply sloping galleries leading up to it are called the *Promotion Road*, which He Shen walked often — and rose, step by step.  
最後走到秘雲洞口，李老師說：「接下來是福字碑。仔細瞧那福字，試試能看出幾個字。」  
At the entrance of the Miyun Cave, Teacher Li said, "Next is the Fu-character Stele. Look carefully at the *fu*; see how many characters you can find inside it."  

同學們一個接一個走進洞，在我前頭的暖暖突然躲到我後面，說：  
The students filed into the cave one by one. Just ahead of me, Nuannuan suddenly ducked behind me and said,  
「你先走。」  
"You go first."  
『為什麼？』我說。  
"Why?"  
「裡頭暗，我怕摔。」暖暖笑說。  
"It's dark in there, I'm afraid I'll trip," she smiled.  
『我也怕啊。』  
"I'm afraid too."  
「別囉唆了。」暖暖輕輕推了推我，「快走便是。」  
"Stop fussing." She gave me a little push. "Just go."  

秘雲洞在假山下，雖有些燈光，但還是昏暗。  
The Miyun Cave is below the rockery; even with some lights, it's dim.  
洞內最亮的地方就是那塊福字碑，因為下頭打了黃色的燈光。  
The brightest point inside was the *fu* stele itself, lit from below in yellow light.  
我靠近一看，碑用塊玻璃保護住，很多人摸不到碑就摸玻璃解解饞。  
Up close I saw the stele was protected by a sheet of glass; people who couldn't touch the stele settled for stroking the glass.  
記得玻璃好像可以指臀部，所以我沒摸玻璃只凝視福字一會，便走出來。  
I remembered that *boli* — glass — can also be slang for buttocks, so I skipped the glass, gazed at the *fu* a moment, and walked out.  
『妳看出幾個字？』我問暖暖。
