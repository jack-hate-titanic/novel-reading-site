# 《暖暖》第一章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

「嘿，我叫暖暖。你呢？」  
"Hey, I'm Nuannuan. What's your name?"  

認識暖暖是在一次海峽兩岸的學生夏令營活動中。  
I met Nuannuan at a cross-strait student summer camp.  
這個夏令營的詳細名稱我忘了，只記得有類似「文化尋根」的關鍵字。  
I've forgotten the camp's full name, only that it had something like "cultural roots" in the title.  
那時我剛通過碩士論文口試，辦離校手續時在學校的網頁裡看到這活動。  
I had just passed my master's defense, and I noticed the program on the school's website while clearing my graduation paperwork.  
由於我打算休息一個月後才要投入職場，索性報了名。  
Since I was planning to take a month off before starting work, I signed up on a whim.  
跟本校幾個學弟妹和其他三所學校的大學生或研究生，一同飛往北京。  
Together with a few juniors from my own school and undergraduates and graduate students from three other schools, I flew to Beijing.  
北京有四所學校的大學生正等著我們。  
In Beijing, students from four more universities were waiting for us.  

這個活動為期八天七夜，活動範圍都在北京附近。  
The program lasted eight days and seven nights, all within the Beijing area.  
四個老師（台灣北京各兩個）領隊，帶領這群五十人左右的學生。  
Four teachers — two from Taiwan and two from Beijing — led the group of about fifty students.  
老師們的年紀比我們大不了多少，而且我們也算是大人了，  
The teachers weren't much older than us, and we were adults ourselves,  
所以他們只是象徵性負責行程安排等雜務，不怎麼管理我們。  
so they handled the logistics in name only and didn't really try to manage us.  
雖然萬一出了事他們得負責，但緊張的反而是我們。  
They would be the ones held responsible if anything went wrong, but somehow we were the nervous ones.  

初見面時，正是準備用晚餐的時分。  
We first met at dinner.  
老師們彼此說些一路上辛苦了、還好還好、您請坐、不不不您先請、  
The teachers exchanged the usual pleasantries — you must be tired, oh, it was nothing, please have a seat, no no, after you,  
千萬別客氣之類的客套話；但所有學生的臉皮都是緊繃著。  
don't stand on ceremony — but every student's face was tight.  
如果你曾睡過很沉的覺，你應該知道剛睡醒時臉皮幾乎是沒有彈性的。  
If you've ever slept deeply, you'll know that just after waking up, your face has almost no give to it.  
沒錯，就是那種缺乏彈性的緊繃感瀰漫在所有學生的臉上。  
That was exactly the kind of stiff, rigid look hanging on every student's face.  

全部的人坐成六桌，上了第一道菜後兩分鐘內，沒人動筷子。  
Everyone was seated at six tables, and for two minutes after the first dish arrived, nobody picked up their chopsticks.  
老師們殷勤勸大家舉筷，學生們則很安靜。  
The teachers urged us to eat; the students stayed quiet.  
我坐的桌子沒有老師，同桌的學生不僅安靜，恐怕已達到肅靜的境界。  
There was no teacher at my table, and the students there were so quiet they had crossed into total silence.  
就在隔壁桌的北京老師勸了第三次「大家開動啊別客氣」的時候，  
Just as the Beijing teacher at the next table was urging us a third time — "Go on, everyone, dig in, don't be shy" —  
坐在我左手邊的女孩開了口，順便問我的名字。  
the girl on my left spoke up, and asked me my name in passing.  

『我叫涼涼。』  
"I'm Liangliang."  

我一定是緊張過了頭，脫口說出這名字。  
I must have been so nervous the name just slipped out.  
如果你是我父母或朋友或同學或認識我的人，你就會知道這不是我名字。  
If you were my parents or my friends or my classmates or anyone who knew me, you would know that wasn't my name.  
「你說真格的嗎？」她的語氣很興奮，「我叫暖暖，你叫涼涼。真巧。」  
"Are you serious?" she said, suddenly excited. "I'm Nuannuan and you're Liangliang. What a coincidence."  
暖暖笑了笑，成為最早恢復臉部肌肉彈性的學生。  
Nuannuan smiled, and became the first student whose face muscles got their elasticity back.  

「同志們，咱們開動吧。」  
"Comrades, let's eat."  
說完後暖暖的右手便拿起筷子，反轉筷頭朝下，輕輕在桌上敲兩聲；  
She picked up her chopsticks with her right hand, flipped them upside down, and tapped the table twice;  
再反轉筷頭朝上，指頭整理好握筷的姿勢，然後右手往盤子伸直。  
then flipped them right side up again, settled her grip, and reached toward a dish.  
暖暖的動作輕，而且把時間拉長，似乎有意讓其他人跟上。  
Her movements were soft and drawn out, as if she were deliberately giving everyone time to follow.  
就像龜縮在戰壕裡的士兵突然看到指揮官直起身慷慨激昂高喊：衝啊！  
It was like soldiers huddled in a trench suddenly seeing their commander stand up and shout: charge!  
於是紛紛爬出戰壕，拿起筷子。  
And so the rest of us climbed out of the trench and picked up our chopsticks.  

暖暖夾起菜到自己的碗上空時停頓一下，再右轉90度放進我碗裡。  
Nuannuan lifted some food, paused over her own bowl, then turned ninety degrees right and dropped it into mine.  
「這菜作得挺地道的，嚐嚐。」她說。  
"This dish is pretty authentic. Try it," she said.  
『這是？』我問。  
"What is it?" I asked.  
「湖北菜。」  
"Hubei cuisine."  
其實我只是想問這看起來紅紅軟軟的是什麼東西，但她既然這麼回答，  
Really I just wanted to know what the soft red thing was, but since she'd answered like that,  
我只好又問：『妳怎麼知道是湖北菜？』  
I had to ask, "How do you know it's Hubei food?"  
「你問的問題挺深奧的。」她回答，「外頭餐廳的招牌上有寫。」  
"That's a deep question," she said. "It's written on the sign out front."  

看來我問了個蠢問題，如果要再開口，得問些真正深奧的問題。  
It seemed I'd asked a stupid question, so if I opened my mouth again, it had better be a genuinely deep one.  
我知道「地道」的台灣說法是「道地」，台灣有太多美食節目說過了。  
I knew that the mainland word *didao* — authentic — gets reversed in Taiwan to *daodi*; food shows back home use it endlessly.  
所以我不會問菜作得地道的說法，是否因為對日抗戰時為躲避日機轟炸，  
So I wasn't about to ask whether calling food *didao* — literally "of the tunnel" — came from the War of Resistance against Japan, when to escape the bombing  
煮菜只得在地道內，於是菜裡有一股堅毅不撓的香味象徵民族刻苦耐勞、  
people had to cook inside underground tunnels, so the food took on a stubborn aroma symbolizing the nation's grit and endurance,  
奮戰不屈的精神，演變到後來要稱讚菜作得很實在便用「地道」來形容？  
its unyielding fighting spirit — until eventually you praised solid, honest cooking by calling it "tunnel-like."  

想了一下後，我開口問的深奧問題是：  
After some thought, the deep question I came up with was:  
『妳是湖北人嗎？』  
"Are you from Hubei?"  
「不是。」暖暖搖搖頭，「我是黑龍江人，來北京念大學。」  
"No." Nuannuan shook her head. "I'm from Heilongjiang. I came to Beijing for university."  
『果然。』我點點頭。  
"I see," I said, nodding.  
「咋了？」  
"What is it?"  

『妳說妳是黑龍江人，對吧？』  
"You said you're from Heilongjiang, right?"  
「嗯。」  
"Mm-hm."  
『這裡是北京，應該在河北省境內。沒錯吧？』  
"And we're in Beijing, which sits inside Hebei Province. Right?"  
「沒錯。」  
"Right."  
『妳沒到過湖北吧？』  
"And you've never been to Hubei?"  
「沒去過。」  
"Never."  
『那妳怎麼會知道這裡的湖北菜很道地——不，很地道呢？』  
"Then how do you know the Hubei food here is so *daodi* — sorry, so *didao*?"  

「這個問題也挺深奧的。」暖暖停住筷子，遲疑了一會，再開口說：  
"That's a pretty deep question too." Nuannuan paused with her chopsticks, hesitated, and then said,  
「我是聽人說的。」  
"I heard it from someone."  
『啊？』  
"Huh?"  
「畢竟你們是從台灣來的，我算是地主，總得硬充一下內行。」  
"You came all the way from Taiwan, after all. I'm the host, so I had to pretend I knew what I was talking about."  
暖暖說完後笑了笑。  
Nuannuan smiled.  
我的緊張感頓時消失了不少。  
A lot of my nervousness fell away in an instant.  

看了看四周，學生們的臉皮已恢復彈性，夾菜舀湯間也會互相點頭微笑。  
I looked around. The students' faces had loosened up, and they were nodding and smiling at each other while passing dishes and ladling soup.  
「對了，我姓秦。」暖暖又開口說，「你呢？」  
"By the way, my surname is Qin," Nuannuan said. "And yours?"  
『我姓蔡。』  
"Cai."  
「蔡涼涼？」暖暖突然笑出聲，「涼涼挺好聽，但跟蔡連在一起就……」  
"Cai Liangliang?" Nuannuan suddenly laughed. "Liangliang is nice on its own, but together with Cai it sounds like..."  
『再怎麼閃亮的名字，跟蔡連在一起都會失去光芒。』  
"No matter how bright the given name, paired with Cai it loses its shine."  
「不見得唷。」  
"Not necessarily."  
『是嗎？』  
"Really?"  

「菜涼了就不好吃了，要趁熱吃。你的名字挺有哲理的。」暖暖笑著說，  
"Once a dish — *cai* — goes cold — *liang* — it's no good; you have to eat while it's hot. Your name is actually quite philosophical," Nuannuan said with a laugh.  
「你父親大概是希望你做人要把握時機、努力向上。」  
"Your father probably wanted you to seize the moment and work hard."  
『那妳叫暖暖有特別的涵義嗎？』我問。  
"And does your name, Nuannuan, mean something special?" I asked.  
「我父親覺得天冷時，暖暖、暖暖這麼叫著，興許就不冷了。」她回答。  
"My father thought that on cold days, calling out *Nuannuan, Nuannuan* — warm, warm — might just keep the cold away," she answered.  
『妳的名字比較好，不深奧又有意境。』  
"Yours is the better name. Not so deep, but full of feeling."  
「謝謝。」暖暖笑了。  
"Thank you," Nuannuan said, smiling.  

我開始感到不安。因為我叫涼涼可不是說真格的，而是說假格的。  
I started to feel uneasy. Because Liangliang wasn't actually my name — that part was a lie.  
沒想到剛剛脫口而出的「涼涼」，會有這麼多的後續發展。  
I never imagined the "Liangliang" that had slipped out earlier would lead to all this.  
幾度想告訴暖暖我不叫涼涼，但始終抓不住良心發現的好時機。  
I thought several times of telling her, but I couldn't find the right moment for my conscience to surface.  
「咋停下筷子呢？」暖暖轉頭對著我說，「快吃唄。」  
"Why did you stop eating?" Nuannuan said, turning to me. "Come on, eat up."  

這頓飯已經吃了一半，很多人開始聊天與談笑。  
Dinner was halfway through, and people had started chatting and laughing.  
跟剛入座時的氣氛相比，真是恍如隔世。  
Compared to the moment we'd first sat down, it felt like another world.  
暖暖和我也閒聊起黑龍江很冷吧台灣很熱吧之類的話題。  
Nuannuan and I drifted into small talk — Heilongjiang must be cold, Taiwan must be hot, that sort of thing.  
聊著聊著便聊到地名的話題，我說在我家鄉有蒜頭、太保、水上等地名。  
That led us to place names, and I mentioned that back home there were towns called Suantou — Garlic — Taibao, and Shuishang.  
『我老家叫布袋。』我說。  
"My hometown is called Budai," I said.  
「就是那個用來裝東西的布袋？」暖暖問。  
"You mean *budai* like a cloth sack?" Nuannuan asked.  
『沒錯。』  
"Exactly."  
「這地名挺有趣的。」  
"That's a fun name for a place."  

『台灣也有個地方叫暖暖喔。』我用突然想起某件事般的口吻說。  
"Taiwan actually has a place called Nuannuan, too," I said, as if it had just occurred to me.  
「你說真格的嗎？」  
"Are you serious?"  
『這次絕對真格，不是假格。』  
"This time really serious — not faking."  
「這次？假格？」  
"This time? Faking?"  
『沒事。』我假裝沒看見暖暖狐疑的眼光，趕緊接著說：  
"Never mind." I pretended not to notice Nuannuan's suspicious look and quickly went on,  
『暖暖應該在基隆，有山有水，是個很寧靜很美的地方。』  
"Nuannuan is in Keelung, I think — mountains, water, a very quiet, beautiful place."  
「你去過嗎？」  
"Have you been?"  
『我也沒去過暖暖。』我笑了笑，『這次該輪到我硬充內行了。』  
"I've never been to Nuannuan either," I said, smiling. "This time it's my turn to fake being the expert."  

「怎麼會有地方取這麼個溫雅賢淑的名字呢？」  
"How does a place end up with such a warm, gentle name?"  
『說得好。暖暖確實是個溫雅賢淑的名字。』  
"Well put. Nuannuan really is a warm, gentle name."  
「多謝誇獎。」暖暖笑了笑。  
"Thanks for the compliment," Nuannuan said with a smile.  
『不客氣。我只是實話實說。』  
"You're welcome. I'm just telling the truth."  
「可以再多告訴我一些關於暖暖這地方的事嗎？」  
"Could you tell me a bit more about Nuannuan, the place?"  

『就我所知，清法戰爭時，清軍和民兵曾在暖暖隔著基隆河與法軍對峙,  
"From what I know, during the Sino-French War, Qing soldiers and militia faced off against the French across the Keelung River at Nuannuan,  
阻止法軍渡河南下攻進台北城。』我想了一會後，說。  
keeping them from crossing the river and pushing south into Taipei," I said after a moment's thought.  
「後來呢？」  
"And then?"  
『法軍始終過不了基隆河。後來清法議和，法軍撤出台灣。』  
"The French never crossed the Keelung River. Eventually the two sides made peace and the French withdrew from Taiwan."  
「還有這段歷史呀。」  
"So there's a story like that."  
『嗯。』我點點頭，『滿清末年難得沒打敗仗，這算其中之一。』  
"Mm." I nodded. "It was one of the rare battles the late Qing didn't lose."  
暖暖也點點頭，然後陷入沉思。  
Nuannuan nodded too, and fell quiet, lost in thought.  

「真想去看看那個有著溫馨名字的地方。」過了幾分鐘，暖暖又開口。  
"I really want to see that place with the warm name," Nuannuan said after a few minutes.  
『很好啊。』  
"That would be nice."  
「那是個什麼樣的地方呢？我真想看。」  
"What's it really like? I'd love to see it."  
『非常好。』  
"Very nice."  
「我是說真格的。」  
"I mean it — really."  
『我知道。』  
"I know."  
「這是約定。」  
"It's a promise."  
『啊？我答應了什麼嗎？』  
"What? Did I agree to something?"  

「總之，」暖暖的臉上帶著古怪的笑容，「我一定要去暖暖瞧瞧。」  
"Anyway," Nuannuan said, with an odd smile, "I'm definitely going to see Nuannuan."  
我看了看她，沒有答話，試著體會她想去暖暖的心情。  
I looked at her without answering, trying to imagine what made her want to go.  
我知道暖暖應該不是那種你不帶我去，我就死給你看的任性女孩；  
She didn't strike me as the kind of girl who would say *take me there or I'll die in front of you*;  
更不是那種你不帶我去，你就死給我看的凶殘女孩。  
and certainly not the kind who would say *take me there or I'll kill you in front of me*.  
也許她口中的約定，只是跟她自己約定而已。  
Maybe the promise she spoke of was only one she was making with herself.  

飯局結束後，我們來到一所大學的宿舍，往後的七個晚上都在這裡。  
After dinner, we moved to a university dormitory, where we'd be staying for the next seven nights.  
因為這頓飯比預期的時間多吃了一個鐘頭，又考慮到台灣學生剛下飛機，  
Since the meal had run an hour longer than scheduled, and the Taiwanese students had just gotten off the plane,  
所以取消預定的自我介紹，將所有學生分成六組後，就各自回房歇息。  
the planned self-introductions were canceled. The students were split into six groups, then sent off to rest.  
取消自我介紹讓我鬆了口氣，因為我可不能在大家面前說我叫蔡涼涼。  
Cancelling the introductions was a relief — I couldn't very well stand up in front of everyone and announce that I was Cai Liangliang.  
四個人一間房，男女分開（這是無可奈何的當然）。  
Four people to a room, men and women separated, of course.  
不過在分房時，還是引起一陣小騷動。  
Even so, the room assignments caused a small commotion.  

台灣學生的姓名，清一色是三個字。  
The Taiwanese students all had three-character names — surname plus two.  
以我來說，小學、初中、高中、大學、研究所，沒碰過兩個字的同學。  
In my own case, from primary school through grad school, I'd never met a classmate with a two-character name.  
但北京學生的姓名，竟然多數是兩個字。  
But most of the Beijing students had two-character names.  
男的名字還算好辨認，有些女孩的名字就很中性甚至偏陽性了。  
The boys' names were recognizable enough, but some of the girls' names were unisex — even leaning masculine.  
有位台灣女孩發現同寢的室友竟然叫岳峰和王克，吃了一驚才引起騷動。  
One Taiwanese girl discovered that her roommates were named Yue Feng and Wang Ke, and her shock set the whole thing off.  

「你能想像一個溫柔端莊的姑娘叫岳峰嗎？」  
"Can you picture a soft, ladylike girl named Yue Feng?"  
叫岳峰的女孩帶著悲憤的語氣說。  
the girl named Yue Feng said with a wounded indignation.  
至於王克，則是個身材嬌小的清秀女孩。  
As for Wang Ke, she was a small, delicate-featured girl.  
岳峰和王克，都是令人猜不透的深奧名字。  
Yue Feng and Wang Ke — both impossibly deep names.  

學生們開始研究起彼此的姓名，有人說三個字好聽、兩個字好記；  
The students started comparing names; some said three characters sounded better, others that two were easier to remember;  
也有人說兩個字如果碰到大姓，就太容易撞名了。  
others noted that with two-character names and a common surname, duplicates were almost guaranteed.  
聊著聊著便忘了回房，老師們過來催說早點歇息明天要早起之類的話。  
We got so caught up in the conversation we forgot to head to our rooms; the teachers came over and reminded us to rest, since we had to be up early.  
回房的路上剛好跟暖暖擦身，「涼涼，明天見囉。」拎個袋子的暖暖說。  
On the way back I happened to pass Nuannuan. "See you tomorrow, Liangliang," she said, a bag in her hand.  
旁人用狐疑的眼光看我，我心想叫涼涼的事早晚會穿幫。  
The others gave me suspicious looks, and I thought to myself that the Liangliang thing was bound to come out sooner or later.  

同寢的室友一個是我學校的學弟，另兩個是北京學生，叫徐馳和高亮。  
My roommates were a junior from my own school and two Beijing students, Xu Chi and Gao Liang.  
徐馳和高亮這種名字就不深奧了。  
Names like Xu Chi and Gao Liang weren't all that deep.  
由於我比他們大兩歲左右，他們便叫我老蔡，學弟也跟著叫。  
Since I was about two years older, they called me Old Cai, and my junior fell in with that too.  
我們四人在房裡打屁閒聊，北京的用語叫砍大山。  
The four of us sat around shooting the breeze — what Beijingers call *kan da shan*, "chopping down the big mountain."  
我掛心涼涼的事，又覺得累，因此砍一下休息兩下，有一搭沒一搭地砍。  
With Liangliang on my mind and exhaustion settling in, I'd talk a little, rest a lot, and only half-keep up.  

閉上眼，我告訴自己這裡是北京、我在北京的天空下、我來到北京了。  
I closed my eyes and told myself: this is Beijing, I'm under Beijing's sky, I've made it to Beijing.  
為了給北京留下初次見面的好印象，我可千萬別失眠。  
To make a good first impression on Beijing, I had better not stay up.  
不過我好像多慮了，因為沒多久我便迷迷糊糊睡著了。  
But I needn't have worried — before long I drifted hazily off to sleep.
