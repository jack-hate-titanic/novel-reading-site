# 《暖暖》第十四章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

尖銳的鈴聲把我拉離夢境，但我還不想離開夢中的雪地。
A sharp ring pulled me from my dream, but I didn't want to leave the snowy dreamland.
「涼涼，起床了。」
"Liangliang, wake up."
感覺右手臂被搖晃，睜開眼看見暖暖，我嚇得坐直了身。
I felt my right arm being shaken. I opened my eyes to see Nuannuan and sat up with a start.
「咋了？」暖暖問。
"What?" she asked.
腦袋空白了幾秒，終於想起我在火車上，而且暖暖在身旁。
My mind was blank for a few seconds before I remembered — I was on the train, and she was right there.
『嘿嘿。我笑了笑。』
"Heh heh," I laughed.

拿著牙刷牙膏毛巾，才剛走出包廂，冷冽的空氣讓我完全清醒。
Toiletries in hand, the moment I stepped out of the compartment, the biting air woke me completely.
還好盥洗室有熱水，如果只有冷水，洗完臉後我的臉就變成冰雕了。
Good thing the washroom had hot water — with cold water, my face would have turned into an ice sculpture.
漱洗完後回到包廂，把鞋子穿上，檢查一下有沒有忘了帶的東西。
I finished washing, returned to the compartment, put on my shoes, and checked that I hadn't forgotten anything.
理了理衣服，背上背包，我和暖暖下了火車。
I straightened my clothes, put on my backpack, and we got off the train.

「終於到了你口中的哈哈哈爾濱了。」暖暖說，「有何感想？」
"Finally arrived at your so-called Hahaharbin," she said. "Any impressions?"
『北京冷、哈爾濱更冷，連暖暖說的笑話都比台灣冷。』我牙齒打顫，
"Beijing is cold, Harbin is colder — even your jokes are colder than Taiwan's," I chattered.
『總之就是一個冷字。』
"In a word: cold."
「還不快把圍巾和毛線帽戴上。」
"Hurry up and put on your scarf and hat."
我把圍巾圍上，但毛線帽因為沒戴過，所以怎麼戴都覺得怪。
I wrapped the scarf around my neck, but the knit cap felt strange — I'd never worn one before.
暖暖幫我把毛線帽往下拉了拉，再調整一下，然後輕拍一下我的頭。
She pulled it down a bit, adjusted it, and lightly patted my head.
「行了。」暖暖笑了。
"All set," she smiled.

準備坐上計程車，手才剛接觸金屬製門把，啪的一聲我的手迅速抽回。
As I reached for the metal door handle of a taxi — *zap* — I jerked my hand back.
「天氣冷。」暖暖笑著說，「靜電特強。」
"It's the cold," she laughed. "Lots of static."
『這樣日子也未免過得太驚險了吧。』我說。
"Life is too dangerous like this," I said.
「電久了，就習慣了。」暖暖說。
"Get zapped enough and you'll get used to it," she said.
暖暖說以前頭髮長，有次搭計程車時髮梢掃到門把，嗶嗶剝剝一陣亂響。
She said when her hair was long, the ends once brushed the handle — crackling sounds everywhere.
「還看到火花呢。」暖暖笑了笑。
"You could even see sparks," she laughed.
我說這樣真好，頭髮電久了就捲了，可省下一筆燙頭髮的錢。
I said that was great — enough static and her hair would curl, saving her a perm.

坐上計程車，透過車窗欣賞哈爾濱的早晨，天空是清澈的藍。
In the taxi, we watched Harbin's morning through the window. The sky was clear blue.
哈爾濱不愧「東方莫斯科」的稱號，市容有股濃厚的俄羅斯風味，
Harbin truly deserved the name "Oriental Moscow" — the city had a strong Russian flavor,
街頭也常見屋頂尖斜像「合」字的俄羅斯建築。
with pointed roofs like Russian Orthodox churches everywhere.
我和暖暖在一家狗不理包子吃早飯，這是天津狗不理包子的加盟店。
We had breakfast at a Goubuli Baozi franchise — a Tianjin chain.
熱騰騰的包子皮薄味美，再加上綠豆粥的香甜，全身開始覺得暖和。
Steaming hot buns with thin wrappers and delicious filling, plus sweet mung bean porridge — my whole body started to warm up.

哈爾濱的商家幾乎都是早上八點營業、晚上七點打烊，
Most Harbin shops opened at 8 AM and closed at 7 PM —
這在台灣實在難以想像。
unimaginable in Taiwan.
我和暖暖來到一家像是茶館的店，進門前暖暖交代：
We arrived at a teahouse-like place. Before entering, she instructed:
「待會碰面的人姓齊，咱們要稱呼他……」
"The person we're meeting is named Qi. We should call him…"
『齊瓦哥醫生。』我打斷她。
"Doctor Zhivago," I interrupted.
「哈爾濱已經夠冷的了，千萬別說冷笑話。」暖暖笑了笑，
"Harbin is cold enough — don't make cold jokes," she smiled.
「而且齊瓦哥醫生在內地改姓了，叫日瓦戈醫生。」
"Besides, Doctor Zhivago changed his surname on the mainland — it's now Doctor Riwago."
『妳自己還不是講冷笑話。』我說。
"You're making cold jokes yourself," I said.
「總之要稱呼他齊老師，而不是齊醫生。」
"Anyway, we call him Teacher Qi, not Doctor Qi."
我點點頭便想推開店門，但接觸門把那瞬間，又被電得哇哇叫。
I nodded and reached for the door — *zap* again.

去過暖暖的工作地方，知道大概是出版社或雜誌社之類的，但沒細問。
I'd been to her office before — some kind of publisher or magazine, but I hadn't asked.
因此暖暖與齊老師對談的語言與內容，不會讓我覺得枯燥。
So when she and Teacher Qi talked, the content didn't bore me.
若我和暖暖角色互調，我談工作她陪我，我猜她聽不到十分鐘就會昏睡。
If our roles were reversed, she'd probably be asleep in ten minutes.
為了不單純只做個裝飾品，我會在筆記本上塗塗鴉，假裝忙碌；
To avoid being just decoration, I doodled in my notebook, pretending to be busy.
偶爾也點頭說些您說得對、說得真好、有道理之類的話。
Occasionally I'd nod and say "You're right," "Well said," "Makes sense."

與齊老師訪談結束後，我們來到一棟像是60年代建築的樓房。
After the interview, we went to a building that looked like it was from the 60s.
這次碰面的是個五十歲左右的大嬸，「姓安。」暖暖說。
This time we met a woman in her fifties. "Her surname is An," she said.
『莫非是安娜·卡列尼娜？』我說，『哈爾濱真的很俄羅斯耶。』
"Could it be Anna Karenina?" I said. "Harbin is really Russian."
「涼涼。」暖暖淡淡地說。
"Liangliang," she said flatly.
『是。』我說，『要稱呼她為安老師。』
"Yes," I said. "We call her Teacher An."
「嗯。」暖暖又笑了，「而且安娜·卡列尼娜應該是姓卡才對。」
"Mm." She smiled again. "And Anna Karenina's surname should be Ka."

離開安老師住所，剛過中午12點。暖暖有些急，因為下個約似乎會遲到。
We left Teacher An's place just past noon. She was a bit anxious — the next appointment might be late.
叫了輛計程車，我急著打開車門時又被電了一次。
We hailed a taxi; I got zipped again rushing to open the door.
下了車，抬頭一看，招牌上寫著「波特曼西餐廳」。
Getting out, I looked up — the sign read "Portman's Western Restaurant."
還好門把是木製的，不然再電下去我就會像周星馳一樣，
Good thing the door handle was wood — otherwise I'd end up like Stephen Chow,
學會電角神拳。
learning the Electric Horn Fist.

「手套戴著唄。」暖暖說，「就不會電著了。」
"Wear your gloves," she said. "Then you won't get zapped."
『為什麼現在才說？』
"Why didn't you say that before?"
「因為我想看你被電呀。」暖暖笑著說。
"Because I wanted to watch you get zapped," she laughed.
我想想自己也真夠笨，打算以後手套就戴著，進屋內再拿掉。
I realized I was pretty dumb. From now on I'd keep my gloves on, taking them off only indoors.

暖暖很快走到一個年約四十歲的中年男子桌旁，說了聲抱歉、來晚了。
She quickly walked to a table where a man in his forties sat. "Sorry I'm late."
他笑了笑說沒事，便示意我們坐下再說。
He smiled and said it was fine, gesturing for us to sit.
「從學生時代便喜歡您的作品，今天很榮幸能見您一面。」暖暖說。
"I've loved your work since I was a student. It's an honor to meet you," she said.
「錢鍾書說得不錯，喜歡吃雞蛋，但不用去看看下蛋的雞長得如何。」
"Qian Zhongshu was right — if you like eggs, you don't need to see the chicken that laid them."
他哈哈大笑，「有些人還是不見的好。」
He laughed heartily. "Some people are better left unseen."
嗯，他應該是個很好相處的人。
Mm — he seemed easy to get along with.

打量了一下這家俄式餐廳，天花板有幅古歐洲地圖，還懸掛著水晶吊燈。
I looked around the Russian restaurant. An old European map was painted on the ceiling; crystal chandeliers hung above.
鵝黃色的燈光並不刺眼，反而令人覺得舒服與溫暖。
The warm yellow light was gentle and comforting.
雕花的桌架、窗戶的彩色玻璃、紅木吧台和走廊、刻了歲月痕跡的燭台；
Carved table frames, stained glass windows, mahogany bar and hallway, time-worn candlesticks.
大大的啤酒桶窩在角落，牆上擺了許多酒瓶，素雅壁面掛了幾幅老照片。
Big beer barrels sat in the corners; wine bottles lined the walls; old photos hung on the simple walls.
音響流瀉出的，是小提琴和鋼琴的旋律，輕柔而優雅。
Soft violin and piano melodies flowed from the speakers — gentle and elegant.
這是寒冷城市裡的一個溫暖角落。
This was a warm corner in a cold city.

暖暖點了俄式豬肉餅、罐燒羊肉、紅菜湯、大馬哈魚子醬等俄羅斯菜，
She ordered Russian meat pies, pot-braised lamb, borscht, salmon caviar,
還點了三杯紅酒。
and three glasses of red wine.
『紅酒？』我輕聲在暖暖耳邊說，『這不像是妳的風格。』
"Red wine?" I whispered. "Doesn't seem like your style."
「讓你喝的。」暖暖也輕聲在我耳邊說，「喝點酒暖暖身子。」
"For you to drink," she whispered back. "Warm yourself up."
『妳的名字還可以當動詞用。』我說，『真令人羨慕。』
"Your name can be used as a verb," I said. "I'm jealous."
暖暖瞄了我一眼，我便知道要閉嘴。
She gave me a look, and I knew to shut up.

這裡的俄羅斯菜道不道地我不知道，但是好吃，價錢也不貴。
I didn't know if the Russian food was authentic, but it was good and not expensive.
紅酒據說是店家自釀的，酒味略淺，香甜而不苦澀，有種獨特的味道。
The red wine was supposedly house-made — light, sweet, not bitter, with a unique flavor.
餐廳內瀰漫溫暖的氣氛，顧客臉上也都有一種淡淡的、看似幸福的笑容。
The warm atmosphere filled the restaurant; even the customers wore faint, contented smiles.
暖暖和那位中年男子邊吃邊談，我專心吃飯和喝酒，三人都有事做。
She and the man talked as they ate; I focused on the food and wine. Everyone had something to do.
當我打算拿出餐巾紙擦擦滿足的嘴角時，發現包著餐巾紙的紙袋外面，
Just as I reached for a napkin to wipe my satisfied mouth, I noticed something printed on the paper wrapper.
印著一首詩。
A poem.

秋天　我回到波特曼
*Autumn — I return to Portman.*
在那首老情歌的末尾
*At the end of that old love song,*
想起你特有的固執
*I think of your particular stubbornness.*

從我信賴地把你當作一件風衣
*From the time I trusted you like a coat,*
直到你縮小成電話簿裡
*until you shrank into a distant number*
一個遙遠的號碼　這期間
*in my phone book — through all this,*
我的堅強　夜夜被思念偷襲
*my strength was ambushed by longing every night.*

你的信皺皺巴巴的
*Your letters are wrinkled,*
像你總被微笑淹沒的額頭
*like your forehead always drowned in smiles.*
我把它對準燭光
*I hold it to the candlelight*
輕輕地撕開
*and gently tear it open.*

當一枚戒指掉進紅酒杯
*When a ring falls into a wine glass,*
我的幸福
*my happiness*
已奪眶而出
*overflows from my eyes.*

「當一枚戒指掉進紅酒杯，我的幸福已奪眶而出。」中年男子說。
"When a ring falls into a wine glass, my happiness overflows from my eyes," the man said.
我抬起頭看了看他，我猜他應該是跟我說話，便點了點頭。
I looked up at him. I guessed he was talking to me, so I nodded.
「這首詩給你的感覺如何？」他問。
"What do you think of this poem?" he asked.
『嗯……』我沉吟一下，『雖然看似得到幸福，卻有一股哀傷的感覺。』
"Mm…" I thought for a moment. "It seems to describe happiness, but there's a sense of sadness."
「是嗎？」他又問，「那你覺得寫詩的人是男的還是女的？」
"Really?" he asked. "Do you think the poet is a man or a woman?"

『字面上像是描述一位終於得到愛情的女性，但我認為寫詩的人是男的，
"On the surface it describes a woman who finally finds love, but I think the poet is a man.
搞不好就是這家餐廳老闆，而且他一定失去所愛的人。』我說。
Maybe it's the restaurant owner — and he must have lost someone he loved."
「挺有趣的。」他笑了笑，「說來聽聽。」
"Interesting," he smiled. "Tell me more."
『也許老闆失去摯愛後，寫下情詩、自釀紅酒，讓顧客們在喝杯紅酒時，
"Maybe after losing his love, he wrote this poem and brewed this wine, so that when customers drink it,
心中便期待得到幸福。』我說，『男生才有這種胸襟。』
they'd feel hope for happiness. Only a man would have that kind of vision."
「那女的呢？」
"And a woman?"
『女的失去摯愛後，還是會快快樂樂的嫁別人。』我說。
"A woman, after losing her love, would just happily marry someone else."
「瞎說！」暖暖開了口。
"Nonsense!" Nuannuan cut in.
一時忘了暖暖在身旁，我朝暖暖打了個哈哈。
I'd forgotten she was there. I laughed sheepishly.

「你的想像力很豐富。」他說。
"You have a vivid imagination," he said.
我有些不好意思，簡單笑了笑。
I felt a bit embarrassed and smiled briefly.
暖暖起身上洗手間，他等暖暖走後，說：
She got up to use the restroom. After she left, he said:
「很多姑娘會把心愛的男人拐到這兒來喝杯紅酒。」
"Many girls bring the man they love here for a glass of red wine."
『就為了那首詩？』我說。
"Because of the poem?" I said.
「嗯。」他點點頭，「你知道嗎？秦小姐原先並非跟我約在這。」
"Mm." He nodded. "You know? Miss Qin originally didn't arrange to meet me here."
『喔？』我有些好奇。
"Oh?" I was curious.
「我猜她是因為你，才改約在這裡。」
"I think she changed it because of you."
『你的想像力也很豐富。』我說。
"You have a vivid imagination too," I said.

暖暖從洗手間回來後，他說：「合同帶了嗎？」
When she returned, he said, "Did you bring the contract?"
『帶了。』暖暖有些驚訝，從包裡拿出合同。
"Yes." She was a bit surprised and took it out of her bag.
「我趕緊簽了。」他笑著說，「你們才有時間好好逛逛哈爾濱。」
"Let me sign it quickly so you two can enjoy Harbin," he smiled.
暖暖將合同遞給他，他只看了幾眼，便俐落地簽上名。
She handed it over. He glanced at it briefly and signed smoothly.

「那首詩給我的感覺，也是哀傷。」他站起身，抖了抖衣角，說：
"That poem feels sad to me too." He stood up and straightened his coat.
「戒指並非藏在信裡，而是拿在手上。將戒指投進紅酒杯時，奪眶而出的
"The ring wasn't hidden in the letter — it was held in the hand. When it fell into the wine glass, what overflowed
不是幸福，而是自己的淚。」
wasn't happiness — it was your own tears."
他說了聲再見後，便離開波特曼。
He said goodbye and left Portman.
「我不在時，你們說了啥？」暖暖問。
"What did you talk about when I was gone?" she asked.
『這是男人之間的秘密。』我搖搖頭，『不能告訴女人。』
"That's a secret between men," I shook my head. "Can't tell women."

走出波特曼，冷風撲面，我呼出一口長長的白氣，卻覺得通體舒暢。
We stepped out of Portman. The cold wind hit my face. I exhaled a long white breath but felt invigorated.
經過一座西式馬車銅雕塑，看見一條又長又寬的大街道，這是中央大街。
Passing a bronze horse-carriage sculpture, we saw a long, wide street — Central Street.
中央大街始建於1898年，舊稱中國大街，但其實一點也不中國。
Built in 1898, originally called China Street, but it didn't look Chinese at all.
全長1450米，寬度超過20米，兩旁都是歐式及仿歐式建築，
1,450 meters long, over 20 meters wide, lined with European and European-style buildings —
匯集文藝復興、巴洛克、哥德、拜占庭、折衷主義、新藝術運動等建築。
Renaissance, Baroque, Gothic, Byzantine, Eclecticism, Art Nouveau.
建築顏色多姿多彩，紅色系、綠色系、黃色系、粉色系、灰色系都有。
Vibrant colors — reds, greens, yellows, pinks, grays.
整條大街像是一條建築藝術長廊，有著驕傲的氣質和浪漫的氣氛。
The whole street was like an architectural gallery — proud and romantic.

地上鋪著花崗岩地磚，因為年代已超過一百年，路面呈現些微高低起伏。
The granite paving stones, over a century old, formed a slightly uneven surface.
這些花崗岩長18公分、寬10公分、高近半米，一塊一塊深深嵌入地面，
Each stone was 18 cm long, 10 cm wide, and nearly half a meter deep — embedded deep into the ground,
鋪出一條長長的石路。每塊花崗岩約等於當時中國百姓一個月生活費。
paving the long stone road. Each granite block cost about a month's salary for an ordinary Chinese person at the time.
全黑的街燈柱子為燭台樣式，燭台上沒插著蠟燭，而是用毛玻璃燈盞。
The all-black lampposts were shaped like candlesticks, with frosted glass shades instead of candles.
像極了十九世紀歐洲街道上的路燈。
Just like 19th-century European streetlamps.
恍惚間聽見達達的馬啼聲，下意識回頭望，以為突然來了輛馬車。
In a daze, I heard the clip-clop of hooves and instinctively turned, thinking a carriage was coming.
腦裡浮現電影《戰爭與和平》中，從馬車走下來的奧黛麗赫本。
In my mind, Audrey Hepburn stepped out of a carriage from *War and Peace*.

今天是星期六，這裡是步行街，汽車不能進來，不知道馬車可不可以？
It was Saturday. This was a pedestrian street — no cars. Were carriages allowed?
街上出現人潮，女孩們的鞋跟踩著石磚，發出清脆聲響。
Crowds filled the street. Girls' heels clicked on the stone.
哈爾濱女孩身材高挑，腰桿總是挺直，眉目之間有股英氣，感覺很酷。
Harbin girls were tall, with straight postures and spirited brows — they looked cool.
如果跟她們搭訕時說話不得體，應該會被打成重傷吧。

She gave a simple "oh," as if used to the different terms across the strait.
『不過不管是斯大林還是史達林，都是死去的愛人的意思。』
"But whether it's Stalin or Shidalin — both sound like *dead darling* in Chinese."
「死去的愛人？」暖暖很疑惑。
"Dead darling?" She looked puzzled.
『嗯。』我點點頭，『死去的愛人，死darling。』
"Mm." I nodded. "Dead darling — *si darling.*"
暖暖突然停下腳步，眼神空洞。
She stopped suddenly, staring blankly.

『這個笑話應該有五顆星。』我很得意。
"This joke must be five stars," I said smugly.
「我凍僵了。」暖暖說，「早跟你說在哈爾濱不能講冷笑話。」
"I'm frozen," she said. "I told you — no cold jokes in Harbin."
『嘿嘿。』我笑了笑。
"Heh heh," I laughed.
暖暖的雙頰依舊凍得發紅，睫毛上似乎有一串串光影流轉的小冰珠。
Her cheeks were still red from the cold. There seemed to be tiny ice beads shimmering on her lashes.
『暖暖！』我嚇了一跳，用手輕拍暖暖的臉頰，『妳真的凍僵了嗎？』
"Nuannuan!" I was startled and gently touched her cheek. "Are you really frozen?"
「說啥呀。」
"What are you talking about?"
暖暖似乎也嚇了一跳，而雙頰的紅，暈滿了整個臉龐。
She seemed startled too. The red flush spread across her whole face.

『妳的睫毛……』我手指著暖暖的眼睛。
"Your eyelashes…" I pointed at her eyes.
「哦。」暖暖恍然大悟，「天冷，睫毛結上了霜，沒事。」
"Oh." She caught on. "It's cold — frost on my eyelashes. It's nothing."
『嚇死我了。』我拍了拍胸口。
"You scared me." I patted my chest.
「那我把它擦了。」暖暖說完便舉起右手。
"Then I'll wipe it off," she said, raising her right hand.
『別擦。』我說，『這樣很美。』
"Don't," I said. "It's beautiful."
暖暖右手停在半空，然後再緩緩放下。
Her hand stopped midair, then slowly lowered.

我們不約而同停下腳步，單純感受哈爾濱的冬天。
Without a word, we both stopped, simply feeling Harbin's winter.
天色漸漸暗了，溫度應該降得更低，不過我分不出來。
It was getting dark. The temperature must have dropped further, but I couldn't tell.
我感覺臉部肌肉好像失去知覺，快成冰雕了。
My face felt numb — I was becoming an ice sculpture.
『暖暖。』我說話有些艱難，『幫我看看，我是不是凍僵了？』
"Nuannuan." I spoke with difficulty. "Check for me — am I frozen?"
「沒事。」暖暖看了我一眼，「春天一到，就好了。」
"It's fine." She glanced at me. "Spring will come, and you'll thaw."
『喂。』我說。
"Hey," I said.
「吃點東西唄。」暖暖笑了笑。
"Let's get something to eat," she smiled.

我們走到附近餐館，各叫了碗熱騰騰的豬肉燉粉條。
We went to a nearby restaurant and each ordered a bowl of steaming pork stew with vermicelli.
肉湯的味道都燉進粉裡頭，吃了一口，奇香無比。
The broth had soaked into the noodles — incredibly fragrant.
我的臉部又回復彈性，不僅可以自然說話，搞不好還可以繞口令。
My face regained its elasticity — I could talk normally, maybe even do tongue twisters.
吃完後走出餐館，天完全黑了。
We finished and walked out. It was completely dark.
但中央大街卻成了一道黃色光廊。
But Central Street had become a corridor of golden light.

中央大街兩旁仿十九世紀歐洲的街燈都亮了，濃黃色的光照亮了石磚。
The 19th-century-style streetlamps had come on, casting thick yellow light over the stones.
踏著石磚緩緩走著，像走進電影裡的十九世紀場景。
Stepping on the stones was like walking into a 19th-century film set.
具有代表性的建築也打上了投射燈，由下往上，因此雖亮卻不刺眼。
Spotlights lit the landmark buildings from below — bright but not harsh.
這些投射燈光以黃色為主，局部地方以藍色、紅色與綠色燈光加強。
The light was mostly yellow, with accents of blue, red, and green.
雖然白天才剛走過這條大街，但此刻卻有完全不一樣的風景。
Though we'd walked this street just hours ago in daylight, the scene was completely different now.
日間的喧譁沒留下痕跡，取而代之的是一派金碧輝煌。
The daytime bustle had left no trace — replaced by a magnificent splendor.
我相信夜晚的哈爾濱更冷，但卻有一種溫暖的美。
I believed Harbin at night was even colder, but there was a warm beauty to it.
我竟然有些傷感，因為即將離開美麗的哈爾濱。
I felt a hint of sadness — I'd soon be leaving this beautiful city.

走回到聖索菲亞教堂，暗紅色的磚已變成亮黃，窗戶的玻璃透著翠綠。
Back at St. Sophia's, the dark red bricks had turned bright yellow; the window glass glowed emerald.
『暖暖，好美喔。』我情不自禁發出讚嘆。
"Nuannuan — it's so beautiful," I said, unable to contain my admiration.
「是呀。」暖暖說。
"Yes," she said.
『我剛講的句子，拿掉逗號也成立。』我說。
"The sentence I just said — it works even without the comma," I said.
暖暖沒說什麼，只是淺淺笑了笑。
She said nothing, just smiled faintly.

我和暖暖坐在階梯上，靜靜感受哈爾濱最後的溫柔。
We sat on the steps, quietly feeling Harbin's last tenderness.
哈爾濱的冬天確實很冷，但我心裡卻開滿了春天的花朵。
Harbin's winter was indeed cold, but in my heart, spring flowers were blooming.
