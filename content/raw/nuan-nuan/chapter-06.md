# 《暖暖》第六章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

「今天換換口味，咱們到北京大學上課。」李老師說。  
"Let's change things up today — we're having class at Peking University," Teacher Li said.  
我們之中的北京學生都不是北大的，去北大上課對他們而言是新鮮的；  
None of the Beijing students in our group were from PKU, so going there was novel for them;  
而對台灣學生來說，多少帶點朝聖的意味前去。  
and for the Taiwanese students, it carried a sense of pilgrimage.  

我們從西門進入北大。  
We entered PKU through its west gate.  
沒想到這個校門竟是古典的宮門建築，三個朱紅色的大門非常搶眼。  
I hadn't expected the gate to be a classical palace-style structure — three brilliant vermilion doors, impossible to miss.  
若不是中間懸掛著「北京大學」的匾額，我還以為是王府或是宮殿呢。  
If not for the plaque reading "Peking University" in the middle, I'd have thought it was a prince's mansion or a palace.  
兩尊雕刻精細的石獅威嚴地蹲坐在校門左右，目光炯炯有神，不怒自威。  
Two finely carved stone lions sat majestically on either side, their eyes gleaming, imposing even without a growl.  
「這是圓明園的石獅。」李老師說。  
"These are from the Old Summer Palace," Teacher Li said.  
校門口人潮川流不息，卻沒人留意這兩尊歷經百年滄桑的石獅子。  
People streamed past in an endless flow, but no one paid attention to these century-old stone lions.  

從西門走進北大，最先映入眼簾的，是兩座聳立在草地上的華表。  
Once through the gate, the first thing to catch the eye was a pair of marble *huabiao* pillars rising from the lawn.  
在翠綠草地的烘托下，頂著陽光的華表顯得格外潔白莊嚴。  
Against the green grass and under the sun, the pillars looked especially white and solemn.  
我想起在紫禁城看到的華表，心裡起了疑問：校園中怎會安置華表？  
I remembered the *huabiao* in the Forbidden City and wondered: what were they doing on a university campus?  
「這對華表也是來自圓明園。」李老師說。  
"This pair is also from the Old Summer Palace," Teacher Li said.  
又是圓明園？  
The Old Summer Palace again?  

一路往東走，見到許多明清建築風格的樓房，很典雅，周圍都是綠化帶。  
Heading east, we passed buildings in Ming-Qing architectural style — elegant, surrounded by greenery.  
暖暖告訴我，李老師是北大畢業生，而圓明園遺址就在北大隔壁。  
Nuannuan told me Teacher Li was a PKU graduate, and that the Old Summer Palace ruins were right next door.  
李老師說北大最有名的就是「一塔湖圖」，像一塌糊塗的諧音。  
Teacher Li said PKU's most famous attraction was "One Tower Lake and Library" — which sounds like "a big mess" in Chinese.  
所謂一塔湖圖，指的就是博雅塔、未名湖、北大圖書館。  
"One Tower Lake and Library" referred to Boya Pagoda, Weiming Lake, and PKU Library.  
穿過一帶樹木茂密的丘陵，便看到未名湖，博雅塔則矗立在東南湖畔。  
Through a stretch of wooded hills, Weiming Lake appeared, with Boya Pagoda standing on its southeastern shore.  

我們一行人沿未名湖畔走著，博雅塔的倒影在湖中隱隱浮現，湖景極美。  
We walked along Weiming Lake; the pagoda's reflection shimmered faintly on the water — a breathtaking scene.  
湖水柔波蕩漾，湖畔低垂的楊柳婀娜多姿，湖中又有小島點綴湖光塔影。  
The lake rippled gently, willow branches hung low along the shore, and a small island in the lake completed the picture of shimmering water and pagoda.  
「當初為未名湖取名時，提出很多名稱，但都不令人滿意。」李老師說，  
"When they were naming Weiming Lake, many names were proposed, but none satisfied anyone," Teacher Li said.  
「最後國學大師錢穆便直接以『未名』稱之，從此未名湖便傳開了。」  
"In the end, the great scholar Qian Mu simply called it *Weiming* — Nameless — and the name stuck."  
『我以後也要當國學大師。』我說。  
"I want to be a great scholar too someday," I said.  
「唷，想奮發向上了？」暖暖笑得有些俏皮。  
"Oh, feeling ambitious?" Nuannuan smiled playfully.  
『嗯。』我點點頭，『我特別不會取名，但當了國學大師後就不會有這種  
"Mm." I nodded. "I'm terrible at naming things, but once I'm a great scholar, that won't be a problem anymore."  
困擾了。』  
暖暖不理我，逕自走開。  
She ignored me and walked off.  

不過萬一國學大師太多，恐怕也會有困擾。  
But if there were too many great scholars, there'd be other problems.  
比方說兩個陌生的中國人在美國相遇，談起過去種種，把酒言歡。  
Like two strangers from China meeting in America, drinking and sharing their stories.  
第一個說他住在未名路上的未名樓，第二個很興奮地說：真巧，我也是。  
The first says he lives in the Unnamed Building on Unnamed Road. The second gets excited: *What a coincidence — me too!*  
第二個說他是未名中學畢業的，學校旁邊的未名河畔是他初戀的地方。  
The second says he went to Unnamed Middle School, and his first love was on the banks of Unnamed River by the school.  
我也是耶！第一個非常激動。  
*Me too!* The first gets very emotional.  
兩人虎目含淚數秒後便緊緊擁抱，兩個炎黃子孫在夷狄之邦異地相逢，  
After a few seconds of teary-eyed staring, they embrace tightly — two descendants of the Yellow Emperor meeting in a foreign land,  
真是他鄉遇故知啊！兩人都嚷著今天一定要讓我請客。  
what a joy to meet a friend far from home! Both then insist: *Today, let me treat you!*  
可是繼續談下去才發覺一個住北京，另一個住上海。  
But as they keep talking, they realize one lives in Beijing and the other in Shanghai.  
最後在北京人說：上海人特現實、上海人說：北京人最頑固的聲音中，  
In the end, as the Beijinger says *Shanghainese are so pragmatic* and the Shanghainese says *Beijingers are so stubborn*,  
夕陽緩緩西沉了，而且兩人都沒付酒錢。  
the sun sets slowly, and neither pays the bill.  

「還沒說完呀。」暖暖停下腳步，回頭瞪我一眼。  
"Still not done?" Nuannuan stopped and turned to glare at me.  
『剩一點點，再忍耐一下。』我說。  
"Just a bit more — bear with me."  
「快說。」  
"Hurry up."  
『既然無名，也就無爭。』我說，『未名二字似乎提醒著所有北大學生要  
"Being nameless means being without contention," I said. "The name *Weiming* seems to remind every PKU student to  
淡泊名利、寬厚無爭。我想這才是錢穆先生的本意吧。』  
be indifferent to fame and fortune, to be generous and non-contentious. I think that was Qian Mu's true intention."  
「這才像句人話。」暖暖笑了。  
"That sounds almost human," Nuannuan said with a smile.  

『如果在這裡唸書，應該很容易交到女朋友。』我說。  
"If I studied here, I'd probably have an easy time getting a girlfriend," I said.  
「嗯？」  
"Mm?"  
『我母校也有座湖，不到十分鐘便可走一圈。但跟女孩散步十分鐘哪夠？  
"My school also has a lake — you can walk around it in under ten minutes. But ten minutes of walking with a girl is nowhere near enough.  
只好繼續繞第二圈、第三圈、第四圈……』我嘆口氣，接著說：  
So you have to go around again: second lap, third lap, fourth lap…" I sighed.  
『最後女孩終於受不了說：別再帶著我繞圈圈了！分手吧！別來找我了！  
"Finally the girl can't take it anymore and says: *Stop dragging me in circles! We're through! Don't come find me!*  
三個驚嘆號便結束一段戀情。』  
Three exclamation marks — and the relationship is over."  

「那為何未名湖會讓人交到女朋友？」暖暖問。  
"So how does Weiming Lake help people get girlfriends?" Nuannuan asked.  
『這未名湖又大又美，青年男女下課後在這散步得走上半天。走著走著，  
"This lake is big and beautiful. A young couple taking a stroll after class takes ages to walk around it. As they walk,  
男的便說：我願化成雄壯挺拔的博雅塔，而妳就像溫柔多情的未名湖，  
the guy says: *I want to be the majestic Boya Pagoda, and you'll be the tender, passionate Weiming Lake —  
我寸步不移，只想將我的身影永遠映在妳心海。湖可能還沒走上一半，  
*I won't move an inch, I just want my reflection to stay forever in the sea of your heart.* Before they've even walked halfway around,  
一對戀人就產生了。』  
a couple is born."  
「哪會這麼簡單。」暖暖的語氣顯得不以為然。  
"It can't be that easy," she said skeptically.  

『如果男的說：我們一定要永遠在一起，長長久久、不離不棄；不管風、  
"What if the guy says: *We have to stay together forever, through the long years, never abandoning each other; through wind,*  
不管雨、也不管打雷閃電。英法聯軍燒得掉圓明園，卻毀不了我心中的  
*through rain, through thunder and lightning. The British and French could burn down the Old Summer Palace, but they can't destroy the*  
石頭，因為那塊堅貞的石頭上刻了妳的名字。」我問，「這樣如何？」  
*stone in my heart, because on that steadfast stone is carved your name.*" I asked. "How's that?"  
「太煽情了。」暖暖說，「你再試試。」  
"Too cheesy," she said. "Try again."  
我歪著頭想了半天，擠不出半句話。  
I tilted my head and thought for a long time, but couldn't come up with anything.  
「想不出來了吧。」暖暖笑了笑，「我可以耳根清淨了。」  
"Out of ideas, huh?" She smiled. "My ears get a break now."  
『反正湖夠大，得走很久。』我說，『在如詩般的美景走久了，泥人也會  
"Well, the lake's big enough that you'd walk for a long time," I said. "Walk long enough in a scene like a poem, even a clay statue  
沾上三分詩意。』  
would pick up some poetry."  
「是你就不會，你只會更瞎說。」暖暖說。  
"Not you — you'd just talk more nonsense."  

約莫再走十五分鐘，博雅塔已近在眼前。  
After about another fifteen minutes, Boya Pagoda was right in front of us.  
博雅塔是仿通州燃燈古塔的樣子而建造的，塔級十三，高37公尺。  
The pagoda was modeled after the ancient Randeng Pagoda in Tongzhou — thirteen stories, 37 meters tall.  
「同學們猜猜看，這塔是幹啥用的？」李老師指著塔問。  
"Any guesses what this pagoda is used for?" Teacher Li asked, pointing.  
大夥開始議論紛紛，有人說塔通常建於佛寺內，建在校園內很怪；  
Everyone started discussing. Some said pagodas were usually in Buddhist temples, so having one on campus was odd;  
也有人說該不會像雷峰塔鎮壓著白娘子一樣，這裡也壓著某種妖怪？  
others wondered if, like Leifeng Pagoda holding down Lady White Snake, this one held down some kind of demon.  

最後李老師公佈答案：它是座水塔，一座以寶塔外型偽裝的自來水塔。  
Finally, Teacher Li revealed the answer: it's a water tower — a water tower disguised as a pagoda.  
博雅塔建於20年代，此後即默默站在湖畔，供應北大師生的生活用水。  
Built in the 1920s, it had stood silently by the lake ever since, providing water for PKU's faculty and students.  
我抬頭仰望高聳入雲霄的博雅塔，它似乎飽經風霜，周圍只有松柏相伴。  
I looked up at the tower, which seemed weathered by time, with only pines and cypresses for company.  
「一座充滿藝術文化之美的建築，可以只扮演簡單的角色；換個角度說，  
"A building full of artistic and cultural beauty can serve a simple role; or to put it another way,  
一個看似卑微的供水工作者，他的內心也可以充滿藝術文化氣息。」  
"a humble water-supply worker can have a heart full of art and culture."  
李老師說，「以前我在北大唸書時，常來這裡沉思，每次都有所得。」  
"When I was a student here, I often came to reflect, and I always gained something."  

離開博雅塔，我們轉向南，暫別未名湖，準備前往上課的地方。  
Leaving the pagoda, we turned south, temporarily saying goodbye to Weiming Lake, heading to where our class would be held.  
『未名湖真美。』我回頭再看了未名湖一眼，說：『但跟妳走在一起時，  
"Weiming Lake is beautiful," I said, turning back for one more look. "But walking with you,  
卻覺得未名湖也只是一般而已。』  
I feel like Weiming Lake is just ordinary."  
暖暖突然停下腳步。  
Nuannuan stopped short.  
而我話一出口便覺異樣，也停下腳步。  
And the moment I said it, I realized something was off — so I stopped too.  
同學們漸漸走遠，我和暖暖還待在原地。  
The other students gradually drifted away, but Nuannuan and I stayed put.  
「學長！」學弟轉頭朝我大喊：「別想溜啊！」  
"Senior!" my junior called back. "Don't even think about ditching!"  

我不知道怎麼會脫口說出這些話？  
I didn't know why those words had come out.  
是因為腦海裡幻想著青年男女在未名湖應有的對話？  
Was it because I'd been imagining the kind of dialogue a young couple should have by Weiming Lake?  
或是我心裡一直覺得暖暖很美於是不自覺跟未名湖的美景相比？  
Or because I'd always thought Nuannuan was beautiful and unconsciously compared her to the lake?  
還是兩者都是，只因我把青年男女想像成我和暖暖？  
Or both — because I was imagining the young couple as Nuannuan and me?  
「這是我剛剛叫你試試的問題的答案？』暖暖終於開口。  
"Is that the answer to the question I asked you to try?" Nuannuan finally said.  
『算是吧。不過……嗯……』我回答，『我也不確定。』  
"Sort of. But… um…" I answered. "I'm not sure."  
氣氛並沒有因為我和暖暖都已開口而改變。  
The atmosphere didn't change, even though we'd both spoken.  

「學長。」學弟跑過來，說：「我們來玩海帶拳。」  
"Senior." My junior ran over. "Let's play seaweed fist."  
『幹嘛？』我說。  
"What?"  
「海帶呀海帶……」學弟雙手大開，像大鵬展翅，手臂模擬海帶飄動。  
"Seaweed ah seaweed…" He spread his arms wide like a roc, waving them to mimic seaweed.  
『你少無聊。』我說。  
"Stop being childish," I said.  
「海帶呀海帶……」  
"Seaweed ah seaweed—"  
學弟高舉雙手，手臂正想向上飄動時，我敲了他頭，說：『你還來！』  
He raised his arms, but just as he was about to wave upward, I knocked him on the head. "Still at it!"  
學弟邊狂笑邊跑走，暖暖也笑出聲。  
He ran off laughing hysterically, and Nuannuan laughed too.  
「咱們跟上唄。」暖暖說完後便往前小跑步。  
"Let's catch up," she said, and jogged ahead.  
我也小跑步，跟上了暖暖，然後跟上了隊伍。  
I jogged too, caught up with her, and then caught up with the group.  

穿過五四大道，看到一座建於晚清年間的四合院，門上寫著：治貝子園。  
Crossing May Fourth Avenue, we saw a late-Qing courtyard house with a plaque reading: Prince Bei's Garden.  
門口還有尊老子石雕立像，高約兩公尺。  
A two-meter-tall stone statue of Laozi stood at the entrance.  
內院是古色古香的小庭院，處處顯得古樸而典雅。  
Inside was an old-world courtyard, rustic and elegant in every detail.  
『今天在這上課？』我問暖暖。  
"Class here today?" I asked Nuannuan.  
「聽說是。」暖暖說。  
"So I hear."  
『嗯。』我點點頭，『這裡跟我的風格很搭。』  
"Mm." I nodded. "Fits my style perfectly."  
暖暖笑彎了腰，好像剛聽到一個五星級的笑話。  
Nuannuan doubled over laughing, as if she'd just heard a five-star joke.  

今天上課的老師一頭白髮，但臉上沒半點鬍渣，講的是老莊思想。  
Today's teacher had a full head of white hair but not a trace of stubble; he lectured on Laozi and Zhuangzi.  
從《道德經》第一章「道可道，非常道；名可名，非常名」開始講起。  
He started with the first chapter of the *Dao De Jing*: "The Dao that can be spoken of is not the eternal Dao; the name that can be named is not the eternal name."  
「道」是可以說的，但可以用言語來表述的道，就不是永恆不變的道；  
The Dao can be spoken of, but the Dao expressed in words is not the eternal, unchanging Dao.  
萬事萬物面目之描述——「名」，也是可以被定義的，  
The appearance of all things — the "name" — can also be defined.  
然而一旦被清楚定義，則萬事萬物的本來面目便不可能被真實描述。  
Yet once clearly defined, the true nature of all things can no longer be accurately described.  
嗯，好深奧。  
Mm. Very deep.  

通常如果聽到這種深奧的課，我都會利用這段時間養精蓄銳。  
Usually when I hear such profound lectures, I use the time to recharge.  
但能在這樣的地方上課是畢生難得的經驗，我的好奇心便輕易擊潰睡意。  
But having class in a place like this was a once-in-a-lifetime experience, and my curiosity easily beat back my drowsiness.  
偷偷打量教室四周，屋上的樑、地下的磚都泛著歷史的痕跡。  
I snuck a look around the room — the beams overhead and the bricks underfoot all bore traces of history.  
空氣的味道也不一樣，有一種淡淡的香味，說不上來。  
Even the air smelled different — a faint fragrance I couldn't place.  

我在暖暖面前的紙上寫著：有沒有聞到一股特殊的香味？  
I wrote on the paper in front of her: *Do you smell something special?*  
暖暖聞了聞後，也在我面前的紙上寫著：沒。是啥味？  
She sniffed and wrote back: *No. What?*  
我又寫：這種味道跟我身上很像。  
I wrote: *Smells like me.*  
暖暖寫：？  
She wrote: *?*  
我寫：那叫書香。  
I wrote: *It's called the scent of books.*  
暖暖寫：閉嘴！  
She wrote: *Shut up!*  
我寫：但我是用手寫的。  
I wrote: *But I'm writing with my hand.*  
暖暖寫：那就住手！  
She wrote: *Then stop your hand!*  

快下課前，老師說人的本性就像一塊埋在心底深處的玉，只露出一小點。  
Near the end of class, the teacher said human nature is like a piece of jade buried deep in the heart, only a tiny bit exposed.  
每個人必須一點一滴去挖掘埋藏在心中的玉石，挖出它、琢磨它。  
Everyone must dig out the jade buried within, bit by bit — excavate it, polish it.  
這便是尋求自我發揮本性的過程。  
This is the process of seeking and expressing one's true nature.  
「要努力挖掘自我。」老師以這句當作課堂結尾。  
"Strive to excavate yourself." He ended the class with these words.  

「你挖到自己了嗎？」離開治貝子園後，暖暖問。  
"Have you excavated yourself yet?" Nuannuan asked as we left Prince Bei's Garden.  
『挖可挖，非常挖。不如不挖也。』我說，『這是道家。』  
"Excavatable excavating, not the eternal excavating. Better not to excavate at all," I said. "That's Daoist."  
「還有別的嗎？」暖暖說。  
"Anything else?"  
『挖即是空，空即是挖。這是佛家。』我說。  
"Excavating is emptiness, emptiness is excavating. That's Buddhist."  
「再來呢？」  
"More?"  
『志士仁人，無硬挖以害仁，有不挖以成仁。這是儒家。』  
"Men of ambition and humanity — none force-excavate to harm humanity; some non-excavate to achieve humanity. That's Confucian."  
暖暖嘆口氣，說：「瞎說好像就是你的本質。」  
She sighed. "Nonsense seems to be your true nature."

因此漫步於長廊內既可欣賞美景，也可避免日曬雨淋。  
Strolling here, you can enjoy the scenery while being sheltered from sun and rain.  
頤和園長廊南面昆明湖，北靠萬壽山，東起邀月門，西至石丈亭；  
The Long Corridor faces Kunming Lake to the south, rests against Longevity Hill to the north, runs from the Inviting-the-Moon Gate in the east to the Stone Pavilion in the west —  
全長728公尺，每四根柱子隔為一間，總共273間。  
728 meters long, divided into 273 bays of four columns each.  
每間的柱子上半部安裝橫木，下半部則設置木製坐凳欄杆。  
The upper half of each bay's columns had horizontal beams; the lower half had wooden bench railings for sitting.

Every beam and architrave in the Long Corridor was covered with vivid paintings — over 14,000 in total, none identical.  
這些彩繪是蘇式彩繪，大體可分為人物、山水、花鳥、建築風景四大類。  
These were Suzhou-style paintings, broadly divided into four categories: figures, landscapes, flowers-and-birds, and architecture.  
而長廊也以建築獨特、繪畫豐富，被譽為世界上最長的畫廊。  
The Long Corridor, with its unique architecture and rich paintings, is known as the world's longest art gallery.  
在長廊中漫步，彷彿走進一座別緻典雅的彩繪畫廊；  
Walking here felt like stepping into an elegant painted gallery.  
每個人也似乎化身成一條魚，在畫境之中優游。  
Everyone seemed to transform into a fish, swimming freely through a world of paintings.  
長廊內的彩繪與長廊外的山水花木、亭台樓閣相映成趣，令人目不暇給。  
The paintings inside and the landscapes, flowers, pavilions outside complemented each other — a feast for the eyes.  
如果走累了，可隨時在兩旁木凳坐下。坐著欣賞彩繪，也是一派悠閒。  
If you got tired, you could sit on the wooden benches and admire the paintings at leisure.

百年前這裡是全世界服務最好的商家，因為顧客上門店員都是跪著迎接。  
A hundred years ago, this was the world's best-serviced shopping street — customers were greeted on their knees.

Nuannuan and I strolled along; trees stood in groves in the distance, willows drooped by the riverbank, small boats glided on the water.  
若不是偶見的告示牌提醒遊人小心腳下別跌入河中，  
If not for the occasional sign warning tourists not to fall into the river,  
一切都讓人彷彿置身於十八世紀的世外桃源。

Seeing a white flag with red trim reading "Money House," we stepped inside out of curiosity.

Sitting on the second floor, looking down at the little bridges and winding water, we wondered if ancient Jiangnan really looked like this.
