# 《暖暖》第十三章中英对照

说明：
- 本文件改编自蔡智恒（痞子蔡）的小说《暖暖》。
- 排版规则为"一句中文，一句英文"。

我在雪地裡站了許久，暖暖才推了推我，說：「快回飯店，會凍著的。」
I stood in the snow for a long time before she pushed me. "Hurry back to the hotel — you'll freeze."
回程的路上，雪持續下著，街景染上白，樹也白了頭。
On the way back, the snow kept falling, painting the streets white and powdering the treetops.
我想嚐嚐雪的味道，便仰起頭張開嘴巴，伸出舌頭。
I wanted to taste the snow, so I tilted my head back, opened my mouth, and stuck out my tongue.
「唉呀，別丟人了。」暖暖笑著說：「像條狗似的。」
"Oh stop, you're embarrassing me," she laughed. "Like a dog."

『我記得去年一起逛小吃一條街時，妳也這麼說過我。』我說。
"I remember you said the same thing last year when we were on Snack Street," I said.
「是呀。」暖暖說，「你一點也沒變。」
"Yeah," she said. "You haven't changed at all."
『不，我變了。』我說，『從小狗長成大狗了。』
"No, I have changed," I said. "From a puppy to a full-grown dog."
暖暖簡單笑了笑，沒多說什麼。
She smiled briefly, saying nothing more.

暖暖還得把車開回單位去，然後再回家。
She still had to return the car to her workplace, then go home.
「明天中午，我來找你吃飯。」暖暖一上車便說。
"I'll come find you for lunch tomorrow," she said as she got in the car.
『所以是明天見？』我說，『而不是再見？』
"So it's *see you tomorrow*," I said, "not *goodbye*?"
「當然是明天見。」暖暖笑了笑，便開車走了。
"Of course it's *see you tomorrow*." She smiled and drove off.
簡單一句明天見，讓我從車子起動笑到車子消失於視線。
Those simple words had me smiling from the moment her car started until it disappeared from sight.

我進了飯店房間，打開落地窗，搬了張椅子到小陽台。
I went to my room, opened the balcony door, and moved a chair out to the small balcony.
泡了杯熱茶，靠躺在椅子上，欣賞雪景。
I made a cup of hot tea, leaned back in the chair, and watched the snow.
之前從沒見過雪，也不知道這樣的雪是大還是小？
I'd never seen snow before — I couldn't tell if this was heavy or light.
突然有股吟詩的衝動，不禁開口吟出：『雪落……』
A sudden urge to recite poetry came over me. I began: "Snow falls…"
只吟了兩字便停，因為接不下去。四下一看，還好沒人。
Two words in, I stopped — couldn't continue. I looked around; fortunately no one was watching.
我果然不是詩人的材料，遇見難得的美景也無法成詩。
I really wasn't poet material — couldn't turn even this rare beauty into verse.

想起該給徐馳打個電話，便撥了通電話給徐馳。
I remembered I should call Xu Chi and dialed his number.
徐馳說20分鐘到，在飯店大堂等我，見了面再說。
He said he'd be there in 20 minutes and to wait in the hotel lobby.
20分鐘後我下了樓，一出電梯便看見徐馳坐在大堂的沙發椅上。
Twenty minutes later I went down; the moment the elevator opened, I saw him on the lobby sofa.
「老蔡！」徐馳站起身，張開雙臂，「來，抱一個。」
"Old Cai!" He stood up, arms open. "Come here — give me a hug."
唉，如果這句話由暖暖口中說出，那該有多好。
Sigh — if only she'd said that instead.

跟徐馳來個熱情的擁抱後，他說：「晚來天欲雪，能飲一杯無？」
After a warm hug, he quoted: "*The evening sky wants snow — can you drink a cup?*"
『一杯可以。』我笑了笑，『兩杯就醉了。』
"One cup is fine," I laughed. "Two and I'd be drunk."
徐馳在飯店門口叫輛計程車，我們直奔什剎海的荷花市場。
Xu Chi hailed a taxi at the hotel entrance, and we headed straight to Shichahai's Lotus Market.
我和暖暖去年夏日午後曾在湖畔漫步，但現在是冬夜，而且還是雪夜。
She and I had walked by the lake on a summer afternoon last year — but now it was a snowy winter night.
片片雪花緩緩灑在什剎海上，沒有半點聲響，也不留下絲毫痕跡。
Snowflakes fell slowly onto Shichahai, soundless, leaving no trace.
想起昨天在杭州西湖遊覽時，總聽人說：晴西湖不如雨西湖；
I remembered at West Lake yesterday, people kept saying: *Sunny West Lake isn't as good as rainy West Lake;*
雨西湖不如夜西湖；夜西湖不如雪西湖。那麼雪夜的西湖一定最美吧？
*rainy West Lake isn't as good as night West Lake; night West Lake isn't as good as snowy West Lake.* Snowy West Lake must be the most beautiful of all.
而什剎海是否也是如此？
And Shichahai — was it the same?

荷花市場古色古香的牌坊，孤傲地立在繽紛的霓虹燈之間；
The ancient-style archway of Lotus Market stood proudly amid the neon lights.
充滿異國情調的酒吧，在滿是古老中國風的湖畔開業，人聲鼎沸。
Exotic bars lined the old Chinese lakeside, bustling with patrons.
客人多半是老外，來此體驗中國風味，又可享受時髦的夜生活。
Most customers were foreigners, here to experience Chinese flavor and enjoy trendy nightlife.
北京這千歲老頭，筋骨是否受得了這折騰？
This thousand-year-old city — could its old bones take all this?

徐馳一坐下來，便滔滔不絕講起自身的事。
The moment Xu Chi sat down, he launched into a nonstop account of his own life.
我們一邊喝酒，一邊聊起過去、現在，以及將來。
We drank and talked about the past, present, and future.
我發覺徐馳的衣著和口吻都變成熟了，人看起來也變得老成。
I noticed Xu Chi's clothes and manner had matured; he looked more seasoned.
「差點忘了。」徐馳突然說，「高亮今天到武漢出差去了，臨走前交代我
"Almost forgot," he said suddenly. "Gao Liang went on a business trip to Wuhan today. Before leaving, he told me
跟你說聲抱歉，只得下回再帶你爬司馬台長城了。」
to say sorry — he'll have to take you to Simatai next time."
說完便從包裡拿出三張照片放在桌上，然後說：「高亮給你的。」
He pulled three photos from his bag and placed them on the table. "From Gao Liang."

這三張照片其實是同一張，只是有大、中、小三種尺寸。
They were all the same photo, just in three sizes — large, medium, and small.
大的幾乎有海報大小；中的約十吋寬；小的只約半個巴掌大。
The big one was almost poster-sized; the medium about ten inches wide; the small one half a palm.
都是暖暖在八達嶺長城北七樓所留下的影像。
All showed Nuannuan at the North Seventh Tower of Badaling.
暖暖筆直站著，雙手各比個V，臉上盡是燦爛的笑。
She stood straight, flashing Vs with both hands, her face full of radiant smiles.

「高亮說了，大的貼牆上，中的擺桌上，小的放皮夾裡。」徐馳笑了笑。
"Gao Liang said: big one on the wall, medium on your desk, small one in your wallet," Xu Chi smiled.
高亮的相機和技術都很好，暖暖的神韻躍然紙上。
Gao Liang's camera and skills were excellent — her spirit shone through the paper.
我滿是驚喜並充滿感激。
I was full of surprise and gratitude.
「來。」徐馳說，「咱們哥倆為高亮喝一杯。」
"Come," he said. "Let's drink one to Gao Liang."
『一杯哪夠？』我說，『起碼得三杯。』
"One's not enough," I said. "At least three."
「行！」徐馳拍拍胸口，「就三杯！」
"Deal!" He slapped his chest. "Three it is!"

我立刻將小張照片收進皮夾，再小心翼翼捲好大張照片，輕輕綁好。
I immediately put the small one in my wallet, carefully rolled up the large one, and tied it gently.
中的則先放我座位旁，陪我坐著。
The medium one I kept by my seat, keeping me company.
又跟徐馳喝了一會後，我發覺他已滿臉通紅、眼神迷濛，大概醉了。
After another round, he was flushed and glassy-eyed — probably drunk.
想起他明天還得上班，便問：「馳哥，你家住哪？」
I remembered he had work tomorrow. "Brother Chi, where do you live?"
「我家住在黃土高坡，大風從坡上颳過，不管是西北風還是東南風，都是
"My home's on the Loess Plateau, where big winds blow across the slopes — whether northwest wind or southeast wind, they're all
我的歌我的歌……」
my song, my song…"
徐馳高聲唱著歌。
He sang loudly.

我心想徐馳應該醉翻了，又試一次：『你在北京住哪？』
He was definitely wasted. I tried again: "Where do you live in Beijing?"
「我家住在黃土高坡，日頭從坡上走過，照著我窯洞曬著我的胳膊，還有
"My home's on the Loess Plateau, where the sun crosses the slopes, shining on my cave house and my arms, and
我的牛跟著我……」
my cow follows me…"
徐馳還是高聲唱著歌。
Still singing.

我扶起徐馳，叫了輛計程車送我們回台灣飯店。
I helped him up, hailed a taxi back to the Taiwan Hotel.
徐馳早就睡得不省人事，只得將他拖上我的房間，扔在床上。
He was dead asleep; I had to drag him to my room and dump him on the bed.
簡單洗個熱水澡，洗完走出浴室時，徐馳已鼾聲大作。
After a quick hot shower, he was already snoring loudly.
看了看錶，已快凌晨一點，搖了搖徐馳，一點反應也沒。
I checked my watch — almost 1 AM. I shook him; no response.
反正是張雙人床，今晚就跟徐馳一起睡吧。
It was a double bed anyway — I'd just share with him.

打了通電話給飯店櫃台，請他們早上六點半morning call。
I called the front desk for a 6:30 AM wake-up call.
以前在台灣時，聽人說大陸上把morning call翻成叫床，很有趣。
Back in Taiwan, I'd heard that on the mainland they translate "morning call" as *jiaochuang* — literally "call bed" — pretty funny.
記得去年教漢字的老師說過，漢字順著唸也行、倒著唸也可以。
I remembered the character teacher last year saying Chinese can be read forward or backward.
大陸是順著唸，所以叫床的意思是「叫你起床」；
On the mainland, reading forward, *jiaochuang* means "call you to get up."
但台灣是倒著唸，叫床的意思就變成「在床上叫」。
But in Taiwan, reading backward, it means "making sounds in bed."

昨天在杭州西湖邊，晚上回蘇州，今早應該從蘇州到上海再回台灣；
Yesterday at West Lake, back to Suzhou at night. This morning I was supposed to go Suzhou-Shanghai-Taiwan.
沒想到因為一念之差，現在卻躺在北京的飯店床上。
But here I was, lying in a Beijing hotel bed, all because of a whim.
回想這段時間內的奔波與心情轉折，疲憊感迅速蔓延全身，便沉沉睡去。
Thinking back on all the rushing and emotional twists, exhaustion spread through me, and I fell into deep sleep.

六點半morning call的電話聲同時吵醒我和徐馳。
The 6:30 wake-up call woke us both.
徐馳見和我一起躺在床上，先是大驚，隨即想起昨夜的事，便哈哈大笑。
Seeing me in the same bed, he was startled at first, then remembered last night and burst out laughing.
他簡單漱洗後，便急著上班。
He washed up quickly and hurried off to work.
「還是那句老話。」徐馳說，「以後到北京，一定得通知我。」
"Same as before," he said. "Whenever you come to Beijing, you have to let me know."
說完又跟我來個熱情的擁抱。
Then another warm hug.
徐馳剛打開門，又回頭說：「老蔡，加油。」
As he opened the door, he turned back. "Old Cai — keep it up."
我知道徐馳話裡的意思，便點點頭表示收到。
I knew what he meant and nodded to show I understood.

徐馳走後，我又繼續睡。
After he left, I went back to sleep.
作了個奇怪的夢，夢裡出現一個山頭，清軍的大砲正往山下猛轟；
I had a strange dream. In it, a hillside appeared — Qing soldiers were firing cannons downhill.
砲台左右兩旁各趴著一列民兵，拿著槍瞄準射擊。
Militia lay on both sides of the cannon platform, rifles aimed and firing.
而山下有十幾隊法軍正往山上進攻。
Below, French troops were advancing up the hill.
我和暖暖在山頭漫步，經過清軍砲台，我告訴暖暖：『這裡就是暖暖。』
Nuannuan and I were strolling on the hilltop. Passing the cannon platform, I told her: "This is Nuannuan."
「你終究還是帶我來暖暖了。」暖暖笑得很燦爛。
"You finally brought me to Nuannuan," she said, beaming.

砲聲隆隆中，隱約傳來尖銳的鈴聲。
Amid the thunder of cannons, I faintly heard a sharp ringing.
好像是拍戰爭片的現場突然響起手機鈴聲，於是導演氣得大叫：「卡！」
Like a cell phone going off on a war movie set — the director yelling: "Cut!"
我被這鈴聲吵醒，花了幾秒鐘才意識到應該是門鈴聲。
The ringing woke me. It took a few seconds to realize it was the doorbell.
我迷迷糊糊走到門邊，打開房門。
Half asleep, I stumbled to the door and opened it.
「還在睡？」暖暖說，「都快中午了。」
"Still sleeping?" she said. "It's almost noon."

我全身的細胞瞬間清醒，法軍也被打跑了。
Every cell in my body snapped awake. The French army was routed too.
『啊？』我嘴巴張得好大，『這……』
"Ah?" My mouth hung open. "This—"
「你是讓我站在這兒？」暖暖笑了笑，「還是在樓下大堂等你？」
"Are you going to leave me standing here?" she smiled. "Or shall I wait in the lobby?"
我趕緊把門拉開，暖暖進來後直接坐在沙發上。
I quickly pulled the door open. She came in and sat on the sofa.
我開始後悔，現在正是兵荒馬亂，暖暖會看笑話的。
I immediately regretted this — the room was a disaster zone. She'd laugh at me.
「慢慢來。」暖暖說，「別急。」
"Take your time," she said. "No rush."

我臉一紅，趕緊衝進浴室，三分鐘內把該做的事搞定。
Blushing, I rushed into the bathroom and took care of business in three minutes.
昨晚因為怕徐馳獸性大發，所以穿了襯衫和長褲睡覺。
I'd slept in my shirt and pants last night, afraid Xu Chi might get any ideas.
沒被暖暖瞧見胸部肌肉和腿部線條，真是好險。
Good thing she didn't see my chest and legs — that would've been embarrassing.
『走吧。』我說。
"Let's go," I said.
「你就穿這樣出門？」暖暖說，「外頭可是零度。」
"You're going out like that?" she said. "It's below zero out there."
在室內暖氣房待久了，一時忘了現在是北京的冬天。
I'd been in the heated room so long I'd forgotten it was winter in Beijing.
趕緊套了件毛衣，拿起外套，暖暖這才起身。
I quickly threw on a sweater and grabbed my coat. Only then did she stand up.

進了電梯，湊巧遇見昨晚在東來順的外國老夫婦。
In the elevator, we ran into the elderly foreign couple from last night.
老先生跟我們打聲招呼後，問：「honeymoon？」
The old man greeted us and asked, "Honeymoon?"
『just lover。』我說。
"Just lover," I said.
「friend！」暖暖急著否認，「We are just friends！」
"Friend!" she quickly denied. "We are just friends!"
老夫婦笑了，我也笑了，只有暖暖跺著腳。
The old couple laughed; I laughed; only she was stomping her foot.

一出電梯，暖暖遞過來一樣東西，說：「給。」
As we left the elevator, she handed me something. "Here."
我接過來，發現是條深灰色的圍巾。
I took it — a dark gray scarf.
「外頭冷。」暖暖說，「待會出去先圍上。」
"It's cold out," she said. "Put it on before we go out."
圍上圍巾走出飯店，突然想起今天還是上班的日子。
I wrapped the scarf around my neck and walked out of the hotel, suddenly remembering it was still a workday.
『暖暖。』我說，『如果妳忙，我可以理解的。』
"Nuannuan," I said. "If you're busy, I understand."
暖暖停下腳步，轉頭看著我說：「難道你現在放假嗎？」
She stopped and turned to look at me. "Are you on vacation right now?"
我楞了楞，沒有答話。
I was caught off guard and didn't answer.
「走唄。」暖暖笑了笑。
"Let's go," she smiled.

跟暖暖並肩走了幾步，心裡還是擔心會誤了暖暖上班的事。
A few steps in, I was still worried about her work.
「涼涼。」暖暖又停下腳步，「當我心情不好時，就希望有個巨大濾網，
"Liangliang." She stopped again. "When I'm in a bad mood, I wish I had a giant filter —
將自己身上煩惱呀憂愁呀等等負面情緒徹底給濾掉，只剩純粹的我。」
*something to filter out all the worries and sadness — all the negative emotions — leaving only the pure me.*"
說完後暖暖便用手在面前先畫了個大方框，再畫許多條交叉的線。
She drew a big square in the air with her hands, then many crossing lines.
「這麼大的網，夠兩個人用了。」暖暖說，「咱們一起跳。」
"This big — big enough for two," she said. "Let's jump in together."
我點了點頭，暖暖數一、二、三，我們便一起縱身飛越暖暖畫下的網。
I nodded. She counted one, two, three, and we leaped together over the net she'd drawn.
暖暖笑得很開心，我也笑了。
She laughed happily; I laughed too.

上了暖暖的車，還是那輛單位的白色車。
We got in her car — still the company's white one.
雪雖然停了，但街景像伍子胥過昭關——一夜之間白了頭。
The snow had stopped, but the streets looked like Wu Zixu crossing the Zhao Pass — turned white overnight.
仿古建築的屋瓦上積了厚厚的雪，樹枝上、地上也是，到處都是。
Thick snow covered the antique-style roofs, the branches, the ground — everywhere.
北京變得好潔白，充滿清新和寧靜的美。
Beijing had turned pure white — fresh and serene.
但路上行人匆匆，沒人停下腳步讚嘆。
But pedestrians hurried past, no one stopping to admire it.
『暖暖。』我終於忍不住了，『可以停下車嗎？』
"Nuannuan." I couldn't hold it in anymore. "Can you pull over?"

暖暖靠邊剛停下車，我立刻打開車門，跑進一塊空曠的雪地。
She pulled over. I immediately opened the door and ran into an open patch of snow.
我蹲下身雙手各抓了一把雪，感覺肩膀有些顫抖。
I crouched and scooped up snow with both hands, my shoulders trembling.
「咋了？」暖暖在我身後問。
"What's wrong?" she asked from behind.
我轉過身，向她攤開雙手，笑了笑說：『是雪耶！』
I turned to her, held out my hands, and smiled. "It's snow!"
暖暖露出無奈的表情。
She looked resigned.

我開始在雪地裡翻滾，越滾越開心。
I started rolling in the snow, the more I rolled the happier I got.
「別丟人了，快起來！」暖暖說。
"Stop embarrassing me — get up!" she said.
我停止滾動，躺了下來，雪地柔柔軟軟的，好舒服。
I stopped rolling and lay there. The snow was soft and comfortable.
「把你扔這兒不管你了！」暖暖又說。
"I'll leave you here!" she added.
我雙手又各抓了一把雪，站起身走到暖暖面前，攤開手說：『是雪耶！』
I scooped up more snow, walked to her, and held out my hands. "It's snow!"
暖暖不知道是該生氣還是該笑，只說了聲：「喂。」
She didn't know whether to be angry or amused. Just said: "Hey."

『讓我在雪地裡游個泳吧。』我說完便趴下身。
"Let me swim in the snow." I lay face down.

坐上計程車，我和暖暖直奔北京火車站。
We took a taxi straight to Beijing Railway Station.
車站好大，人潮非常擁擠，暖暖帶著我繞來繞去才走進月台。
The station was huge and crowded. She led me through a maze to the platform.
台灣的鐵路軌道是窄軌，這裡的軌道寬一些，應該是標準軌。
Taiwan's rails are narrow gauge; here they were wider — standard gauge, probably.
上了火車，找到我們的包廂，拉開門一看，左右各上下兩層床鋪。
We boarded the train, found our compartment, and slid the door open: upper and lower berths on both sides.
門的對面是一整塊玻璃窗，窗前有張小桌子。
Opposite the door was a large window with a small table in front.
門的上方有一個可置放大型行李的空間。
Above the door was space for large luggage.

我和暖暖在左右兩邊的下鋪坐了下來，兩人膝蓋間的距離不到一人寬。
We sat on the lower berths, our knees less than a person's width apart.
一對中年夫婦拖著一個笨重的行李箱走進來，先生先爬到上鋪，
A middle-aged couple dragged in a heavy suitcase. The husband climbed to the upper berth;
我在下面托高行李箱，先生接住，把它放進門上的空間。
I lifted the suitcase from below; he took it and stowed it above the door.
「謝謝。」他說。
"Thanks," he said.
『沒事。』我說。
"No problem."
服務員也進來了，說了聲晚上好，給我們每人一包東西便離開。
The attendant came in, said good evening, gave us each a pack, and left.
裡頭有紙拖鞋、牙刷牙膏肥皂、沾水後便可揉成毛巾的塊狀物，
Inside were paper slippers, toiletries, a compressed towel that expanded with water,
還有一小包花生米。
and a small pack of peanuts.

我和暖暖把鞋脫了，換上紙拖鞋，坐在下鋪吃花生米。
We took off our shoes, put on the slippers, and sat on the lower berth eating peanuts.
床上有個10吋左右的液晶螢幕，可收看幾個頻道，但收視效果不怎麼好。
There was a small LCD screen on the wall — a few channels, but poor reception.
折騰了一下午，現在終於可以喘口氣，甚至有開始旅行的感覺。
After a hectic afternoon, we could finally breathe. It almost felt like the start of a trip.
低沉的砰隆一聲，火車起動了，我和暖暖都笑了。
With a low rumble, the train started moving. We both smiled.
問了暖暖軟臥硬臥的差別，是否在於床鋪的軟與硬？
I asked her the difference between soft and hard sleepers — was it the hardness of the bed?
暖暖說床鋪沒差多少，但硬臥包廂內左右各上中下三層，一間有六個人。
She said the beds weren't much different, but hard sleepers had three tiers per side — six people per compartment.

「咱們去吃飯唄。」暖暖站起身。
"Let's go eat," she said, standing.
『嗯。』我也站起身。
"Mm," I stood too.
我們穿過幾節車廂來到餐車，火車行駛很平穩，一路走來沒什麼搖晃。
We walked through a few carriages to the dining car. The ride was smooth, barely any swaying.
餐車內很多人，我和暖暖找了個位子坐下，叫了兩碗麵。
The dining car was crowded. We found seats and ordered two bowls of noodles.
位子很小，我和暖暖面對面吃麵（這時用簡體字就很酷，連續三個面），
The table was tiny. We ate facing each other — *mian* across from *mian*, sharing *mian*.
中途還不小心撞到對方的頭，惹得我們哈哈大笑。
Our heads bumped mid-meal, making us both laugh.

「台灣這時還有傳統嗎？」麵吃完後，暖暖說。
"Is there a Taiwan tradition for this too?" she asked after we finished.
『台灣有個傳統，如果第三次和女生單獨吃飯卻讓女生付錢，男生會倒楣
"Yes — if a guy lets a girl pay on the third dinner date, he'll have bad luck
一個月。』我說。
for a month," I said.
「那第四次呢？」
"And the fourth?"
『第四次就換女生倒楣了。』
"On the fourth, the girl gets the bad luck."
暖暖說就這三次，下次別再搶著付錢了。
She said these three times were enough — I shouldn't fight to pay next time.
我點點頭，付了麵錢。
I nodded and paid for the noodles.

走回包廂，窗外是一片漆黑，沒有半點光亮。
Back in the compartment, the window showed nothing but darkness.
常聽說東北的黑土地，但現在看來什麼都是黑的。
I'd heard about the Northeast's black soil, but right now everything looked black.
暖暖拿出一副撲克牌，笑著說：「來玩橋牌。」
She took out a deck of cards. "Let's play bridge."
我很驚訝，仔細打量暖暖的神情，看不出異樣。
I was surprised and studied her expression — nothing unusual.
「咋了？」暖暖很疑惑。
"What?" She looked puzzled.
『沒事。』我說，『來玩吧。』
"Nothing," I said. "Let's play."

雙人橋又叫蜜月橋，我以為這應該是大家都知道的。
Two-person bridge is also called honeymoon bridge — I thought everyone knew that.
原本這就是新婚夫婦度蜜月時打發時間的遊戲。
It was originally a game for honeymooners to pass the time.
而且還有個規矩，輸了得脫一件衣服。
And there was a rule: the loser had to remove an item of clothing.
這樣打完了牌，雙方衣服也脫得差不多，上床睡覺就方便多了。
By the end, you'd be mostly undressed, making bedtime much easier.
也可避免新婚夫婦要脫衣上床一起睡覺時的尷尬。
It also avoided the awkwardness of newlyweds getting undressed and into bed together.

暖暖應該是不曉得這規矩，我一面打牌一面猶豫該不該告訴她？
She probably didn't know the rule. I hesitated — should I tell her?
沒想到暖暖牌技精湛，我竟然連輸十幾把，被她電假的。
But she was so good I lost over a dozen hands in a row — she crushed me.
真要脫的話，我早就脫得精光，連自尊也脫掉了。
If we'd been playing strip bridge, I'd have been naked — dignity and all.
還好沒說，還好。
Good thing I didn't tell her. Good thing.

上鋪的中年夫婦睡了，暖暖把包廂的燈熄了。
The couple above went to sleep. She turned off the compartment light.
整個世界變成一片黑暗，窗外也是。
The whole world went dark — the window too.
只有火車輪子壓著鐵軌所發出的聲音，規律而細碎。
Only the rhythmic, fragmentary sound of wheels on tracks remained.
在黑暗中我看著暖暖的臉龐，有些夢幻，有些朦朧。
In the darkness, I looked at her face — dreamlike, hazy.
我們壓低音量說話，暖暖的聲音又輕又細，像從遙遠的地方傳來。
We spoke in lowered voices; her voice was soft and thin, as if from far away.

暖暖說明天還得忙一整天，先睡唄。
She said she had a full day tomorrow — we should sleep.
我調了手機鬧鐘，怕睡過頭醒來時就到西伯利亞了。
I set my phone alarm — afraid I'd oversleep and wake up in Siberia.
暖暖說這班車直達哈爾濱，火車一停就表示哈爾濱到了，不會再往北開。
She said the train went straight to Harbin — when it stopped, we were there, not farther north.
「萬一真到了西伯利亞，我也在呀。」暖暖說。
"Even if we did end up in Siberia," she said, "I'd be there too."
『嗯。』我說，『那麼西伯利亞就有春天了。』
"Mm," I said. "Then Siberia would have spring."
暖暖抿著嘴輕輕笑著，眼睛閃閃亮亮，像夜空中的星星。
She smiled softly, her eyes sparkling like stars in the night sky.

我躺了下來，閉上眼睛，暖暖應該也躺下了。
I lay down and closed my eyes. She must have lain down too.
「涼涼。」暖暖說。
"Liangliang," she said.
『嗯？』
"Mm?"

"Sorry for dragging you all the way to Harbin."
『哈爾濱不遠，心的距離才遠。』
"Harbin isn't far — the distance between hearts is what's far."
「那你猜猜我正在想啥？」
"Guess what I'm thinking right now."
『妳一定在想明天得趕緊把事辦完，然後帶我逛逛。』
"You're thinking you need to finish work quickly tomorrow, then take me sightseeing."
「還有呢？」
"And?"
『妳也在想要帶我逛哪裡。』
"You're also thinking about where to take me."

"And?"

"You're worried I'll freeze because I'm not dressed warmly enough."

"You got them all right." She laughed again.

『那妳猜我正在想什麼？』我說。
"Now you guess what I'm thinking," I said.
「你肯定在想，到了西伯利亞咋跟俄羅斯姑娘聊天。」
"You're definitely thinking about how to chat up Russian girls if we end up in Siberia."
『妳好厲害。』我笑了笑，『還有呢？』
"Amazing," I laughed. "And?"

"Maybe you think you're dreaming," she said.
我很驚訝，不自覺睜開眼睛，像夜半突然醒過來只看見黑。
I was startled and opened my eyes instinctively — like waking up in the middle of the night to nothing but darkness.

「涼涼。」
"Liangliang."
『嗯？』
"Mm?"

"You're not dreaming. I'm alive, and I'm right next to you."

"If you don't believe me, reach out and touch."
我右手向右伸出，手臂在黑暗中緩緩摸索，終於碰觸暖暖的手心。
I reached out my right hand, groping slowly in the dark, until I touched her palm.
暖暖輕輕握住我的手。
She gently held my hand.

"Is it warm?" she asked.

"Mm."
然後手背傳來些微刺痛，我猜是暖暖用指甲掐了一下我的手背。

"Mm."

"So you're not dreaming. I'm alive, and I'm right next to you."

我有些漂動的心，緩緩安定，像進了港下了錨的船。
My drifting heart slowly settled, like a boat anchoring in harbor.

"Nuannuan," I said in the dark. "It's so good to see you."
